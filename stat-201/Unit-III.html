3.1 Concept & History of Regression

"Statistics is the grammar of science." — Karl Pearson. In real life and scientific investigations, we are frequently interested in estimating or predicting the value of one variable based on the known values of another variable. For example, an agricultural scientist wants to predict crop yield based on fertilizer dose, or an economist wants to forecast consumer expenditure based on national income. Regression analysis provides the mathematical framework for such estimation and cause-and-effect modeling.

Definition of Linear Regression

Linear Regression is the mathematical and statistical technique used to estimate or predict the unknown value of a dependent variable (\(Y\)) from the known value of an independent variable (\(X\)) by establishing a functional cause-and-effect relationship between them.

  • Dependent Variable (\(Y\)): The variable whose value is to be predicted or estimated (also called the regressand or response variable). Example: Crop Yield.
  • Independent Variable (\(X\)): The variable whose value is used to make the prediction (also called the regressor or explanatory variable). Example: Fertilizer Dose.

Historical Origin of the Term "Regression"

The term "Regression" (literally meaning "stepping back" or "returning to a former state") was coined by Sir Francis Galton in 1885 during his biometric studies on human genetics and heredity. Galton analyzed the heights of 928 adult children and their 205 pairs of parents.

Galton observed that tall parents tend to have tall children, and short parents tend to have short children. However, the average height of children of tall parents was less than their parents' height, while the average height of children of short parents was greater than their parents' height. In other words, human height tended to "regress" or "revert back" toward the general population mean height. Galton named this biological principle the "Law of Universal Regression to Mediocrity".

Example 3.1.1 (Variable Identification):

Problem: In an agricultural trial, a researcher collects data on Fertilizer Application (\(X\) in kg/hectare) and Paddy Yield (\(Y\) in quintals/hectare). State which is the dependent variable and which is the independent variable. Write the linear regression model equation and explain what parameters \(a\) and \(b\) represent.

Solution:

  • Independent Variable (\(X\)): Fertilizer Application (kg/ha), because it is controlled or varied by the researcher.
  • Dependent Variable (\(Y\)): Paddy Yield (q/ha), because its value depends on the amount of fertilizer applied.
  • Linear Model Equation: \(Y = a + bX\).
  • Parameter \(a\) (Y-Intercept): Represents the baseline yield expected when no fertilizer is applied (\(X = 0\)).
  • Parameter \(b\) (Regression Slope): Represents the average change in paddy yield per unit increase (1 kg/ha) in fertilizer application.

Example 3.1.2 (Basic Prediction Concept):

Problem: A simple regression model relating Wheat Yield (\(Y\) in q/ha) to Annual Rainfall (\(X\) in cm) is given by \(Y = 12.5 + 0.65 X\). Predict the expected wheat yield when rainfall is \(35\) cm.

Solution: Substitute \(X = 35\) into the regression equation:

\[ Y = 12.5 + 0.65 (35) = 12.5 + 22.75 = \mathbf{35.25 \text{ quintals/hectare}} \]

Thus, for a rainfall of 35 cm, the estimated wheat yield is 35.25 q/ha.

3.2 Two Lines of Regression

In bivariate data, when variables \(X\) and \(Y\) are not perfectly correlated (\(r \neq \pm 1\)), we obtain two distinct lines of regression depending on which variable is chosen as dependent and which as independent.

Why two lines? Because minimizing the sum of squared vertical errors \(\sum (Y - \hat{Y})^2\) (predicting \(Y\) from \(X\)) yields a completely different line than minimizing the sum of squared horizontal errors \(\sum (X - \hat{X})^2\) (predicting \(X\) from \(Y\)).

1. Line of Regression of \(Y\) on \(X\)

This line is used to estimate or predict the unknown value of \(Y\) for a given known value of \(X\). Here \(Y\) is dependent variable and \(X\) is independent variable.

\[ (Y - \bar{Y}) = b_{yx} (X - \bar{X}) \]

where \(b_{yx}\) is the Regression Coefficient of \(Y\) on \(X\) (representing the slope of the line of \(Y\) on \(X\)).

\[ b_{yx} = r \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x} = \frac{\text{Cov}(X,Y)}{\sigma_x^2} = \frac{n \sum XY - \sum X \sum Y}{n \sum X^2 - (\sum X)^2} \]

2. Line of Regression of \(X\) on \(Y\)

This line is used to estimate or predict the unknown value of \(X\) for a given known value of \(Y\). Here \(X\) is dependent variable and \(Y\) is independent variable.

\[ (X - \bar{X}) = b_{xy} (Y - \bar{Y}) \]

where \(b_{xy}\) is the Regression Coefficient of \(X\) on \(Y\) (representing the slope of the line of \(X\) on \(Y\)).

\[ b_{xy} = r \frac{\sigma_x}{\sigma_y} = \frac{\text{Cov}(X,Y)}{\sigma_y^2} = \frac{n \sum XY - \sum X \sum Y}{n \sum Y^2 - (\sum Y)^2} \]

Step-by-Step Derivation of Regression Equations using Least Squares

Let the line of \(Y\) on \(X\) be \(Y = a + bX\). The sum of squared vertical residuals is \(S = \sum_{i=1}^{n} (y_i - a - b x_i)^2\). Differentiating partially:

\[ \frac{\partial S}{\partial a} = 0 \implies \sum Y = n a + b \sum X \] \[ \frac{\partial S}{\partial b} = 0 \implies \sum XY = a \sum X + b \sum X^2 \]

Solving these normal equations gives \(b_{yx} = \frac{n \sum XY - \sum X \sum Y}{n \sum X^2 - (\sum X)^2}\) and \(a = \bar{Y} - b_{yx} \bar{X}\), leading to \(\mathbf{(Y - \bar{Y}) = b_{yx} (X - \bar{X})}\).

3.3 6 Fundamental Properties of Regression Coefficients

Property 1: Geometric Mean Rule

The correlation coefficient \(r\) is the geometric mean of the two regression coefficients \(b_{yx}\) and \(b_{xy}\): \(r = \pm \sqrt{b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy}}\).

Property 2: Same Sign Rule

Both regression coefficients \(b_{yx}\) and \(b_{xy}\) MUST have the exact same sign matching \(r\).

Property 3: Magnitude Reciprocity Rule

If \(|b_{yx}| > 1\), then \(|b_{xy}| < 1\).

Property 4: Arithmetic Mean Inequality

The arithmetic mean of regression coefficients satisfies: \(\frac{b_{yx} + b_{xy}}{2} \ge |r|\).

Property 5: Independence of Origin but Dependence on Scale

If \(u = \frac{X - a}{h}\) and \(v = \frac{Y - b}{k}\), then \(b_{yx} = \left(\frac{k}{h}\right) b_{vu}\), and \(b_{xy} = \left(\frac{h}{k}\right) b_{uv}\).

Property 6: Point of Intersection

The two regression lines intersect at the point of means \((\bar{X}, \bar{Y})\).

Example 3.3.1 (Verification of Properties):

Problem: Given regression coefficients \(b_{yx} = 1.2\) and \(b_{xy} = 0.3\). 1) Calculate correlation coefficient \(r\), 2) Verify Same Sign rule, 3) Verify Arithmetic Mean inequality.

Solution:

  1. \(r = +\sqrt{b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy}} = \sqrt{1.2 \times 0.3} = \sqrt{0.36} = \mathbf{+0.6}\).
  2. Both \(b_{yx} = +1.2\) and \(b_{xy} = +0.3\) are positive, matching \(r = +0.6\) (Same Sign Rule Verified).
  3. Arithmetic mean \(= \frac{1.2 + 0.3}{2} = 0.75\). Since \(0.75 \ge 0.6\), the AM Inequality \(\frac{b_{yx} + b_{xy}}{2} \ge r\) is Verified.

Example 3.3.2 (Origin & Scale Transformation):

Problem: If variables are transformed to \(u = \frac{X - 100}{10}\) and \(v = \frac{Y - 50}{5}\), and the calculated regression coefficient of \(v\) on \(u\) is \(b_{vu} = 0.8\), find the original regression coefficient \(b_{yx}\).

Solution: Here scale factors are \(h = 10\) and \(k = 5\). Using Property 5:

\[ b_{yx} = \left(\frac{k}{h}\right) b_{vu} = \left(\frac{5}{10}\right) (0.8) = 0.5 \times 0.8 = \mathbf{0.4} \]

3.4 Angle Between Two Regression Lines (\(\theta\))

The acute angle \(\theta\) between the two regression lines measures the strength of linear association:

\[ \tan \theta = \left( \frac{1 - r^2}{|r|} \right) \left( \frac{\sigma_x \sigma_y}{\sigma_x^2 + \sigma_y^2} \right) \]

Example 3.4.1 (Angle Calculation):

Problem: If standard deviation of \(X\) is \(\sigma_x = 3\), standard deviation of \(Y\) is \(\sigma_y = 4\), and correlation coefficient is \(r = 0.6\), calculate the acute angle \(\theta\) between the two regression lines.

Solution: Substitute values into the angle formula:

\[ \tan \theta = \left( \frac{1 - (0.6)^2}{0.6} \right) \left( \frac{3 \times 4}{3^2 + 4^2} \right) = \left( \frac{1 - 0.36}{0.6} \right) \left( \frac{12}{9 + 25} \right) = \left( \frac{0.64}{0.6} \right) \left( \frac{12}{34} \right) \] \[ \tan \theta = 1.0667 \times 0.3529 = 0.3765 \implies \mathbf{\theta = \tan^{-1}(0.3765) \approx 20.63^\circ} \]

Example 3.4.2 (Extreme Cases Verification):

Problem: Find the angle \(\theta\) between the regression lines when: 1) Correlation is perfect (\(r = 1\)), 2) Variables are uncorrelated (\(r = 0\)).

Solution:

  1. When \(r = 1\): \(\tan \theta = \left(\frac{1 - 1^2}{1}\right) (\cdots) = 0 \implies \mathbf{\theta = 0^\circ}\) (lines coincide completely).
  2. When \(r = 0\): \(\tan \theta = \left(\frac{1 - 0}{0}\right) (\cdots) = \infty \implies \mathbf{\theta = 90^\circ}\) (lines are perpendicular).

3.4 Regression Lines के बीच का कोण (\(\theta\))

3.5 Standard Error of Estimate (\(S_{yx}\) & \(S_{xy}\))

\[ S_{yx} = \sigma_y \sqrt{1 - r^2}, \quad S_{xy} = \sigma_x \sqrt{1 - r^2} \]

Example 3.5.1 (Standard Error S_yx):

Problem: In a study of crop production, standard deviation of yield \(Y\) is \(\sigma_y = 8\) quintals, and correlation with fertilizer \(X\) is \(r = 0.8\). Calculate the Standard Error of Estimate \(S_{yx}\) and state the proportion of unexplained variance.

Solution:

\[ S_{yx} = \sigma_y \sqrt{1 - r^2} = 8 \sqrt{1 - (0.8)^2} = 8 \sqrt{1 - 0.64} = 8 \sqrt{0.36} = 8 \times 0.6 = \mathbf{4.8 \text{ quintals}} \]

Proportion of explained variance \(= r^2 = (0.8)^2 = 0.64 = 64\%\).

Proportion of unexplained variance \(= 1 - r^2 = 0.36 = \mathbf{36\%}\).

Example 3.5.2 (Standard Error S_xy):

Problem: If standard deviation of fertilizer dose \(X\) is \(\sigma_x = 10\) kg and correlation is \(r = 0.6\), calculate \(S_{xy}\).

Solution:

\[ S_{xy} = \sigma_x \sqrt{1 - r^2} = 10 \sqrt{1 - (0.6)^2} = 10 \sqrt{0.64} = 10 \times 0.8 = \mathbf{8.0 \text{ kg}} \]

3.5 Standard Error of Estimate (\(S_{yx}\) एवं \(S_{xy}\))

Example 3.5.1 (Standard Error S_yx):

Problem: \(\sigma_y = 8, r = 0.8\) के लिए \(S_{yx}\) एवं unexplained variance प्रतिशत ज्ञात कीजिए।

Solution: \(S_{yx} = 8 \sqrt{0.36} = \mathbf{4.8}\)। Unexplained variance \(= \mathbf{36\%}\)।

Example 3.5.2 (Standard Error S_xy):

Problem: \(\sigma_x = 10, r = 0.6\) के लिए \(S_{xy}\) ज्ञात कीजिए।

Solution: \(S_{xy} = 10 \sqrt{0.64} = \mathbf{8.0}\)।

3.6 Distinction Between Correlation and Regression

Example 3.6.1 (Symmetry vs Asymmetry Demonstration):

Problem: Given \(\sigma_x = 4, \sigma_y = 8,\) and correlation \(r = 0.75\). Calculate: 1) Correlation coefficients \(r_{xy}\) and \(r_{yx}\), 2) Regression coefficients \(b_{yx}\) and \(b_{xy}\). Use these results to demonstrate symmetry vs asymmetry.

Solution:

  1. Correlation Symmetry: \(r_{xy} = r_{yx} = \mathbf{0.75}\) (Symmetric, independent of direction).
  2. Regression Asymmetry:
    \(b_{yx} = r \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x} = 0.75 \left(\frac{8}{4}\right) = 0.75 \times 2 = \mathbf{1.5}\).
    \(b_{xy} = r \frac{\sigma_x}{\sigma_y} = 0.75 \left(\frac{4}{8}\right) = 0.75 \times 0.5 = \mathbf{0.375}\).
    Since \(b_{yx} = 1.5 \neq b_{xy} = 0.375\), regression is Asymmetric.

Example 3.6.2 (Prediction Ability Demonstration):

Problem: Given \(\bar{X} = 5, \bar{Y} = 12,\) and \(b_{yx} = 1.4\). Demonstrate how regression line predicts \(Y\) when \(X = 8\), and explain why correlation coefficient \(r\) alone cannot perform this prediction.

Solution: Regression line of \(Y\) on \(X\): \((Y - 12) = 1.4 (X - 5) \implies Y = 1.4 X + 5\). For \(X = 8\): \(Y = 1.4(8) + 5 = \mathbf{16.2}\). Correlation coefficient \(r\) only indicates strength of relationship, but does not provide a linear intercept or slope to calculate specific predictions.

3.6 Correlation और Regression में अंतर

Example 3.6.1 (Symmetry vs Asymmetry):

Problem: \(\sigma_x = 4, \sigma_y = 8, r = 0.75\) के लिए \(r_{xy}, r_{yx}\) तथा \(b_{yx}, b_{xy}\) ज्ञात करके सममिति सिद्ध कीजिए।

Solution: \(r_{xy} = r_{yx} = 0.75\) (Symmetric)। \(b_{yx} = 1.5 \ne b_{xy} = 0.375\) (Asymmetric)।

Example 3.6.2 (Prediction Ability):

Problem: \(\bar{X} = 5, \bar{Y} = 12, b_{yx} = 1.4\) के साथ \(X = 8\) पर prediction कीजिए।

Solution: \(Y = 1.4(8) + 5 = \mathbf{16.2}\)।

3.7 Multiple Regression & Partial Correlation (Trivariate Regression)

\[ (X_1 - \bar{X}_1) = b_{12.3} (X_2 - \bar{X}_2) + b_{13.2} (X_3 - \bar{X}_3) \] \[ b_{12.3} = \left( \frac{\sigma_1}{\sigma_2} \right) \left( \frac{r_{12} - r_{13} r_{23}}{1 - r_{23}^2} \right), \quad b_{13.2} = \left( \frac{\sigma_1}{\sigma_3} \right) \left( \frac{r_{13} - r_{12} r_{23}}{1 - r_{23}^2} \right) \]

Example 3.7.1 (Trivariate Regression Plane):

Problem: In a trial predicting Crop Yield (\(X_1\)) from Fertilizer (\(X_2\)) and Irrigation (\(X_3\)), given: \(\bar{X}_1 = 50, \bar{X}_2 = 15, \bar{X}_3 = 8\), \(\sigma_1 = 8, \sigma_2 = 3, \sigma_3 = 2\), and correlation coefficients \(r_{12} = 0.75, r_{13} = 0.60, r_{23} = 0.40\). Calculate partial regression coefficients \(b_{12.3}\) and \(b_{13.2}\) and write the multiple regression plane equation.

Solution:

1) Partial regression coefficient \(b_{12.3}\):

\[ b_{12.3} = \left(\frac{8}{3}\right) \left[\frac{0.75 - (0.60)(0.40)}{1 - (0.40)^2}\right] = 2.667 \left[\frac{0.75 - 0.24}{1 - 0.16}\right] = 2.667 \left[\frac{0.51}{0.84}\right] = \mathbf{1.619} \]

2) Partial regression coefficient \(b_{13.2}\):

\[ b_{13.2} = \left(\frac{8}{2}\right) \left[\frac{0.60 - (0.75)(0.40)}{1 - (0.40)^2}\right] = 4 \left[\frac{0.60 - 0.30}{0.84}\right] = 4 \left[\frac{0.30}{0.84}\right] = \mathbf{1.429} \]

3) Regression plane equation:

\[ (X_1 - 50) = 1.619 (X_2 - 15) + 1.429 (X_3 - 8) \implies \mathbf{X_1 = 14.28 + 1.619 X_2 + 1.429 X_3} \]

Example 3.7.2 (Partial & Multiple Correlation):

Problem: Using zero-order correlations \(r_{12} = 0.75, r_{13} = 0.60, r_{23} = 0.40\), calculate: 1) Partial Correlation Coefficient \(r_{12.3}\), 2) Multiple Correlation Coefficient \(R_{1.23}\).

Solution:

1) Partial Correlation Coefficient \(r_{12.3}\):

\[ r_{12.3} = \frac{0.75 - (0.60)(0.40)}{\sqrt{[1 - (0.60)^2][1 - (0.40)^2]}} = \frac{0.51}{\sqrt{0.64 \times 0.84}} = \frac{0.51}{\sqrt{0.5376}} = \frac{0.51}{0.7332} = \mathbf{0.696} \]

2) Multiple Correlation Coefficient \(R_{1.23}\):

\[ R_{1.23}^2 = \frac{(0.75)^2 + (0.60)^2 - 2(0.75)(0.60)(0.40)}{1 - (0.40)^2} = \frac{0.5625 + 0.36 - 0.36}{0.84} = \frac{0.5625}{0.84} = 0.6696 \] \[ R_{1.23} = \sqrt{0.6696} = \mathbf{0.818} \]

3.7 Multiple Regression एवं Partial Correlation (Trivariate Regression)

Example 3.7.1 (Multiple Regression Plane):

Problem: \(\bar{X}_1 = 50, \bar{X}_2 = 15, \bar{X}_3 = 8, \sigma_1 = 8, \sigma_2 = 3, \sigma_3 = 2, r_{12} = 0.75, r_{13} = 0.60, r_{23} = 0.40\) के लिए multiple regression plane समीकरण ज्ञात कीजिए।

Solution: \(r_{12.3} = \mathbf{0.696}\), \(R_{1.23} = \mathbf{0.818}\)।

\[ b_{yx} = \frac{5(89) - (15)(25)}{5(55) - (15)^2} = \frac{445 - 375}{275 - 225} = \frac{70}{50} = 1.4 \]

Regression Line of \(Y\) on \(X\): \((Y - 5) = 1.4 (X - 3) \implies \mathbf{Y = 1.4 X + 0.8}\).

For \(X = 6\): \(Y = 1.4(6) + 0.8 = \mathbf{9.2}\).

2) Regression Coefficient \(b_{xy}\):

\[ b_{xy} = \frac{5(89) - (15)(25)}{5(151) - (25)^2} = \frac{70}{755 - 625} = \frac{70}{130} = 0.538 \]

Regression Line of \(X\) on \(Y\): \((X - 3) = 0.538 (Y - 5) \implies \mathbf{X = 0.538 Y + 0.31}\).

For \(Y = 10\): \(X = 0.538(10) + 0.31 = \mathbf{5.69}\).

Solved Example 2: Finding Means and Correlation from Given Regression Lines

Problem: Given two regression lines: \(8X - 10Y + 66 = 0\) and \(40X - 18Y - 214 = 0\). Find: 1) Means \(\bar{X}\) and \(\bar{Y}\), 2) Correlation coefficient \(r\).

Solution:

1) Both lines intersect at \((\bar{X}, \bar{Y})\). Solving simultaneously:

\(8\bar{X} - 10\bar{Y} = -66\) ---(1), and \(40\bar{X} - 18\bar{Y} = 214\) ---(2).

Multiply Eq (1) by 5: \(40\bar{X} - 50\bar{Y} = -330\). Subtracting from Eq (2): \(32\bar{Y} = 544 \implies \mathbf{\bar{Y} = 17}\).

Substituting into Eq (1): \(8\bar{X} = 10(17) - 66 = 104 \implies \mathbf{\bar{X} = 13}\).

2) Assume line (1) is \(Y\) on \(X\): \(Y = 0.8 X + 6.6 \implies b_{yx} = 0.8\).

Assume line (2) is \(X\) on \(Y\): \(X = 0.45 Y + 5.35 \implies b_{xy} = 0.45\).

Since both slopes are positive, \(r = +\sqrt{b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy}} = \sqrt{0.8 \times 0.45} = \sqrt{0.36} = \mathbf{+0.6}\).

Solved Example 3: Multiple Regression Plane for Agricultural Yield

Problem: For an agricultural experiment with Crop Yield (\(X_1\)), Fertilizer (\(X_2\)), and Rainfall (\(X_3\)), we have: \(\bar{X}_1 = 60, \bar{X}_2 = 20, \bar{X}_3 = 10\), \(\sigma_1 = 10, \sigma_2 = 4, \sigma_3 = 3\), and \(r_{12} = 0.8, r_{13} = 0.6, r_{23} = 0.5\). Find the multiple regression plane equation of \(X_1\) on \(X_2\) and \(X_3\).

Solution:

1) Partial regression coefficient \(b_{12.3}\):

\[ b_{12.3} = \left(\frac{10}{4}\right) \left[\frac{0.8 - (0.6)(0.5)}{1 - (0.5)^2}\right] = 2.5 \left[\frac{0.5}{0.75}\right] = 1.67 \]

2) Partial regression coefficient \(b_{13.2}\):

\[ b_{13.2} = \left(\frac{10}{3}\right) \left[\frac{0.6 - (0.8)(0.5)}{1 - (0.5)^2}\right] = 3.33 \left[\frac{0.2}{0.75}\right] = 0.89 \]

3) Regression Plane Equation:

\[ (X_1 - 60) = 1.67 (X_2 - 20) + 0.89 (X_3 - 10) \implies \mathbf{X_1 = 17.7 + 1.67 X_2 + 0.89 X_3} \]

📝 Subjective Questions — 30 Questions

Q1. Define Linear Regression. Explain Sir Francis Galton's historical concept of regression.
Solution:

Linear Regression is the statistical method of modeling the functional relationship between a dependent variable \(Y\) and one or more independent variables \(X\) to predict \(Y\). History: Introduced by Sir Francis Galton in 1885 during biometric studies on human inheritance (heights of fathers and sons). Galton observed that tall fathers tend to have tall sons, but the sons' average height reverted back toward the general population mean. He called this phenomenon 'Regression to Mediocrity'.

Q2. Derive the equations for the two lines of regression (\(Y\) on \(X\) and \(X\) on \(Y\)) using the Principle of Least Squares.
Solution:

1) Line of \(Y\) on \(X\) (\(Y = a + bX\)): Minimize \(S = \sum (y_i - a - b x_i)^2\). Setting \(\partial S/\partial a = 0 \implies \sum Y = n a + b \sum X\). Setting \(\partial S/\partial b = 0 \implies \sum XY = a \sum X + b \sum X^2\). Solving gives \(b = b_{yx} = \frac{\text{Cov}(X,Y)}{\text{Var}(X)} = r \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}\) and \(a = \bar{Y} - b_{yx} \bar{X}\). Hence line is \((Y - \bar{Y}) = b_{yx} (X - \bar{X})\). 2) Line of \(X\) on \(Y\) (\(X = c + dY\)): Minimize \(S = \sum (x_i - c - d y_i)^2\). Solving normal equations gives \(d = b_{xy} = \frac{\text{Cov}(X,Y)}{\text{Var}(Y)} = r \frac{\sigma_x}{\sigma_y}\) and \(c = \bar{X} - b_{xy} \bar{Y}\). Hence line is \((X - \bar{X}) = b_{xy} (Y - \bar{Y})\).

Q3. State and prove the 5 fundamental properties of regression coefficients.
Solution:

1) Geometric Mean: \(r = \pm \sqrt{b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy}}\). Proof: \(b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy} = \left(r \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}\right) \left(r \frac{\sigma_x}{\sigma_y}\right) = r^2 \implies r = \pm \sqrt{b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy}}\). 2) Same Sign: Both \(b_{yx}\) and \(b_{xy}\) must have the same sign as \(r\) because \(\sigma_x > 0\) and \(\sigma_y > 0\). 3) Magnitude Reciprocity: If \(|b_{yx}| > 1\), then \(|b_{xy}| < 1\) because \(b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy} = r^2 \le 1\). 4) Arithmetic Mean Inequality: \(\frac{b_{yx} + b_{xy}}{2} \ge |r|\). Proof: \((\sqrt{b_{yx}} - \sqrt{b_{xy}})^2 \ge 0 \implies b_{yx} + b_{xy} \ge 2 \sqrt{b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy}} = 2|r|\). 5) Change of Origin and Scale: Independent of origin, dependent on scale: \(b_{yx} = \left(\frac{k}{h}\right) b_{vu}\).

Q4. Derive the formula for the angle \(\theta\) between the two lines of regression.
Solution:

Slopes of regression lines: \(m_1 = b_{yx} = r \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}\) and \(m_2 = \frac{1}{b_{xy}} = \frac{1}{r} \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}\). Angle formula: \(\tan \theta = \left| \frac{m_1 - m_2}{1 + m_1 m_2} \right| = \left(\frac{1 - r^2}{|r|}\right) \left(\frac{\sigma_x \sigma_y}{\sigma_x^2 + \sigma_y^2}\right)\). Special cases: 1) If \(r = \pm 1\), \(\tan \theta = 0 \implies \theta = 0^\circ\) (lines coincide). 2) If \(r = 0\), \(\tan \theta = \infty \implies \theta = 90^\circ\) (lines perpendicular).

Q5. Define Standard Error of Estimate \(S_{yx}\). Derive its expression in terms of \(r\) and \(\sigma_y\).
Solution:

Standard Error of Estimate \(S_{yx}\) measures the dispersion of observed \(Y\) values around the fitted regression line: \(S_{yx} = \sqrt{\frac{\sum (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2}{n}}\). Substituting \(\hat{y}_i = \bar{Y} + b_{yx} (x_i - \bar{X})\): \(S_{yx}^2 = \frac{1}{n} \sum [(y_i - \bar{Y}) - b_{yx} (x_i - \bar{X})]^2 = \sigma_y^2 (1 - r^2)\). Thus \(S_{yx} = \sigma_y \sqrt{1 - r^2}\).

Q6. Distinguish clearly between Correlation and Regression analysis.
Solution:

1) Meaning: Correlation measures strength of linear association; Regression models cause-and-effect functional prediction. 2) Variables: Correlation treats \(X\) and \(Y\) symmetrically; Regression treats them asymmetrically (\(Y\) dependent, \(X\) independent). 3) Coefficient Limits: \(r\) lies in \([-1, +1]\); regression coefficients \(b_{yx}, b_{xy}\) can exceed 1. 4) Origin & Scale: \(r\) is independent of both origin and scale; \(b_{yx}\) is independent of origin but dependent on scale. 5) Prediction: Correlation cannot predict values; Regression enables predicting \(Y\) for a given \(X\).

Q7. Explain Trivariate Multiple Linear Regression. Write the equation of the regression plane and define partial regression coefficients.
Solution:

Trivariate Multiple Regression involves 3 variables (\(X_1, X_2, X_3\)) where \(X_1\) is predicted from \(X_2\) and \(X_3\). Regression plane equation: \(X_1 - \bar{X}_1 = b_{12.3} (X_2 - \bar{X}_2) + b_{13.2} (X_3 - \bar{X}_3)\). Here \(b_{12.3}\) is the partial regression coefficient of \(X_1\) on \(X_2\) keeping \(X_3\) constant: \(b_{12.3} = \left(\frac{\sigma_1}{\sigma_2}\right) \left(\frac{r_{12} - r_{13} r_{23}}{1 - r_{23}^2}\right)\). Similarly \(b_{13.2} = \left(\frac{\sigma_1}{\sigma_3}\right) \left(\frac{r_{13} - r_{12} r_{23}}{1 - r_{23}^2}\right)\).

Q8. Explain Multiple Correlation Coefficient \(R_{1.23}\) and Partial Correlation Coefficient \(r_{12.3}\).
Solution:

1) Multiple Correlation \(R_{1.23}\): Measures the overall linear association between \(X_1\) and its combined joint linear estimate based on \(X_2\) and \(X_3\). Formula: \(R_{1.23}^2 = \frac{r_{12}^2 + r_{13}^2 - 2 r_{12} r_{13} r_{23}}{1 - r_{23}^2}\). Formula: \(r_{12.3} = \frac{r_{12} - r_{13} r_{23}}{\sqrt{(1 - r_{13}^2)(1 - r_{23}^2)}}\). Range: \(-1 \le r_{12.3} \le +1\).

1) Line of \(Y\) on \(X\) (\(Y = a + bX\)): Minimize \(S = \sum (y_i - a - b x_i)^2\). Setting \(\partial S/\partial a = 0 \implies \sum Y = n a + b \sum X\). Setting \(\partial S/\partial b = 0 \implies \sum XY = a \sum X + b \sum X^2\). Solving gives \(b = b_{yx} = \frac{\text{Cov}(X,Y)}{\text{Var}(X)} = r \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}\) and \(a = \bar{Y} - b_{yx} \bar{X}\). Hence line is \((Y - \bar{Y}) = b_{yx} (X - \bar{X})\). 2) Line of \(X\) on \(Y\) (\(X = c + dY\)): Minimize \(S = \sum (x_i - c - d y_i)^2\). Solving normal equations gives \(d = b_{xy} = \frac{\text{Cov}(X,Y)}{\text{Var}(Y)} = r \frac{\sigma_x}{\sigma_y}\) and \(c = \bar{X} - b_{xy} \bar{Y}\). Hence line is \((X - \bar{X}) = b_{xy} (Y - \bar{Y})\).

Q3. State and prove the 5 fundamental properties of regression coefficients.
Solution:

1) Geometric Mean: \(r = \pm \sqrt{b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy}}\). Proof: \(b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy} = \left(r \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}\right) \left(r \frac{\sigma_x}{\sigma_y}\right) = r^2 \implies r = \pm \sqrt{b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy}}\). 2) Same Sign: Both \(b_{yx}\) and \(b_{xy}\) must have the same sign as \(r\) because \(\sigma_x > 0\) and \(\sigma_y > 0\). 3) Magnitude Reciprocity: If \(|b_{yx}| > 1\), then \(|b_{xy}| < 1\) because \(b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy} = r^2 \le 1\). 4) Arithmetic Mean Inequality: \(\frac{b_{yx} + b_{xy}}{2} \ge |r|\). Proof: \((\sqrt{b_{yx}} - \sqrt{b_{xy}})^2 \ge 0 \implies b_{yx} + b_{xy} \ge 2 \sqrt{b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy}} = 2|r|\). 5) Change of Origin and Scale: Independent of origin, dependent on scale: \(b_{yx} = \left(\frac{k}{h}\right) b_{vu}\).

Q4. Derive the formula for the angle \(\theta\) between the two lines of regression.
Solution:

Slopes of regression lines: \(m_1 = b_{yx} = r \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}\) and \(m_2 = \frac{1}{b_{xy}} = \frac{1}{r} \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}\). Angle formula: \(\tan \theta = \left| \frac{m_1 - m_2}{1 + m_1 m_2} \right| = \left(\frac{1 - r^2}{|r|}\right) \left(\frac{\sigma_x \sigma_y}{\sigma_x^2 + \sigma_y^2}\right)\). Special cases: 1) If \(r = \pm 1\), \(\tan \theta = 0 \implies \theta = 0^\circ\) (lines coincide). 2) If \(r = 0\), \(\tan \theta = \infty \implies \theta = 90^\circ\) (lines perpendicular).

Q5. Define Standard Error of Estimate \(S_{yx}\). Derive its expression in terms of \(r\) and \(\sigma_y\).
Solution:

Standard Error of Estimate \(S_{yx}\) measures the dispersion of observed \(Y\) values around the fitted regression line: \(S_{yx} = \sqrt{\frac{\sum (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2}{n}}\). Substituting \(\hat{y}_i = \bar{Y} + b_{yx} (x_i - \bar{X})\): \(S_{yx}^2 = \frac{1}{n} \sum [(y_i - \bar{Y}) - b_{yx} (x_i - \bar{X})]^2 = \sigma_y^2 (1 - r^2)\). Thus \(S_{yx} = \sigma_y \sqrt{1 - r^2}\).

Q6. Distinguish clearly between Correlation and Regression analysis.
Solution:

1) Meaning: Correlation measures strength of linear association; Regression models cause-and-effect functional prediction. 2) Variables: Correlation treats \(X\) and \(Y\) symmetrically; Regression treats them asymmetrically (\(Y\) dependent, \(X\) independent). 3) Coefficient Limits: \(r\) lies in \([-1, +1]\); regression coefficients \(b_{yx}, b_{xy}\) can exceed 1. 4) Origin & Scale: \(r\) is independent of both origin and scale; \(b_{yx}\) is independent of origin but dependent on scale. 5) Prediction: Correlation cannot predict values; Regression enables predicting \(Y\) for a given \(X\).

Q7. Explain Trivariate Multiple Linear Regression. Write the equation of the regression plane and define partial regression coefficients.
Solution:

Trivariate Multiple Regression involves 3 variables (\(X_1, X_2, X_3\)) where \(X_1\) is predicted from \(X_2\) and \(X_3\). Regression plane equation: \(X_1 - \bar{X}_1 = b_{12.3} (X_2 - \bar{X}_2) + b_{13.2} (X_3 - \bar{X}_3)\). Here \(b_{12.3}\) is the partial regression coefficient of \(X_1\) on \(X_2\) keeping \(X_3\) constant: \(b_{12.3} = \left(\frac{\sigma_1}{\sigma_2}\right) \left(\frac{r_{12} - r_{13} r_{23}}{1 - r_{23}^2}\right)\). Similarly \(b_{13.2} = \left(\frac{\sigma_1}{\sigma_3}\right) \left(\frac{r_{13} - r_{12} r_{23}}{1 - r_{23}^2}\right)\).

Q8. Explain Multiple Correlation Coefficient \(R_{1.23}\) and Partial Correlation Coefficient \(r_{12.3}\).
Solution:

1) Multiple Correlation \(R_{1.23}\): Measures the overall linear association between \(X_1\) and its combined joint linear estimate based on \(X_2\) and \(X_3\). Formula: \(R_{1.23}^2 = \frac{r_{12}^2 + r_{13}^2 - 2 r_{12} r_{13} r_{23}}{1 - r_{23}^2}\). Range: \(0 \le R_{1.23} \le 1\). 2) Partial Correlation \(r_{12.3}\): Measures the net correlation between \(X_1\) and \(X_2\) after eliminating the linear influence of \(X_3\). Formula: \(r_{12.3} = \frac{r_{12} - r_{13} r_{23}}{\sqrt{(1 - r_{13}^2)(1 - r_{23}^2)}}\). Range: \(-1 \le r_{12.3} \le +1\).

Q9. The two regression lines are \(8X - 10Y + 66 = 0\) and \(40X - 18Y - 214 = 0\). Find: 1) Mean of \(X\) and \(Y\), 2) Correlation coefficient \(r\), 3) Variance of \(Y\) if \(\text{Var}(X) = 9\).
Solution:

1) Means: Solving \(8\bar{X} - 10\bar{Y} = -66\) and \(40\bar{X} - 18\bar{Y} = 214\). Multiply first by 5: \(40\bar{X} - 50\bar{Y} = -330\). Subtract from second: \(32\bar{Y} = 544 \implies \bar{Y} = 17\). Then \(8\bar{X} = 10(17) - 66 = 104 \implies \bar{X} = 13\). Means are \((\bar{X} = 13, \bar{Y} = 17)\). 2) Slopes: Assume \(Y\) on \(X\) is \(8X - 10Y + 66 = 0 \implies Y = 0.8 X + 6.6 \implies b_{yx} = 0.8\). Assume \(X\) on \(Y\) is \(40X - 18Y - 214 = 0 \implies X = 0.45 Y + 5.35 \implies b_{xy} = 0.45\). \(r = \sqrt{0.8 \times 0.45} = \sqrt{0.36} = +0.6\). 3) Variance of \(Y\): \(b_{yx} = r \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x} \implies 0.8 = 0.6 \frac{\sigma_y}{3} \implies \sigma_y = 4 \implies \text{Var}(Y) = 16\).

Q10. Given crop yield \(X_1\), fertilizer \(X_2\), and rainfall \(X_3\) with means \(\bar{X}_1 = 60, \bar{X}_2 = 20, \bar{X}_3 = 10\), std devs \(\sigma_1 = 10, \sigma_2 = 4, \sigma_3 = 3\), and correlations \(r_{12} = 0.8, r_{13} = 0.6, r_{23} = 0.5\). Calculate partial regression coefficients \(b_{12.3}\) and \(b_{13.2}\) and write the regression equation.
Solution:

1) \(b_{12.3} = \left(\frac{10}{4}\right) \left[\frac{0.8 - (0.6)(0.5)}{1 - (0.5)^2}\right] = 2.5 \left[\frac{0.5}{0.75}\right] = 1.67\). 2) \(b_{13.2} = \left(\frac{10}{3}\right) \left[\frac{0.6 - (0.8)(0.5)}{1 - (0.5)^2}\right] = 3.33 \left[\frac{0.2}{0.75}\right] = 0.89\). 3) Regression equation: \(X_1 - 60 = 1.67 (X_2 - 20) + 0.89 (X_3 - 10) \implies X_1 = 17.7 + 1.67 X_2 + 0.89 X_3\).
