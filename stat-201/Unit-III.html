3.1 Concept & History of Regression

"Statistics is the grammar of science." — Karl Pearson. In real life and scientific investigations, correlation analysis tells us whether two variables move together, but it cannot forecast or estimate the value of one variable when the other is known. For example, an agricultural scientist wants to predict crop yield based on fertilizer dose, or an economist wants to forecast consumer expenditure based on national income. Regression Analysis provides the mathematical framework for such functional prediction and cause-and-effect modeling.

Formal Definition of Linear Regression

Linear Regression is the statistical and mathematical technique used to estimate or predict the unknown value of a dependent variable (\(Y\)) from the known value of an independent variable (\(X\)) by establishing a mathematical functional relationship between them.

  • Dependent Variable (\(Y\)): The variable whose value is to be predicted or estimated. Also known as the regressand, response variable, or endogenous variable (e.g., Paddy Crop Yield).
  • Independent Variable (\(X\)): The variable whose known value is used to make the prediction. Also known as the regressor, explanatory variable, or exogenous variable (e.g., Fertilizer Dose in kg/ha).

Historical Origin: Sir Francis Galton's Biometric Discovery (1885)

The term "Regression" (literally meaning "stepping back" or "reverting to a former state") was introduced into statistical literature by Sir Francis Galton in 1885 during his biometric research on human genetics and heredity. Galton analyzed the heights of 928 adult children and their 205 pairs of parents.

Galton observed that tall parents tend to have tall children, and short parents tend to have short children. However, the average height of children born to exceptionally tall parents was shorter than their parents, while the average height of children born to exceptionally short parents was taller than their parents. In other words, human height tended to "regress" or "revert back" toward the general population mean height (\(\bar{Y}\)). Galton formulated this fundamental biological law as the "Law of Universal Regression to Mediocrity".

The Simple Linear Regression Model & Parameter Guide

The simple linear regression model relating dependent variable \(Y\) to independent variable \(X\) is expressed as:

\[ Y = a + b X + e \]
Parameter Verbal Explanation & Symbol Guide:
  • \(\mathbf{Y}\) = Observed value of the dependent variable (regressand).
  • \(\mathbf{X}\) = Known value of the independent variable (regressor).
  • \(\mathbf{a}\) = \(Y\)-intercept. Represents the baseline expected value of \(Y\) when \(X = 0\).
  • \(\mathbf{b}\) = Slope Coefficient (Regression Coefficient). Represents the average change in \(Y\) per 1 unit increase in \(X\).
  • \(\mathbf{e}\) = Random Error / Residual Term. Represents unexplained disturbance or measurement noise.

Example 3.1.1 (Variable Identification & Model Formulation):

Problem: In an agricultural trial, a researcher collects data on Fertilizer Application (\(X\) in kg/hectare) and Paddy Yield (\(Y\) in quintals/hectare). State which is the dependent variable and which is the independent variable. Write the linear regression model equation and explain what parameters \(a\) and \(b\) represent.

Solution:

  • Independent Variable (\(X\)): Fertilizer Application (kg/ha), because it is controlled or varied by the researcher.
  • Dependent Variable (\(Y\)): Paddy Yield (q/ha), because its value depends on the amount of fertilizer applied.
  • Linear Model Equation: \(Y = a + bX\).
  • Parameter \(a\) (\(Y\)-Intercept): Represents the baseline yield expected when no fertilizer is applied (\(X = 0\)).
  • Parameter \(b\) (Regression Slope): Represents the average change in paddy yield per unit increase (1 kg/ha) in fertilizer application.

Example 3.1.2 (Basic Prediction Concept):

Problem: A simple regression model relating Wheat Yield (\(Y\) in q/ha) to Annual Rainfall (\(X\) in cm) is given by \(Y = 12.5 + 0.65 X\). Predict the expected wheat yield when rainfall is \(35\) cm.

Solution: Substitute \(X = 35\) into the regression equation:

\[ Y = 12.5 + 0.65 (35) = 12.5 + 22.75 = \mathbf{35.25 \text{ quintals/hectare}} \]

Thus, for a rainfall of 35 cm, the estimated wheat yield is 35.25 q/ha.

3.1 Regression की अवधारणा एवं इतिहास (Concept & History of Regression)

"Statistics is the grammar of science." — Karl Pearson. वास्तविक जीवन तथा वैज्ञानिक अनुसंधानों में, correlation analysis केवल यह बताता है कि दो variables साथ-साथ बदलते हैं या नहीं, परंतु यह किसी एक variable के known value के आधार पर दूसरे variable का forecast या estimation नहीं कर सकता। उदाहरण के लिए, एक कृषि वैज्ञानिक fertilizer dose के आधार पर crop yield का prediction करना चाहता है। Regression Analysis इस प्रकार के functional prediction तथा cause-and-effect modeling के लिए गणितीय ढांचा प्रदान करता है।

Linear Regression की औपचारिक परिभाषा

Linear Regression एक ऐसी सांख्यिकीय तथा गणितीय विधि है जिसके द्वारा independent variable (\(X\)) के known मान का उपयोग करके dependent variable (\(Y\)) के unknown मान का mathematical functional relationship स्थापित करके estimation या prediction किया जाता है।

  • Dependent Variable (\(Y\)): वह चर जिसका मान अनुमानित या predict किया जाना है। इसे regressand, response variable, या endogenous variable भी कहते हैं (जैसे Paddy Crop Yield)।
  • Independent Variable (\(X\)): वह चर जिसका known मान prediction हेतु उपयोग किया जाता है। इसे regressor, explanatory variable, या exogenous variable कहते हैं (जैसे Fertilizer Dose in kg/ha)।

ऐतिहासिक उद्गम: Sir Francis Galton की बायोमेट्रिक खोज (1885)

"Regression" शब्द (जिसका शाब्दिक अर्थ "पीछे लौटना" या "पूर्व स्थिति में आना" है) का प्रतिपादन Sir Francis Galton द्वारा 1885 में मानव आनुवंशिकी (biometrics) के अध्ययन के दौरान किया गया था। Galton ने 928 वयस्क बच्चों तथा उनके 205 माता-पिता की ऊँचाइयों का विश्लेषण किया।

Galton ने पाया कि लंबे माता-पिता के बच्चे औसतन लंबे होते हैं और नाटे माता-पिता के बच्चे नाटे होते हैं। परंतु अत्यधिक लंबे माता-पिता के बच्चों की औसत ऊँचाई उनके माता-पिता की तुलना में कम थी, जबकि अत्यधिक नाटे माता-पिता के बच्चों की औसत ऊँचाई उनके माता-पिता की तुलना में अधिक थी। शब्दों में, बच्चों की ऊँचाई सामान्य जनसंख्या के औसत मान (\(\bar{Y}\)) की ओर "regress" (लौटने) की प्रवृत्ति दर्शाती है। Galton ने इस जैविक नियम को "Law of Universal Regression to Mediocrity" कहा।

3.2 Two Lines of Regression & Derivation

In bivariate data involving two variables \(X\) and \(Y\), unless the correlation is perfect (\(r = \pm 1\)), there are always two distinct lines of regression. One line cannot serve both prediction directions.

Why Are There Two Lines of Regression? (Textual Explanation)

The distinction arises from the choice of dependent and independent variables and the direction of error minimization:

  1. Line of Regression of \(Y\) on \(X\): Formulated when \(X\) is the known independent variable and \(Y\) is the unknown dependent variable. It minimizes the sum of squared vertical distances \(\sum (Y - \hat{Y})^2\).
  2. Line of Regression of \(X\) on \(Y\): Formulated when \(Y\) is the known independent variable and \(X\) is the unknown dependent variable. It minimizes the sum of squared horizontal distances \(\sum (X - \hat{X})^2\).

1. Line of Regression of \(Y\) on \(X\) (Definition & Formulas)

The line of regression of \(Y\) on \(X\) in point-slope form is given by:

\[ (Y - \bar{Y}) = b_{yx} (X - \bar{X}) \]
Symbol Guide:
  • \(\bar{Y}\) = Arithmetic mean of variable \(Y\) (\(\bar{Y} = \frac{\sum Y}{n}\)).
  • \(\bar{X}\) = Arithmetic mean of variable \(X\) (\(\bar{X} = \frac{\sum X}{n}\)).
  • \(b_{yx}\) = Regression Coefficient of \(Y\) on \(X\) (represents the slope of line \(Y\) on \(X\)).

2. Line of Regression of \(X\) on \(Y\) (Definition & Formulas)

The line of regression of \(X\) on \(Y\) in point-slope form is given by:

\[ (X - \bar{X}) = b_{xy} (Y - \bar{Y}) \]
Symbol Guide:
  • \(\bar{X}, \bar{Y}\) = Sample means of \(X\) and \(Y\).
  • \(b_{xy}\) = Regression Coefficient of \(X\) on \(Y\) (represents the slope of line \(X\) on \(Y\)).

Step-by-Step Mathematical Derivation using Principle of Least Squares

Let the line of regression of \(Y\) on \(X\) be represented as \(Y = a + bX\). For an observed data point \((x_i, y_i)\), the predicted value is \(\hat{y}_i = a + b x_i\). The vertical residual is \(e_i = y_i - (a + b x_i)\).

According to the Principle of Least Squares, we minimize the sum of squared residuals \(S\):

\[ S = \sum_{i=1}^{n} e_i^2 = \sum_{i=1}^{n} (y_i - a - b x_i)^2 \]

To minimize \(S\), we differentiate \(S\) partially with respect to \(a\) and \(b\) and equate to zero:

\[ \frac{\partial S}{\partial a} = -2 \sum (y_i - a - b x_i) = 0 \implies \mathbf{\sum Y = n a + b \sum X} \quad \text{--- Normal Equation (1)} \] \[ \frac{\partial S}{\partial b} = -2 \sum x_i (y_i - a - b x_i) = 0 \implies \mathbf{\sum XY = a \sum X + b \sum X^2} \quad \text{--- Normal Equation (2)} \]

Dividing Normal Equation (1) by \(n\) gives \(a = \bar{Y} - b \bar{X}\). Substituting \(a\) into Normal Equation (2) yields:

\[ \sum XY = (\bar{Y} - b \bar{X}) \sum X + b \sum X^2 \implies b = \frac{\sum XY - \bar{Y} \sum X}{\sum X^2 - \bar{X} \sum X} = \frac{\sum (X - \bar{X})(Y - \bar{Y})}{\sum (X - \bar{X})^2} \]

Hence, the regression slope \(b_{yx}\) can be written as:

\[ b_{yx} = \frac{\text{Cov}(X,Y)}{\text{Var}(X)} = \frac{\text{Cov}(X,Y)}{\sigma_x^2} = r \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x} \]

Substituting \(a = \bar{Y} - b_{yx} \bar{X}\) back into \(Y = a + b_{yx} X\) gives the final point-slope form: \(\mathbf{(Y - \bar{Y}) = b_{yx} (X - \bar{X})}\).

Computational Formulas Box for Regression Coefficients

\[ b_{yx} = \frac{n \sum XY - \sum X \sum Y}{n \sum X^2 - (\sum X)^2} = \frac{\text{Cov}(X,Y)}{\sigma_x^2} = r \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x} \] \[ b_{xy} = \frac{n \sum XY - \sum X \sum Y}{n \sum Y^2 - (\sum Y)^2} = \frac{\text{Cov}(X,Y)}{\sigma_y^2} = r \frac{\sigma_x}{\sigma_y} \]

3.2 दो Regression Lines एवं उनकी व्युत्पत्ति (Two Lines of Regression & Derivation)

दो variables \(X\) तथा \(Y\) वाले bivariate data में, जब तक correlation perfect (\(r = \pm 1\)) न हो, हमेशा दो अलग-अलग regression lines प्राप्त होती हैं। एक अकेली रेखा दोनों दिशाओं के पूर्वानुमान नहीं कर सकती।

दो Regression Lines क्यों होती हैं? (शाब्दिक व्याख्या)

यह अंतर dependent तथा independent variables के चयन और error minimization की दिशा के कारण उत्पन्न होता है:

  1. Line of Regression of \(Y\) on \(X\): जब \(X\) known independent variable तथा \(Y\) unknown dependent variable होता है। यह vertical distances \(\sum (Y - \hat{Y})^2\) के योग को न्यूनतम करता है।
  2. Line of Regression of \(X\) on \(Y\): जब \(Y\) known independent variable तथा \(X\) unknown dependent variable होता है। यह horizontal distances \(\sum (X - \hat{X})^2\) के योग को न्यूनतम करता है।

1. Line of Regression of \(Y\) on \(X\) (परिभाषा एवं सूत्र)

Point-slope रूप में \(Y\) on \(X\) की regression line का समीकरण:

\[ (Y - \bar{Y}) = b_{yx} (X - \bar{X}) \]
Symbol Guide:
  • \(\bar{Y}\) = Variable \(Y\) का arithmetic mean (\(\bar{Y} = \frac{\sum Y}{n}\))।
  • \(\bar{X}\) = Variable \(X\) का arithmetic mean (\(\bar{X} = \frac{\sum X}{n}\))।
  • \(b_{yx}\) = Regression Coefficient of \(Y\) on \(X\) (\(Y\) on \(X\) रेखा की slope)।

2. Line of Regression of \(X\) on \(Y\) (परिभाषा एवं सूत्र)

Point-slope रूप में \(X\) on \(Y\) की regression line का समीकरण:

\[ (X - \bar{X}) = b_{xy} (Y - \bar{Y}) \]
Symbol Guide:
  • \(\bar{X}, \bar{Y}\) = \(X\) तथा \(Y\) के sample means।
  • \(b_{xy}\) = Regression Coefficient of \(X\) on \(Y\) (\(X\) on \(Y\) रेखा की slope)।

Principle of Least Squares द्वारा चरण-दर-चरण व्युत्पत्ति (Derivation)

माना \(Y\) on \(X\) की regression line \(Y = a + bX\) द्वारा व्यक्त की जाती है। किसी observed बिंदु \((x_i, y_i)\) के लिए predicted मान \(\hat{y}_i = a + b x_i\) है। Vertical residual \(e_i = y_i - (a + b x_i)\) है।

Principle of Least Squares के अनुसार, हम residuals के वर्गों के योग \(S\) को न्यूनतम करते हैं:

\[ S = \sum_{i=1}^{n} e_i^2 = \sum_{i=1}^{n} (y_i - a - b x_i)^2 \]

\(S\) को न्यूनतम करने हेतु, हम \(S\) का \(a\) तथा \(b\) के सापेक्ष आंशिक अवकलन (partial differentiation) करके शून्य के बराबर रखते हैं:

\[ \frac{\partial S}{\partial a} = -2 \sum (y_i - a - b x_i) = 0 \implies \mathbf{\sum Y = n a + b \sum X} \quad \text{--- Normal Equation (1)} \] \[ \frac{\partial S}{\partial b} = -2 \sum x_i (y_i - a - b x_i) = 0 \implies \mathbf{\sum XY = a \sum X + b \sum X^2} \quad \text{--- Normal Equation (2)} \]

Normal Equation (1) को \(n\) से विभाजित करने पर \(a = \bar{Y} - b \bar{X}\) प्राप्त होता है। \(a\) का मान Normal Equation (2) में रखने पर:

\[ \sum XY = (\bar{Y} - b \bar{X}) \sum X + b \sum X^2 \implies b = \frac{\sum (X - \bar{X})(Y - \bar{Y})}{\sum (X - \bar{X})^2} \]

अतः regression slope \(b_{yx}\) को लिखा जा सकता है:

\[ b_{yx} = \frac{\text{Cov}(X,Y)}{\text{Var}(X)} = \frac{\text{Cov}(X,Y)}{\sigma_x^2} = r \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x} \]

\(a = \bar{Y} - b_{yx} \bar{X}\) को \(Y = a + b_{yx} X\) में रखने पर अंतिम point-slope रूप प्राप्त होता है: \(\mathbf{(Y - \bar{Y}) = b_{yx} (X - \bar{X})}\)।

Regression Coefficients के गणना सूत्र (Computational Formulas)

\[ b_{yx} = \frac{n \sum XY - \sum X \sum Y}{n \sum X^2 - (\sum X)^2} = \frac{\text{Cov}(X,Y)}{\sigma_x^2} = r \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x} \] \[ b_{xy} = \frac{n \sum XY - \sum X \sum Y}{n \sum Y^2 - (\sum Y)^2} = \frac{\text{Cov}(X,Y)}{\sigma_y^2} = r \frac{\sigma_x}{\sigma_y} \]

Problem: Example 3.2.1 के data का उपयोग करके \(X\) on \(Y\) की regression line ज्ञात कीजिए तथा \(Y = 28\) होने पर \(X\) का अनुमान लगाइए।

Solution: Table कुल योग से: \(N = 5, \sum Y = 120, \sum Y^2 = 3318, \sum XY = 852\)।

\[ b_{xy} = \frac{5(852) - (30)(120)}{5(3318) - (120)^2} = \frac{660}{16590 - 14400} = \frac{660}{2190} = 0.301 \]

Regression line: \((X - 6) = 0.301 (Y - 24) \implies \mathbf{X = 0.301 Y - 1.224}\)।

\(Y = 28\) के लिए: \(X = 0.301(28) - 1.224 = \mathbf{7.20}\)।

3.3 6 Fundamental Properties of Regression Coefficients

Regression coefficients \(b_{yx}\) and \(b_{xy}\) possess 6 fundamental mathematical properties that govern their behavior, algebraic limits, and relationships with correlation coefficient \(r\).

Property 1: Geometric Mean Rule

Statement: The correlation coefficient \(r\) is the geometric mean of the two regression coefficients \(b_{yx}\) and \(b_{xy}\).

\[ r = \pm \sqrt{b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy}} \]

Verbal Explanation & Proof: Multiplying the correlation forms of regression coefficients:

\[ b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy} = \left(r \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}\right) \cdot \left(r \frac{\sigma_x}{\sigma_y}\right) = r^2 \]

Taking square root gives \(r = \pm \sqrt{b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy}}\). The sign of \(r\) matches the common sign of both coefficients.

Property 2: Same Sign Rule

Statement: Both regression coefficients \(b_{yx}\) and \(b_{xy}\) MUST have the exact same sign, which is also identical to the sign of correlation coefficient \(r\).

Verbal Explanation: Standard deviations \(\sigma_x > 0\) and \(\sigma_y > 0\) are strictly positive numbers. Since \(b_{yx} = r \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}\) and \(b_{xy} = r \frac{\sigma_x}{\sigma_y}\), the algebraic sign of both coefficients is entirely governed by the sign of \(r\).

Property 3: Magnitude Reciprocity Rule

Statement: If one regression coefficient is strictly greater than 1 in absolute value (\(|b_{yx}| > 1\)), then the other regression coefficient MUST be strictly less than 1 (\(|b_{xy}| < 1\)).

Verbal Explanation & Proof: We know \(b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy} = r^2\). Since \(|r| \le 1 \implies r^2 \le 1\), their product cannot exceed 1. Thus if \(|b_{yx}| > 1\), it forces \(|b_{xy}| \le \frac{1}{|b_{yx}|} < 1\).

Property 4: Arithmetic Mean Inequality

Statement: The arithmetic mean of the two regression coefficients is always greater than or equal to the correlation coefficient \(r\).

\[ \frac{b_{yx} + b_{xy}}{2} \ge |r| \]

Proof: Consider the perfect square \((\sqrt{b_{yx}} - \sqrt{b_{xy}})^2 \ge 0 \implies b_{yx} + b_{xy} - 2\sqrt{b_{yx} b_{xy}} \ge 0 \implies b_{yx} + b_{xy} \ge 2|r| \implies \frac{b_{yx} + b_{xy}}{2} \ge |r|\).

Property 5: Independence of Origin & Scale Dependence

Statement: Regression coefficients are completely independent of shift of origin, but they are dependent on change of scale.

Proof: Let \(u = \frac{X - a}{h}\) and \(v = \frac{Y - b}{k}\) where \(a, b\) are origin constants and \(h, k\) are scale factors. Then \(X = a + h u\) and \(Y = b + k v\). Substituting into covariance and variance formulas:

\[ b_{yx} = \left(\frac{k}{h}\right) b_{vu}, \quad \text{and} \quad b_{xy} = \left(\frac{h}{k}\right) b_{uv} \]

If scale factors are equal (\(h = k\)), then regression coefficients remain invariant.

Property 6: Point of Intersection at Means

Statement: The two regression lines always intersect at the point of means \((\bar{X}, \bar{Y})\).

Proof: Substituting \(X = \bar{X}\) into line \(Y - \bar{Y} = b_{yx} (X - \bar{X})\) yields \(Y - \bar{Y} = 0 \implies Y = \bar{Y}\). Thus \((\bar{X}, \bar{Y})\) satisfies both equations.

Example 3.3.1 (Verification of Properties):

Problem: Given regression coefficients \(b_{yx} = 1.2\) and \(b_{xy} = 0.3\). 1) Calculate correlation coefficient \(r\), 2) Verify Same Sign rule, 3) Verify Arithmetic Mean inequality.

Solution:

  1. \(r = +\sqrt{b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy}} = \sqrt{1.2 \times 0.3} = \sqrt{0.36} = \mathbf{+0.6}\).
  2. Both \(b_{yx} = +1.2\) and \(b_{xy} = +0.3\) are positive, matching \(r = +0.6\) (Same Sign Rule Verified).
  3. Arithmetic mean \(= \frac{1.2 + 0.3}{2} = 0.75\). Since \(0.75 \ge 0.6\), the AM Inequality \(\frac{b_{yx} + b_{xy}}{2} \ge r\) is Verified.

Example 3.3.2 (Origin & Scale Transformation):

Problem: If variables are transformed to \(u = \frac{X - 100}{10}\) and \(v = \frac{Y - 50}{5}\), and the calculated regression coefficient of \(v\) on \(u\) is \(b_{vu} = 0.8\), find the original regression coefficient \(b_{yx}\).

Solution: Here scale factors are \(h = 10\) and \(k = 5\). Using Property 5:

\[ b_{yx} = \left(\frac{k}{h}\right) b_{vu} = \left(\frac{5}{10}\right) (0.8) = 0.5 \times 0.8 = \mathbf{0.4} \]

3.3 Regression Coefficients के 6 मौलिक गुण (6 Fundamental Properties)

Regression coefficients \(b_{yx}\) तथा \(b_{xy}\) के 6 मौलिक गणितीय गुण होते हैं जो उनके व्यवहार, बीजीय सीमाओं तथा correlation coefficient \(r\) के साथ संबंधों को नियंत्रित करते हैं।

Property 1: Geometric Mean Rule

कथन: Correlation coefficient \(r\), दोनों regression coefficients \(b_{yx}\) तथा \(b_{xy}\) का geometric mean होता है।

\[ r = \pm \sqrt{b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy}} \]

शाब्दिक व्याख्या एवं प्रमाण: दोनों regression coefficients का गुणा करने पर:

\[ b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy} = \left(r \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}\right) \cdot \left(r \frac{\sigma_x}{\sigma_y}\right) = r^2 \]

वर्गमूल लेने पर \(r = \pm \sqrt{b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy}}\) प्राप्त होता है। \(r\) का चिन्ह दोनों coefficients के उभयनिष्ठ चिन्ह के समान होता है।

Property 2: Same Sign Rule

कथन: दोनों regression coefficients \(b_{yx}\) तथा \(b_{xy}\) का चिन्ह हमेशा अनिवार्य रूप से समान होता है, जो correlation coefficient \(r\) के चिन्ह के समान होता है।

शाब्दिक व्याख्या: Standard deviations \(\sigma_x > 0\) तथा \(\sigma_y > 0\) धनात्मक संख्याएँ हैं। चूँकि \(b_{yx} = r \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}\) तथा \(b_{xy} = r \frac{\sigma_x}{\sigma_y}\), अतः दोनों का चिन्ह पूर्णतः \(r\) के चिन्ह पर निर्भर करता है।

Property 3: Magnitude Reciprocity Rule

कथन: यदि एक regression coefficient निरपेक्ष मान में 1 से बड़ा है (\(|b_{yx}| > 1\)), तो दूसरा अनिवार्य रूप से 1 से छोटा होगा (\(|b_{xy}| < 1\))।

शाब्दिक व्याख्या एवं प्रमाण: हम जानते हैं कि \(b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy} = r^2\)। चूँकि \(|r| \le 1 \implies r^2 \le 1\), अतः उनका गुणनफल 1 से अधिक नहीं हो सकता। यदि \(|b_{yx}| > 1\) है, तो यह बाध्य करता है कि \(|b_{xy}| \le \frac{1}{|b_{yx}|} < 1\)।

Property 4: Arithmetic Mean Inequality

कथन: दोनों regression coefficients का arithmetic mean हमेशा correlation coefficient \(r\) के निरपेक्ष मान से बड़ा या बराबर होता है।

\[ \frac{b_{yx} + b_{xy}}{2} \ge |r| \]

प्रमाण: पूर्ण वर्ग \((\sqrt{b_{yx}} - \sqrt{b_{xy}})^2 \ge 0 \implies b_{yx} + b_{xy} - 2\sqrt{b_{yx} b_{xy}} \ge 0 \implies b_{yx} + b_{xy} \ge 2|r| \implies \frac{b_{yx} + b_{xy}}{2} \ge |r|\)।

Property 5: Independence of Origin & Scale Dependence

कथन: Regression coefficients shift of origin से पूर्णतः स्वतंत्र होते हैं, परंतु change of scale पर निर्भर करते हैं।

प्रमाण: माना \(u = \frac{X - a}{h}\) तथा \(v = \frac{Y - b}{k}\) जहाँ \(a, b\) origin constants हैं तथा \(h, k\) scale factors हैं। तब \(X = a + h u\) तथा \(Y = b + k v\)। Covariance एवं variance सूत्रों में रखने पर:

\[ b_{yx} = \left(\frac{k}{h}\right) b_{vu}, \quad \text{तथा} \quad b_{xy} = \left(\frac{h}{k}\right) b_{uv} \]

यदि scale factors समान हों (\(h = k\)), तो regression coefficients अपरिवर्तित रहते हैं।

Property 6: Point of Intersection at Means

कथन: दोनों regression lines हमेशा means के बिंदु \((\bar{X}, \bar{Y})\) पर प्रतिच्छेद करती हैं।

प्रमाण: रेखा \(Y - \bar{Y} = b_{yx} (X - \bar{X})\) में \(X = \bar{X}\) रखने पर \(Y - \bar{Y} = 0 \implies Y = \bar{Y}\)। अतः \((\bar{X}, \bar{Y})\) दोनों समीकरणों को संतुष्ट करता है।

Example 3.3.1 (Verification of Properties):

Problem: दी गई regression coefficients \(b_{yx} = 1.2\) तथा \(b_{xy} = 0.3\)। 1) Correlation coefficient \(r\) ज्ञात कीजिए, 2) Same Sign नियम सत्यापित कीजिए, 3) Arithmetic Mean असमानता सत्यापित कीजिए।

Solution:

  1. \(r = +\sqrt{b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy}} = \sqrt{1.2 \times 0.3} = \sqrt{0.36} = \mathbf{+0.6}\)।
  2. दोनों \(b_{yx} = +1.2\) तथा \(b_{xy} = +0.3\) धनात्मक हैं, जो \(r = +0.6\) से मेल खाते हैं (Same Sign Rule Verified)।
  3. Arithmetic mean \(= \frac{1.2 + 0.3}{2} = 0.75\)। चूँकि \(0.75 \ge 0.6\), अतः AM Inequality \(\frac{b_{yx} + b_{xy}}{2} \ge r\) सत्यापित हुई।

Example 3.3.2 (Origin & Scale Transformation):

Problem: यदि variables को \(u = \frac{X - 100}{10}\) तथा \(v = \frac{Y - 50}{5}\) में रूपांतरित किया जाता है, तथा \(v\) on \(u\) का परिकलित regression coefficient \(b_{vu} = 0.8\) है, तो मूल regression coefficient \(b_{yx}\) ज्ञात कीजिए।

Solution: यहाँ scale factors \(h = 10\) तथा \(k = 5\) हैं। Property 5 का उपयोग करने पर:

\[ b_{yx} = \left(\frac{k}{h}\right) b_{vu} = \left(\frac{5}{10}\right) (0.8) = 0.5 \times 0.8 = \mathbf{0.4} \]

3.4 Angle Between Two Regression Lines (\(\theta\))

The acute angle \(\theta\) between the line of regression of \(Y\) on \(X\) and the line of regression of \(X\) on \(Y\) provides a geometric measure of the degree of linear association between the two variables.

Derivation of Formula for Angle \(\theta\)

Let slope of line \(Y\) on \(X\) be \(m_1 = b_{yx} = r \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}\). Slope of line \(X\) on \(Y\) written as \(Y = \frac{1}{b_{xy}} X + c\) is \(m_2 = \frac{1}{b_{xy}} = \frac{1}{r} \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}\).

Using standard coordinate geometry formula for angle between two lines \(\tan \theta = \left|\frac{m_1 - m_2}{1 + m_1 m_2}\right|\):

\[ \tan \theta = \left| \frac{r \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x} - \frac{1}{r} \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}}{1 + \left(r \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}\right) \left(\frac{1}{r} \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}\right)} \right| = \left| \frac{\left(r - \frac{1}{r}\right) \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}}{1 + \frac{\sigma_y^2}{\sigma_x^2}} \right| = \left( \frac{1 - r^2}{|r|} \right) \left( \frac{\sigma_x \sigma_y}{\sigma_x^2 + \sigma_y^2} \right) \]

Formula Box for Angle \(\theta\)

\[ \tan \theta = \left( \frac{1 - r^2}{|r|} \right) \left( \frac{\sigma_x \sigma_y}{\sigma_x^2 + \sigma_y^2} \right) \]

Textual Analysis of the Two Extreme Cases

  1. Case 1: Perfect Correlation (\(r = \pm 1\)) \(\implies \theta = 0^\circ\)
    When correlation is perfect (\(r = +1\) or \(-1\)), \(\tan \theta = \left(\frac{1 - 1}{1}\right) (\dots) = 0 \implies \mathbf{\theta = 0^\circ}\). Geometrically, both regression lines coincide with each other into a single line. All observed points lie perfectly on the line.
  2. Case 2: Zero Correlation (\(r = 0\)) \(\implies \theta = 90^\circ\)
    When variables are completely uncorrelated (\(r = 0\)), \(\tan \theta = \left(\frac{1 - 0}{0}\right) (\dots) = \infty \implies \mathbf{\theta = 90^\circ}\). Geometrically, the two regression lines are strictly perpendicular to each other. Line \(Y\) on \(X\) becomes a horizontal line (\(Y = \bar{Y}\)) and line \(X\) on \(Y\) becomes a vertical line (\(X = \bar{X}\)).

Example 3.4.1 (Angle Calculation):

Problem: If standard deviation of \(X\) is \(\sigma_x = 3\), standard deviation of \(Y\) is \(\sigma_y = 4\), and correlation coefficient is \(r = 0.6\), calculate the acute angle \(\theta\) between the two regression lines.

Solution: Substitute values into the angle formula:

\[ \tan \theta = \left( \frac{1 - (0.6)^2}{0.6} \right) \left( \frac{3 \times 4}{3^2 + 4^2} \right) = \left( \frac{0.64}{0.6} \right) \left( \frac{12}{25} \right) = 1.0667 \times 0.48 = 0.512 \] \[ \tan \theta = 0.512 \implies \mathbf{\theta = \tan^{-1}(0.512) \approx 27.11^\circ} \]

Example 3.4.2 (Extreme Cases Verification):

Problem: Find the angle \(\theta\) between the regression lines when: 1) Correlation is perfect (\(r = 1\)), 2) Variables are uncorrelated (\(r = 0\)).

Solution:

  1. When \(r = 1\): \(\tan \theta = \left(\frac{1 - 1^2}{1}\right) (\cdots) = 0 \implies \mathbf{\theta = 0^\circ}\) (lines coincide completely).
  2. When \(r = 0\): \(\tan \theta = \left(\frac{1 - 0}{0}\right) (\cdots) = \infty \implies \mathbf{\theta = 90^\circ}\) (lines are perpendicular).

3.4 Regression Lines के बीच का कोण (\(\theta\))

\(Y\) on \(X\) की regression line तथा \(X\) on \(Y\) की regression line के बीच न्यून कोण (acute angle) \(\theta\), दो variables के बीच linear association की तीव्रता का ज्यामितीय माप प्रदान करता है।

कोण \(\theta\) के सूत्र की व्युत्पत्ति (Derivation)

माना रेखा \(Y\) on \(X\) की slope \(m_1 = b_{yx} = r \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}\) है। रेखा \(X\) on \(Y\) की slope \(m_2 = \frac{1}{b_{xy}} = \frac{1}{r} \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}\) है।

दो रेखाओं के बीच कोण का निर्देशांक ज्यामिति सूत्र \(\tan \theta = \left|\frac{m_1 - m_2}{1 + m_1 m_2}\right|\) प्रयुक्त करने पर:

\[ \tan \theta = \left( \frac{1 - r^2}{|r|} \right) \left( \frac{\sigma_x \sigma_y}{\sigma_x^2 + \sigma_y^2} \right) \]

कोण \(\theta\) का सूत्र Box

\[ \tan \theta = \left( \frac{1 - r^2}{|r|} \right) \left( \frac{\sigma_x \sigma_y}{\sigma_x^2 + \sigma_y^2} \right) \]

दो मुख्य स्थितियों का शाब्दिक विश्लेषण

  1. स्थिति 1: Perfect Correlation (\(r = \pm 1\)) \(\implies \theta = 0^\circ\)
    जब correlation perfect (\(r = +1\) या \(-1\)) होता है, \(\tan \theta = 0 \implies \mathbf{\theta = 0^\circ}\)। ज्यामितीय रूप से, दोनों regression lines एक-दूसरे के ऊपर पूरी तरह संपाती (coincide) हो जाती हैं। सभी observed बिंदु रेखा पर स्थित होते हैं।
  2. स्थिति 2: Zero Correlation (\(r = 0\)) \(\implies \theta = 90^\circ\)
    जब variables पूर्णतः uncorrelated (\(r = 0\)) होते हैं, \(\tan \theta = \infty \implies \mathbf{\theta = 90^\circ}\)। ज्यामितीय रूप से, दोनों regression lines एक-दूसरे पर लंबवत (perpendicular) होती हैं। रेखा \(Y\) on \(X\) क्षैतिज (\(Y = \bar{Y}\)) तथा रेखा \(X\) on \(Y\) ऊर्ध्वाधर (\(X = \bar{X}\)) हो जाती है।

Example 3.4.1 (Angle Calculation):

Problem: यदि \(X\) का standard deviation \(\sigma_x = 3\), \(Y\) का standard deviation \(\sigma_y = 4\), तथा correlation coefficient \(r = 0.6\) है, तो दोनों regression lines के बीच न्यून कोण \(\theta\) ज्ञात कीजिए।

Solution: कोण सूत्र में मान रखने पर:

\[ \tan \theta = \left( \frac{1 - (0.6)^2}{0.6} \right) \left( \frac{3 \times 4}{3^2 + 4^2} \right) = \left( \frac{0.64}{0.6} \right) \left( \frac{12}{25} \right) = 1.0667 \times 0.48 = 0.512 \] \[ \tan \theta = 0.512 \implies \mathbf{\theta = \tan^{-1}(0.512) \approx 27.11^\circ} \]

Example 3.4.2 (Extreme Cases Verification):

Problem: Regression lines के बीच कोण \(\theta\) ज्ञात कीजिए जब: 1) Correlation perfect (\(r = 1\)) है, 2) Variables uncorrelated (\(r = 0\)) हैं।

Solution:

  1. जब \(r = 1\): \(\tan \theta = 0 \implies \mathbf{\theta = 0^\circ}\) (रेखाएँ पूर्णतः संपाती हैं)।
  2. जब \(r = 0\): \(\tan \theta = \infty \implies \mathbf{\theta = 90^\circ}\) (रेखाएँ लंबवत हैं)।

3.5 Standard Error of Estimate (\(S_{yx}\) & \(S_{xy}\))

The Standard Error of Estimate measures the reliability, precision, or accuracy of predictions made by the fitted regression equation by evaluating the standard deviation of observed data points around the regression line.

Formulas for Standard Error of Estimate & Symbol Guide

\[ S_{yx} = \sqrt{\frac{\sum (Y - \hat{Y})^2}{n}} = \sigma_y \sqrt{1 - r^2} \] \[ S_{xy} = \sqrt{\frac{\sum (X - \hat{X})^2}{n}} = \sigma_x \sqrt{1 - r^2} \]
Symbol Guide:
  • \(S_{yx}\) = Standard Error of Estimate of \(Y\) on \(X\) (measured in units of \(Y\)).
  • \(S_{xy}\) = Standard Error of Estimate of \(X\) on \(Y\) (measured in units of \(X\)).
  • \(\sigma_y, \sigma_x\) = Standard deviations of variables \(Y\) and \(X\).
  • \(r\) = Simple correlation coefficient between \(X\) and \(Y\).

Partitioning Total Variance: Explained vs Unexplained Variation

The total variance in \(Y\) is \(\sigma_y^2\). It is partitioned into two components:

\[ \text{Total Variance } (\sigma_y^2) = \text{Explained Variance } (r^2 \sigma_y^2) + \text{Unexplained Variance } (S_{yx}^2) \]
  • Explained Variance (\(r^2 \sigma_y^2\)): The variation in \(Y\) accounted for by linear relationship with \(X\). \(r^2\) is called the Coefficient of Determination.
  • Unexplained Variance (\(S_{yx}^2 = \sigma_y^2 (1 - r^2)\)): The residual variance due to external unmeasured factors.

Example 3.5.1 (Standard Error S_yx):

Problem: In a study of crop production, standard deviation of yield \(Y\) is \(\sigma_y = 8\) quintals, and correlation with fertilizer \(X\) is \(r = 0.8\). Calculate the Standard Error of Estimate \(S_{yx}\) and state the proportion of unexplained variance.

Solution:

\[ S_{yx} = \sigma_y \sqrt{1 - r^2} = 8 \sqrt{1 - (0.8)^2} = 8 \sqrt{1 - 0.64} = 8 \sqrt{0.36} = 8 \times 0.6 = \mathbf{4.8 \text{ quintals}} \]

Proportion of explained variance \(= r^2 = (0.8)^2 = 0.64 = 64\%\).

Proportion of unexplained variance \(= 1 - r^2 = 0.36 = \mathbf{36\%}\).

Example 3.5.2 (Standard Error S_xy):

Problem: If standard deviation of fertilizer dose \(X\) is \(\sigma_x = 10\) kg and correlation is \(r = 0.6\), calculate \(S_{xy}\).

Solution:

\[ S_{xy} = \sigma_x \sqrt{1 - r^2} = 10 \sqrt{1 - (0.6)^2} = 10 \sqrt{0.64} = 10 \times 0.8 = \mathbf{8.0 \text{ kg}} \]

3.5 Standard Error of Estimate (\(S_{yx}\) एवं \(S_{xy}\))

Standard Error of Estimate fitted regression equation द्वारा किए गए पूर्वानुमानों की विश्वसनीयता, सटीकता (accuracy) तथा परिशुद्धता (precision) को regression line के चारों ओर observed डाटा बिंदुओं के मानक विचलन द्वारा मापता है।

Standard Error of Estimate के सूत्र एवं Symbol Guide

\[ S_{yx} = \sqrt{\frac{\sum (Y - \hat{Y})^2}{n}} = \sigma_y \sqrt{1 - r^2} \] \[ S_{xy} = \sqrt{\frac{\sum (X - \hat{X})^2}{n}} = \sigma_x \sqrt{1 - r^2} \]
Symbol Guide:
  • \(S_{yx}\) = Standard Error of Estimate of \(Y\) on \(X\) (\(Y\) की इकाइयों में माप)।
  • \(S_{xy}\) = Standard Error of Estimate of \(X\) on \(Y\) (\(X\) की इकाइयों में माप)।
  • \(\sigma_y, \sigma_x\) = Variables \(Y\) तथा \(X\) के standard deviations।
  • \(r\) = \(X\) तथा \(Y\) के बीच simple correlation coefficient।

Total Variance का विभाजन: Explained vs Unexplained Variation

\(Y\) में कुल variance \(\sigma_y^2\) है। इसे दो घटकों में विभाजित किया जाता है:

\[ \text{Total Variance } (\sigma_y^2) = \text{Explained Variance } (r^2 \sigma_y^2) + \text{Unexplained Variance } (S_{yx}^2) \]
  • Explained Variance (\(r^2 \sigma_y^2\)): \(X\) के साथ linear संबंध द्वारा व्याख्यायित variance। \(r^2\) को Coefficient of Determination कहते हैं।
  • Unexplained Variance (\(S_{yx}^2 = \sigma_y^2 (1 - r^2)\)): बाह्य अनावृत कारकों के कारण अवशिष्ट variance।

Example 3.5.1 (Standard Error S_yx):

Problem: फसल उत्पादन अध्ययन में, yield \(Y\) का standard deviation \(\sigma_y = 8\) क्विंटल है, तथा fertilizer \(X\) के साथ correlation \(r = 0.8\) है। Standard Error of Estimate \(S_{yx}\) की गणना कीजिए तथा unexplained variance का प्रतिशत बताइए।

Solution:

\[ S_{yx} = \sigma_y \sqrt{1 - r^2} = 8 \sqrt{1 - (0.8)^2} = 8 \sqrt{1 - 0.64} = 8 \sqrt{0.36} = 8 \times 0.6 = \mathbf{4.8 \text{ quintals}} \]

Explained variance का अनुपात \(= r^2 = (0.8)^2 = 0.64 = 64\%\)।

Unexplained variance का अनुपात \(= 1 - r^2 = 0.36 = \mathbf{36\%}\)।

Example 3.5.2 (Standard Error S_xy):

Problem: यदि fertilizer dose \(X\) का standard deviation \(\sigma_x = 10\) kg है तथा correlation \(r = 0.6\) है, तो \(S_{xy}\) की गणना कीजिए।

Solution:

\[ S_{xy} = \sigma_x \sqrt{1 - r^2} = 10 \sqrt{1 - (0.6)^2} = 10 \sqrt{0.64} = 10 \times 0.8 = \mathbf{8.0 \text{ kg}} \]

3.6 7-Point Comprehensive Distinction Between Correlation and Regression

Although Correlation and Regression both analyze bivariate numerical data, they differ fundamentally in purpose, variable symmetry, mathematical bounds, origin/scale invariance, and predictive capability.

# Basis of Comparison Correlation Analysis Regression Analysis
1 Meaning & Definition Measures the degree, strength, and direction of linear relationship between two variables. Establishes a functional cause-and-effect mathematical equation to predict unknown variable values.
2 Purpose & Objective To evaluate whether two variables vary together (co-vary). To estimate or forecast the unknown dependent variable (\(Y\)) for a known independent variable (\(X\)).
3 Nature of Variables (Symmetry) Symmetric: \(r_{xy} = r_{yx}\). Both variables \(X\) and \(Y\) are treated equally without dependent/independent classification. Asymmetric: \(b_{yx} \neq b_{xy}\). Variables are strictly classified into Dependent (\(Y\)) and Independent (\(X\)).
4 Mathematical Limits & Bounds Bounded strictly within \(-1 \le r \le +1\). Regression coefficients \(b_{yx}, b_{xy}\) can take any real value from \(-\infty\) to \(+\infty\).
5 Origin & Scale Transformations Independent of both shift of origin and change of scale. Independent of shift of origin, but **dependent on change of scale** (\(b_{yx} = \frac{k}{h} b_{vu}\)).
6 Error / Residual Term (\(e\)) Does not involve any residual error term. Explicitly incorporates a residual error term \(e_i = Y_i - \hat{Y}_i\) minimized by Least Squares.
7 Intercept & Forecasting Power Has no intercept term; cannot forecast quantitative values. Includes intercept parameter \(a\); explicitly engineered for quantitative forecasting.

Example 3.6.1 (Symmetry vs Asymmetry Demonstration):

Problem: Given \(\sigma_x = 4, \sigma_y = 8,\) and correlation \(r = 0.75\). Calculate: 1) Correlation coefficients \(r_{xy}\) and \(r_{yx}\), 2) Regression coefficients \(b_{yx}\) and \(b_{xy}\). Use these results to demonstrate symmetry vs asymmetry.

Solution:

  1. Correlation Symmetry: \(r_{xy} = r_{yx} = \mathbf{0.75}\) (Symmetric, independent of direction).
  2. Regression Asymmetry:
    \(b_{yx} = r \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x} = 0.75 \left(\frac{8}{4}\right) = 0.75 \times 2 = \mathbf{1.5}\).
    \(b_{xy} = r \frac{\sigma_x}{\sigma_y} = 0.75 \left(\frac{4}{8}\right) = 0.75 \times 0.5 = \mathbf{0.375}\).
    Since \(b_{yx} = 1.5 \neq b_{xy} = 0.375\), regression is Asymmetric.

Example 3.6.2 (Prediction Ability Demonstration):

Problem: Given \(\bar{X} = 5, \bar{Y} = 12,\) and \(b_{yx} = 1.4\). Demonstrate how regression line predicts \(Y\) when \(X = 8\), and explain why correlation coefficient \(r\) alone cannot perform this prediction.

Solution: Regression line of \(Y\) on \(X\): \((Y - 12) = 1.4 (X - 5) \implies Y = 1.4 X + 5\). For \(X = 8\): \(Y = 1.4(8) + 5 = \mathbf{16.2}\). Correlation coefficient \(r\) only indicates strength of relationship, but does not provide a linear intercept or slope to calculate specific predictions.

3.6 Correlation और Regression में 7 मुख्य अंतर (7-Point Distinction)

यद्यपि Correlation तथा Regression दोनों bivariate numerical data का विश्लेषण करते हैं, परंतु वे उद्देश्य, variable symmetry, गणितीय सीमाओं, origin/scale invariance, तथा predictive क्षमता में मौलिक रूप से भिन्न हैं।

# अंतर का आधार Correlation Analysis Regression Analysis
1 अर्थ एवं परिभाषा दो चरों के बीच linear संबंध की दिशा, तीव्रता एवं मात्रा को मापता है। अज्ञात variable मानों के पूर्वानुमान हेतु कारण-और-प्रभाव (cause-and-effect) गणितीय समीकरण स्थापित करता है।
2 उद्देश्य एवं अनुप्रयोग यह मूल्यांकन करना कि दो variables साथ-साथ बदलते हैं या नहीं। ज्ञात independent variable (\(X\)) से unknown dependent variable (\(Y\)) के मान का forecast करना।
3 सममिति (Symmetry) Symmetric: \(r_{xy} = r_{yx}\)। दोनों variables \(X\) तथा \(Y\) बिना वर्गीकृत किए समान रूप से माने जाते हैं। Asymmetric: \(b_{yx} \neq b_{xy}\)। Variables को strictly Dependent (\(Y\)) तथा Independent (\(X\)) में वर्गीकृत किया जाता है।
4 गणितीय सीमाएँ (Bounds) \(-1 \le r \le +1\) के भीतर सीमित। Regression coefficients \(b_{yx}, b_{xy}\) \(-\infty\) से \(+\infty\) तक कोई भी वास्तविक मान ले सकते हैं।
5 Origin & Scale रूपांतरण Origin के shift तथा Scale परिवर्तन दोनों से स्वतंत्र। Origin के shift से स्वतंत्र, परंतु **Scale परिवर्तन पर निर्भर** (\(b_{yx} = \frac{k}{h} b_{vu}\))।
6 Residual Error Term (\(e\)) किसी residual error term को शामिल नहीं करता। Residual error term \(e_i = Y_i - \hat{Y}_i\) शामिल करता है जिसे Least Squares द्वारा न्यूनतम किया जाता है।
7 Intercept & Prediction क्षमता कोई intercept नहीं होता; मान का पूर्वानुमान नहीं कर सकता। Intercept parameter \(a\) शामिल होता है; परिमाणात्मक forecasting हेतु डिज़ाइन किया गया है।

Example 3.6.1 (Symmetry vs Asymmetry Demonstration):

Problem: दी गई \(\sigma_x = 4, \sigma_y = 8,\) तथा correlation \(r = 0.75\)। गणना कीजिए: 1) Correlation coefficients \(r_{xy}\) एवं \(r_{yx}\), 2) Regression coefficients \(b_{yx}\) एवं \(b_{xy}\)। इन परिणामों का उपयोग करके symmetry vs asymmetry प्रदर्शित कीजिए।

Solution:

  1. Correlation Symmetry: \(r_{xy} = r_{yx} = \mathbf{0.75}\) (Symmetric, दिशा से स्वतंत्र)।
  2. Regression Asymmetry:
    \(b_{yx} = r \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x} = 0.75 \left(\frac{8}{4}\right) = 0.75 \times 2 = \mathbf{1.5}\)।
    \(b_{xy} = r \frac{\sigma_x}{\sigma_y} = 0.75 \left(\frac{4}{8}\right) = 0.75 \times 0.5 = \mathbf{0.375}\)।
    चूँकि \(b_{yx} = 1.5 \neq b_{xy} = 0.375\), अतः regression Asymmetric है।

Example 3.6.2 (Prediction Ability Demonstration):

Problem: दी गई \(\bar{X} = 5, \bar{Y} = 12,\) तथा \(b_{yx} = 1.4\)। प्रदर्शित कीजिए कि regression line \(X = 8\) पर \(Y\) का पूर्वानुमान कैसे करती है, तथा समझाएं कि correlation coefficient \(r\) अकेला यह पूर्वानुमान क्यों नहीं कर सकता।

Assume line (2) is \(X\) on \(Y\): \(X = 0.45 Y + 5.35 \implies b_{xy} = 0.45\).

Since both slopes are positive, \(r = +\sqrt{b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy}} = \sqrt{0.8 \times 0.45} = \sqrt{0.36} = \mathbf{+0.6}\).

Solved Example 3: Multiple Regression Plane for Agricultural Yield

Problem: For an agricultural experiment with Crop Yield (\(X_1\)), Fertilizer (\(X_2\)), and Rainfall (\(X_3\)), we have: \(\bar{X}_1 = 60, \bar{X}_2 = 20, \bar{X}_3 = 10\), \(\sigma_1 = 10, \sigma_2 = 4, \sigma_3 = 3\), and \(r_{12} = 0.8, r_{13} = 0.6, r_{23} = 0.5\). Find the multiple regression plane equation of \(X_1\) on \(X_2\) and \(X_3\).

Solution:

1) Partial regression coefficient \(b_{12.3}\):

\[ b_{12.3} = \left(\frac{10}{4}\right) \left[\frac{0.8 - (0.6)(0.5)}{1 - (0.5)^2}\right] = 2.5 \left[\frac{0.5}{0.75}\right] = 1.67 \]

2) Partial regression coefficient \(b_{13.2}\):

\[ b_{13.2} = \left(\frac{10}{3}\right) \left[\frac{0.6 - (0.8)(0.5)}{1 - (0.5)^2}\right] = 3.33 \left[\frac{0.2}{0.75}\right] = 0.89 \]

3) Regression Plane Equation:

\[ (X_1 - 60) = 1.67 (X_2 - 20) + 0.89 (X_3 - 10) \implies \mathbf{X_1 = 17.7 + 1.67 X_2 + 0.89 X_3} \]

Solved Example 1: Calculation of Bivariate Regression Lines

Problem: निम्नलिखित data के लिए दोनों regression lines ज्ञात कीजिए:

X12345
Y25387

\(X = 6\) होने पर \(Y\) का पूर्वानुमान कीजिए, तथा \(Y = 10\) होने पर \(X\) का पूर्वानुमान कीजिए।

Solution: \(N = 5\)। \(\sum X = 15 \implies \bar{X} = 3\)। \(\sum Y = 25 \implies \bar{Y} = 5\)।

\(\sum X^2 = 55\), \(\sum Y^2 = 151\), \(\sum XY = 89\)।

1) Regression Coefficient \(b_{yx}\):

\[ b_{yx} = \frac{5(89) - (15)(25)}{5(55) - (15)^2} = \frac{445 - 375}{275 - 225} = \frac{70}{50} = 1.4 \]

Regression Line of \(Y\) on \(X\): \((Y - 5) = 1.4 (X - 3) \implies \mathbf{Y = 1.4 X + 0.8}\)।

\(X = 6\) के लिए: \(Y = 1.4(6) + 0.8 = \mathbf{9.2}\)।

2) Regression Coefficient \(b_{xy}\):

\[ b_{xy} = \frac{5(89) - (15)(25)}{5(151) - (25)^2} = \frac{70}{755 - 625} = \frac{70}{130} = 0.538 \]

Regression Line of \(X\) on \(Y\): \((X - 3) = 0.538 (Y - 5) \implies \mathbf{X = 0.538 Y + 0.31}\)।

\(Y = 10\) के लिए: \(X = 0.538(10) + 0.31 = \mathbf{5.69}\)।

Solved Example 2: Finding Means and Correlation from Given Regression Lines

Problem: दो दी गई regression lines: \(8X - 10Y + 66 = 0\) तथा \(40X - 18Y - 214 = 0\)। ज्ञात कीजिए: 1) Means \(\bar{X}\) एवं \(\bar{Y}\), 2) Correlation coefficient \(r\)।

Solution:

1) दोनों रेखाएँ \((\bar{X}, \bar{Y})\) पर प्रतिच्छेद करती हैं। एक साथ हल करने पर:

\(8\bar{X} - 10\bar{Y} = -66\) ---(1), तथा \(40\bar{X} - 18\bar{Y} = 214\) ---(2)।

Eq (1) को 5 से गुणा करने पर: \(40\bar{X} - 50\bar{Y} = -330\)। Eq (2) से घटाने पर: \(32\bar{Y} = 544 \implies \mathbf{\bar{Y} = 17}\)।

Eq (1) में रखने पर: \(8\bar{X} = 10(17) - 66 = 104 \implies \mathbf{\bar{X} = 13}\)। Means हैं \(\mathbf{(\bar{X} = 13, \bar{Y} = 17)}\)।

2) मानिए रेखा (1) \(Y\) on \(X\) है: \(Y = 0.8 X + 6.6 \implies b_{yx} = 0.8\)।

मानिए रेखा (2) \(X\) on \(Y\) है: \(X = 0.45 Y + 5.35 \implies b_{xy} = 0.45\)।

चूँकि दोनों slopes धनात्मक हैं, \(r = +\sqrt{b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy}} = \sqrt{0.8 \times 0.45} = \sqrt{0.36} = \mathbf{+0.6}\)।

Solved Example 3: Multiple Regression Plane for Agricultural Yield

📝 Subjective Questions — 30 Questions

Q1. Define Linear Regression. Explain Sir Francis Galton's historical concept of regression.
Solution:

Linear Regression is the statistical method of modeling the functional relationship between a dependent variable \(Y\) and one or more independent variables \(X\) to predict \(Y\). History: Introduced by Sir Francis Galton in 1885 during biometric studies on human inheritance (heights of fathers and sons). Galton observed that tall fathers tend to have tall sons, but the sons' average height reverted back toward the general population mean. He called this phenomenon 'Regression to Mediocrity'.

Q2. Derive the equations for the two lines of regression (\(Y\) on \(X\) and \(X\) on \(Y\)) using the Principle of Least Squares.
Solution:

1) Line of \(Y\) on \(X\) (\(Y = a + bX\)): Minimize \(S = \sum (y_i - a - b x_i)^2\). Setting \(\partial S/\partial a = 0 \implies \sum Y = n a + b \sum X\). Setting \(\partial S/\partial b = 0 \implies \sum XY = a \sum X + b \sum X^2\). Solving gives \(b = b_{yx} = \frac{\text{Cov}(X,Y)}{\text{Var}(X)} = r \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}\) and \(a = \bar{Y} - b_{yx} \bar{X}\). Hence line is \((Y - \bar{Y}) = b_{yx} (X - \bar{X})\). 2) Line of \(X\) on \(Y\) (\(X = c + dY\)): Minimize \(S = \sum (x_i - c - d y_i)^2\). Solving normal equations gives \(d = b_{xy} = \frac{\text{Cov}(X,Y)}{\text{Var}(Y)} = r \frac{\sigma_x}{\sigma_y}\) and \(c = \bar{X} - b_{xy} \bar{Y}\). Hence line is \((X - \bar{X}) = b_{xy} (Y - \bar{Y})\).

Q3. State and prove the 5 fundamental properties of regression coefficients.
Solution:

1) Geometric Mean: \(r = \pm \sqrt{b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy}}\). Proof: \(b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy} = \left(r \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}\right) \left(r \frac{\sigma_x}{\sigma_y}\right) = r^2 \implies r = \pm \sqrt{b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy}}\). 2) Same Sign: Both \(b_{yx}\) and \(b_{xy}\) must have the same sign as \(r\) because \(\sigma_x > 0\) and \(\sigma_y > 0\). 3) Magnitude Reciprocity: If \(|b_{yx}| > 1\), then \(|b_{xy}| < 1\) because \(b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy} = r^2 \le 1\). 4) Arithmetic Mean Inequality: \(\frac{b_{yx} + b_{xy}}{2} \ge |r|\). Proof: \((\sqrt{b_{yx}} - \sqrt{b_{xy}})^2 \ge 0 \implies b_{yx} + b_{xy} \ge 2 \sqrt{b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy}} = 2|r|\). 5) Change of Origin and Scale: Independent of origin, dependent on scale: \(b_{yx} = \left(\frac{k}{h}\right) b_{vu}\).

Q4. Derive the formula for the angle \(\theta\) between the two lines of regression.
Solution:

Slopes of regression lines: \(m_1 = b_{yx} = r \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}\) and \(m_2 = \frac{1}{b_{xy}} = \frac{1}{r} \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}\). Angle formula: \(\tan \theta = \left| \frac{m_1 - m_2}{1 + m_1 m_2} \right| = \left(\frac{1 - r^2}{|r|}\right) \left(\frac{\sigma_x \sigma_y}{\sigma_x^2 + \sigma_y^2}\right)\). Special cases: 1) If \(r = \pm 1\), \(\tan \theta = 0 \implies \theta = 0^\circ\) (lines coincide). 2) If \(r = 0\), \(\tan \theta = \infty \implies \theta = 90^\circ\) (lines perpendicular).

Q5. Define Standard Error of Estimate \(S_{yx}\). Derive its expression in terms of \(r\) and \(\sigma_y\).
Solution:

Standard Error of Estimate \(S_{yx}\) measures the dispersion of observed \(Y\) values around the fitted regression line: \(S_{yx} = \sqrt{\frac{\sum (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2}{n}}\). Substituting \(\hat{y}_i = \bar{Y} + b_{yx} (x_i - \bar{X})\): \(S_{yx}^2 = \frac{1}{n} \sum [(y_i - \bar{Y}) - b_{yx} (x_i - \bar{X})]^2 = \sigma_y^2 (1 - r^2)\). Thus \(S_{yx} = \sigma_y \sqrt{1 - r^2}\).

Q6. Distinguish clearly between Correlation and Regression analysis.
Solution:

1) Meaning: Correlation measures strength of linear association; Regression models cause-and-effect functional prediction. 2) Variables: Correlation treats \(X\) and \(Y\) symmetrically; Regression treats them asymmetrically (\(Y\) dependent, \(X\) independent). 3) Coefficient Limits: \(r\) lies in \([-1, +1]\); regression coefficients \(b_{yx}, b_{xy}\) can exceed 1. 4) Origin & Scale: \(r\) is independent of both origin and scale; \(b_{yx}\) is independent of origin but dependent on scale. 5) Prediction: Correlation cannot predict values; Regression enables predicting \(Y\) for a given \(X\).

Q7. Explain Trivariate Multiple Linear Regression. Write the equation of the regression plane and define partial regression coefficients.
Solution:

Trivariate Multiple Regression involves 3 variables (\(X_1, X_2, X_3\)) where \(X_1\) is predicted from \(X_2\) and \(X_3\). Regression plane equation: \(X_1 - \bar{X}_1 = b_{12.3} (X_2 - \bar{X}_2) + b_{13.2} (X_3 - \bar{X}_3)\). Here \(b_{12.3}\) is the partial regression coefficient of \(X_1\) on \(X_2\) keeping \(X_3\) constant: \(b_{12.3} = \left(\frac{\sigma_1}{\sigma_2}\right) \left(\frac{r_{12} - r_{13} r_{23}}{1 - r_{23}^2}\right)\). Similarly \(b_{13.2} = \left(\frac{\sigma_1}{\sigma_3}\right) \left(\frac{r_{13} - r_{12} r_{23}}{1 - r_{23}^2}\right)\).

Q8. Explain Multiple Correlation Coefficient \(R_{1.23}\) and Partial Correlation Coefficient \(r_{12.3}\).
Solution:

1) Multiple Correlation \(R_{1.23}\): Measures the overall linear association between \(X_1\) and its combined joint linear estimate based on \(X_2\) and \(X_3\). Formula: \(R_{1.23}^2 = \frac{r_{12}^2 + r_{13}^2 - 2 r_{12} r_{13} r_{23}}{1 - r_{23}^2}\). Range: \(0 \le R_{1.23} \le 1\). 2) Partial Correlation \(r_{12.3}\): Measures the net correlation between \(X_1\) and \(X_2\) after eliminating the linear influence of \(X_3\). Formula: \(r_{12.3} = \frac{r_{12} - r_{13} r_{23}}{\sqrt{(1 - r_{13}^2)(1 - r_{23}^2)}}\). Range: \(-1 \le r_{12.3} \le +1\).

Means are \((\bar{X} = 13, \bar{Y} = 17)\). 2) Slopes: Assume \(Y\) on \(X\) is \(8X - 10Y + 66 = 0 \implies Y = 0.8 X + 6.6 \implies b_{yx} = 0.8\). Q1. Define Linear Regression. Explain Sir Francis Galton's historical concept of regression.
Solution:

Linear Regression is the statistical method of modeling the functional relationship between a dependent variable \(Y\) and one or more independent variables \(X\) to predict \(Y\). History: Introduced by Sir Francis Galton in 1885 during biometric studies on human inheritance (heights of fathers and sons). Galton observed that tall fathers tend to have tall sons, but the sons' average height reverted back toward the general population mean. He called this phenomenon 'Regression to Mediocrity'.

Q2. Derive the equations for the two lines of regression (\(Y\) on \(X\) and \(X\) on \(Y\)) using the Principle of Least Squares.
Solution:

1) Line of \(Y\) on \(X\) (\(Y = a + bX\)): Minimize \(S = \sum (y_i - a - b x_i)^2\). Setting \(\partial S/\partial a = 0 \implies \sum Y = n a + b \sum X\). Setting \(\partial S/\partial b = 0 \implies \sum XY = a \sum X + b \sum X^2\). Solving gives \(b = b_{yx} = \frac{\text{Cov}(X,Y)}{\text{Var}(X)} = r \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}\) and \(a = \bar{Y} - b_{yx} \bar{X}\). Hence line is \((Y - \bar{Y}) = b_{yx} (X - \bar{X})\). 2) Line of \(X\) on \(Y\) (\(X = c + dY\)): Minimize \(S = \sum (x_i - c - d y_i)^2\). Solving normal equations gives \(d = b_{xy} = \frac{\text{Cov}(X,Y)}{\text{Var}(Y)} = r \frac{\sigma_x}{\sigma_y}\) and \(c = \bar{X} - b_{xy} \bar{Y}\). Hence line is \((X - \bar{X}) = b_{xy} (Y - \bar{Y})\).

Q3. State and prove the 5 fundamental properties of regression coefficients.
Solution:

1) Geometric Mean: \(r = \pm \sqrt{b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy}}\). Proof: \(b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy} = \left(r \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}\right) \left(r \frac{\sigma_x}{\sigma_y}\right) = r^2 \implies r = \pm \sqrt{b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy}}\). 2) Same Sign: Both \(b_{yx}\) and \(b_{xy}\) must have the same sign as \(r\) because \(\sigma_x > 0\) and \(\sigma_y > 0\). 3) Magnitude Reciprocity: If \(|b_{yx}| > 1\), then \(|b_{xy}| < 1\) because \(b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy} = r^2 \le 1\). 4) Arithmetic Mean Inequality: \(\frac{b_{yx} + b_{xy}}{2} \ge |r|\). Proof: \((\sqrt{b_{yx}} - \sqrt{b_{xy}})^2 \ge 0 \implies b_{yx} + b_{xy} \ge 2 \sqrt{b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy}} = 2|r|\). 5) Change of Origin and Scale: Independent of origin, dependent on scale: \(b_{yx} = \left(\frac{k}{h}\right) b_{vu}\).

Q4. Derive the formula for the angle \(\theta\) between the two lines of regression.
Solution:

Slopes of regression lines: \(m_1 = b_{yx} = r \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}\) and \(m_2 = \frac{1}{b_{xy}} = \frac{1}{r} \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}\). Angle formula: \(\tan \theta = \left| \frac{m_1 - m_2}{1 + m_1 m_2} \right| = \left(\frac{1 - r^2}{|r|}\right) \left(\frac{\sigma_x \sigma_y}{\sigma_x^2 + \sigma_y^2}\right)\). Special cases: 1) If \(r = \pm 1\), \(\tan \theta = 0 \implies \theta = 0^\circ\) (lines coincide). 2) If \(r = 0\), \(\tan \theta = \infty \implies \theta = 90^\circ\) (lines perpendicular).

Q5. Define Standard Error of Estimate \(S_{yx}\). Derive its expression in terms of \(r\) and \(\sigma_y\).
Solution:

Standard Error of Estimate \(S_{yx}\) measures the dispersion of observed \(Y\) values around the fitted regression line: \(S_{yx} = \sqrt{\frac{\sum (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2}{n}}\). Substituting \(\hat{y}_i = \bar{Y} + b_{yx} (x_i - \bar{X})\): \(S_{yx}^2 = \frac{1}{n} \sum [(y_i - \bar{Y}) - b_{yx} (x_i - \bar{X})]^2 = \sigma_y^2 (1 - r^2)\). Thus \(S_{yx} = \sigma_y \sqrt{1 - r^2}\).

Q6. Distinguish clearly between Correlation and Regression analysis.
Solution:

1) Meaning: Correlation measures strength of linear association; Regression models cause-and-effect functional prediction. 2) Variables: Correlation treats \(X\) and \(Y\) symmetrically; Regression treats them asymmetrically (\(Y\) dependent, \(X\) independent). 3) Coefficient Limits: \(r\) lies in \([-1, +1]\); regression coefficients \(b_{yx}, b_{xy}\) can exceed 1. 4) Origin & Scale: \(r\) is independent of both origin and scale; \(b_{yx}\) is independent of origin but dependent on scale. 5) Prediction: Correlation cannot predict values; Regression enables predicting \(Y\) for a given \(X\).

Q7. Explain Trivariate Multiple Linear Regression. Write the equation of the regression plane and define partial regression coefficients.
Solution:

Trivariate Multiple Regression involves 3 variables (\(X_1, X_2, X_3\)) where \(X_1\) is predicted from \(X_2\) and \(X_3\). Regression plane equation: \(X_1 - \bar{X}_1 = b_{12.3} (X_2 - \bar{X}_2) + b_{13.2} (X_3 - \bar{X}_3)\). Here \(b_{12.3}\) is the partial regression coefficient of \(X_1\) on \(X_2\) keeping \(X_3\) constant: \(b_{12.3} = \left(\frac{\sigma_1}{\sigma_2}\right) \left(\frac{r_{12} - r_{13} r_{23}}{1 - r_{23}^2}\right)\). Similarly \(b_{13.2} = \left(\frac{\sigma_1}{\sigma_3}\right) \left(\frac{r_{13} - r_{12} r_{23}}{1 - r_{23}^2}\right)\).

Q8. Explain Multiple Correlation Coefficient \(R_{1.23}\) and Partial Correlation Coefficient \(r_{12.3}\).
Solution:

1) Multiple Correlation \(R_{1.23}\): Measures the overall linear association between \(X_1\) and its combined joint linear estimate based on \(X_2\) and \(X_3\). Formula: \(R_{1.23}^2 = \frac{r_{12}^2 + r_{13}^2 - 2 r_{12} r_{13} r_{23}}{1 - r_{23}^2}\). Range: \(0 \le R_{1.23} \le 1\). 2) Partial Correlation \(r_{12.3}\): Measures the net correlation between \(X_1\) and \(X_2\) after eliminating the linear influence of \(X_3\). Formula: \(r_{12.3} = \frac{r_{12} - r_{13} r_{23}}{\sqrt{(1 - r_{13}^2)(1 - r_{23}^2)}}\). Range: \(-1 \le r_{12.3} \le +1\).

Q9. The two regression lines are \(8X - 10Y + 66 = 0\) and \(40X - 18Y - 214 = 0\). Find: 1) Mean of \(X\) and \(Y\), 2) Correlation coefficient \(r\), 3) Variance of \(Y\) if \(\text{Var}(X) = 9\).
Solution:

1) Means: Solving \(8\bar{X} - 10\bar{Y} = -66\) and \(40\bar{X} - 18\bar{Y} = 214\). Multiply first by 5: \(40\bar{X} - 50\bar{Y} = -330\). Subtract from second: \(32\bar{Y} = 544 \implies \bar{Y} = 17\). Then \(8\bar{X} = 10(17) - 66 = 104 \implies \bar{X} = 13\). Means are \((\bar{X} = 13, \bar{Y} = 17)\). 2) Slopes: Assume \(Y\) on \(X\) is \(8X - 10Y + 66 = 0 \implies Y = 0.8 X + 6.6 \implies b_{yx} = 0.8\). Assume \(X\) on \(Y\) is \(40X - 18Y - 214 = 0 \implies X = 0.45 Y + 5.35 \implies b_{xy} = 0.45\). \(r = \sqrt{0.8 \times 0.45} = \sqrt{0.36} = +0.6\). 3) Variance of \(Y\): \(b_{yx} = r \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x} \implies 0.8 = 0.6 \frac{\sigma_y}{3} \implies \sigma_y = 4 \implies \text{Var}(Y) = 16\).
