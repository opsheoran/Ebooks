3.1 Concept & History of Regression

"Statistics is the grammar of science." — Karl Pearson. In real life and scientific investigations, we are frequently interested in estimating or predicting the value of one variable based on the known values of another variable. For example, an agricultural scientist wants to predict crop yield based on fertilizer dose, or an economist wants to forecast consumer expenditure based on national income. Regression analysis provides the mathematical framework for such estimation and cause-and-effect modeling.

Definition of Linear Regression

Linear Regression is the mathematical and statistical technique used to estimate or predict the unknown value of a dependent variable (\(Y\)) from the known value of an independent variable (\(X\)) by establishing a functional cause-and-effect relationship between them.

Historical Origin of the Term "Regression"

The term "Regression" (literally meaning "stepping back" or "returning to a former state") was coined by Sir Francis Galton in 1885 during his biometric studies on human genetics and heredity. Galton analyzed the heights of 928 adult children and their 205 pairs of parents.

Galton observed that tall parents tend to have tall children, and short parents tend to have short children. However, the average height of children of tall parents was less than their parents' height, while the average height of children of short parents was greater than their parents' height. In other words, human height tended to "regress" or "revert back" toward the general population mean height. Galton named this biological principle the "Law of Universal Regression to Mediocrity".

3.1 Regression की अवधारणा एवं इतिहास (Concept & History of Regression)

"Statistics is the grammar of science." — Karl Pearson. वास्तविक जीवन तथा प्रयोगात्मक विज्ञानों में, हम अक्सर किसी एक known variable के मान के आधार पर दूसरे unknown variable के मान का estimation (अनुमान) लगाने या prediction (पूर्वानुमान) करने में रुचि रखते हैं। उदाहरण के लिए, एक कृषि वैज्ञानिक fertilizer dose के आधार पर crop yield का पूर्वानुमान लगाना चाहता है। Regression Analysis ऐसे पूर्वानुमानों के लिए गणितीय ढांचा प्रदान करता है।

Linear Regression की परिभाषा

Linear Regression एक ऐसी सांख्यिकीय विधि है जिसके द्वारा independent variable (\(X\)) के known मान का उपयोग करके dependent variable (\(Y\)) के unknown मान का estimation लगाया जाता है।

"Regression" शब्द का ऐतिहासिक उद्गम

"Regression" शब्द (जिसका अर्थ है "पीछे लौटना") का प्रतिपादन Sir Francis Galton द्वारा 1885 में मानव आनुवंशिकी (biometrics) के अध्ययन के दौरान किया गया था। Galton ने 928 बच्चों तथा उनके 205 माता-पिता की ऊँचाइयों का विश्लेषण किया।

Galton ने पाया कि लंबे माता-पिता के बच्चे औसतन लंबे होते हैं और नाटे माता-पिता के बच्चे नाटे होते हैं, परंतु बच्चों की औसत ऊँचाई सामान्य जनसंख्या के औसत मान (\(\bar{Y}\)) की ओर "regress" (लौटने) की प्रवृत्ति दर्शाती है। इसे "Law of Universal Regression to Mediocrity" कहा गया।

3.2 Two Lines of Regression

In bivariate data, when variables \(X\) and \(Y\) are not perfectly correlated (\(r \neq \pm 1\)), we obtain two distinct lines of regression depending on which variable is chosen as dependent and which as independent.

1. Line of Regression of \(Y\) on \(X\)

This line is used to estimate or predict the unknown value of \(Y\) for a given known value of \(X\). Here \(Y\) is dependent variable and \(X\) is independent variable.

\[ (Y - \bar{Y}) = b_{yx} (X - \bar{X}) \]

where \(b_{yx}\) is the Regression Coefficient of \(Y\) on \(X\) (representing the slope of the line of \(Y\) on \(X\)).

\[ b_{yx} = r \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x} = \frac{\text{Cov}(X,Y)}{\sigma_x^2} = \frac{n \sum XY - \sum X \sum Y}{n \sum X^2 - (\sum X)^2} \]

2. Line of Regression of \(X\) on \(Y\)

This line is used to estimate or predict the unknown value of \(X\) for a given known value of \(Y\). Here \(X\) is dependent variable and \(Y\) is independent variable.

\[ (X - \bar{X}) = b_{xy} (Y - \bar{Y}) \]

where \(b_{xy}\) is the Regression Coefficient of \(X\) on \(Y\) (representing the slope of the line of \(X\) on \(Y\)).

\[ b_{xy} = r \frac{\sigma_x}{\sigma_y} = \frac{\text{Cov}(X,Y)}{\sigma_y^2} = \frac{n \sum XY - \sum X \sum Y}{n \sum Y^2 - (\sum Y)^2} \]

Derivation of Regression Equations using Least Squares

To fit the line \(Y = a + bX\), we minimize the sum of squared vertical residuals \(S = \sum (y_i - a - b x_i)^2\). Setting partial derivatives to zero:

\[ \frac{\partial S}{\partial a} = 0 \implies \sum Y = n a + b \sum X \] \[ \frac{\partial S}{\partial b} = 0 \implies \sum XY = a \sum X + b \sum X^2 \]

Solving these normal equations yields \(b = b_{yx} = \frac{\sum (X - \bar{X})(Y - \bar{Y})}{\sum (X - \bar{X})^2}\) and \(a = \bar{Y} - b_{yx} \bar{X}\). Substituting \(a\) into \(Y = a + bX\) gives \((Y - \bar{Y}) = b_{yx} (X - \bar{X})\).

3.2 दो Regression Lines (Two Lines of Regression)

Bivariate data में, जब \(X\) और \(Y\) में perfect correlation नहीं होता (\(r \neq \pm 1\)), तो हमारे पास दो अलग-अलग regression lines होती हैं:

1. Line of Regression of \(Y\) on \(X\)

\(X\) के ज्ञात मान के लिए \(Y\) का मान अनुमानित करने हेतु समीकरण:

\[ (Y - \bar{Y}) = b_{yx} (X - \bar{X}) \]

यहाँ \(b_{yx}\), \(Y\) on \(X\) का Regression Coefficient है:

\[ b_{yx} = r \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x} = \frac{n \sum XY - \sum X \sum Y}{n \sum X^2 - (\sum X)^2} \]

2. Line of Regression of \(X\) on \(Y\)

\(Y\) के ज्ञात मान के लिए \(X\) का मान अनुमानित करने हेतु समीकरण:

\[ (X - \bar{X}) = b_{xy} (Y - \bar{Y}) \]

यहाँ \(b_{xy}\), \(X\) on \(Y\) का Regression Coefficient है:

\[ b_{xy} = r \frac{\sigma_x}{\sigma_y} = \frac{n \sum XY - \sum X \sum Y}{n \sum Y^2 - (\sum Y)^2} \]

3.3 6 Fundamental Properties of Regression Coefficients

Property 1: Geometric Mean Rule

The correlation coefficient \(r\) is the geometric mean of the two regression coefficients \(b_{yx}\) and \(b_{xy}\):

\[ r = \pm \sqrt{b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy}} \]

Proof: Multiply the two regression coefficient formulas: \(b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy} = \left(r \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}\right) \cdot \left(r \frac{\sigma_x}{\sigma_y}\right) = r^2\). Taking square root on both sides yields \(r = \pm \sqrt{b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy}}\).

Property 2: Same Sign Rule

Both regression coefficients \(b_{yx}\) and \(b_{xy}\) MUST have the exact same sign (either both positive or both negative), which is also identical to the sign of the correlation coefficient \(r\).

Reason: Standard deviations \(\sigma_x > 0\) and \(\sigma_y > 0\) are strictly positive. Since \(b_{yx} = r \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}\) and \(b_{xy} = r \frac{\sigma_x}{\sigma_y}\), the sign of both coefficients is entirely determined by \(r\).

Property 3: Magnitude Reciprocity Rule

If one of the regression coefficients is strictly greater than 1 in absolute value (\(|b_{yx}| > 1\)), then the other regression coefficient MUST be strictly less than 1 (\(|b_{xy}| < 1\)).

Proof: Since \(b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy} = r^2\) and \(|r| \le 1\), we have \(|b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy}| \le 1\). If \(|b_{yx}| > 1\), it mathematically forces \(|b_{xy}| \le \frac{1}{|b_{yx}|} < 1\).

Property 4: Arithmetic Mean Inequality

The arithmetic mean of the two regression coefficients is always greater than or equal to the correlation coefficient \(r\):

\[ \frac{b_{yx} + b_{xy}}{2} \ge |r| \]

Proof: Since \((\sqrt{b_{yx}} - \sqrt{b_{xy}})^2 \ge 0 \implies b_{yx} + b_{xy} - 2\sqrt{b_{yx} b_{xy}} \ge 0 \implies b_{yx} + b_{xy} \ge 2|r| \implies \frac{b_{yx} + b_{xy}}{2} \ge |r|\).

Property 5: Independence of Origin but Dependence on Scale

Regression coefficients are completely independent of shift of origin, but they are dependent on change of scale.

If \(u = \frac{X - a}{h}\) and \(v = \frac{Y - b}{k}\), then:

\[ b_{yx} = \left(\frac{k}{h}\right) b_{vu}, \quad \text{and} \quad b_{xy} = \left(\frac{h}{k}\right) b_{uv} \]

Property 6: Point of Intersection

The point of intersection of the line of regression of \(Y\) on \(X\) and the line of regression of \(X\) on \(Y\) is strictly the point of means \((\bar{X}, \bar{Y})\).

3.3 Regression Coefficients के 6 मौलिक गुण (Properties)

Property 1: Geometric Mean Rule

Correlation coefficient \(r\), दोनों regression coefficients का geometric mean होता है:

\[ r = \pm \sqrt{b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy}} \]

Property 2: Same Sign Rule

दोनों regression coefficients \(b_{yx}\) और \(b_{xy}\) का चिन्ह (sign) हमेशा समान होता है (दोनों धनात्मक या दोनों ऋणात्मक)।

Property 3: Magnitude Reciprocity Rule

यदि एक regression coefficient मान में 1 से बड़ा है (\(|b_{yx}| > 1\)), तो दूसरा अनिवार्य रूप से 1 से छोटा होगा (\(|b_{xy}| < 1\))।

Property 4: Arithmetic Mean Inequality

दोनों regression coefficients का arithmetic mean हमेशा \(|r|\) से बड़ा या बराबर होता है: \(\frac{b_{yx} + b_{xy}}{2} \ge |r|\)।

Property 5: Independence of Origin & Scale Dependence

Regression coefficients, shift of origin से स्वतंत्र होते हैं, परंतु change of scale पर निर्भर करते हैं: \(b_{yx} = \left(\frac{k}{h}\right) b_{vu}\)।

Property 6: Intersection Point

दोनों regression lines हमेशा means के बिंदु \((\bar{X}, \bar{Y})\) पर प्रतिच्छेद करती हैं।

3.4 Angle Between Two Regression Lines (\(\theta\))

The angle \(\theta\) between the regression line of \(Y\) on \(X\) and the regression line of \(X\) on \(Y\) indicates the degree of linear relationship between the variables.

Formula for \(\tan \theta\):
\[ \tan \theta = \left( \frac{1 - r^2}{|r|} \right) \left( \frac{\sigma_x \sigma_y}{\sigma_x^2 + \sigma_y^2} \right) \]

Two Extreme Cases:

  1. Case 1: Perfect Correlation (\(r = \pm 1\))
    When \(r = \pm 1\), \(\tan \theta = 0 \implies \mathbf{\theta = 0^\circ}\). The two regression lines coincide with each other completely and form a single line.
  2. Case 2: Zero Correlation (\(r = 0\))
    When \(r = 0\), \(\tan \theta = \infty \implies \mathbf{\theta = 90^\circ}\). The two regression lines are perpendicular to each other (\(Y = \bar{Y}\) is horizontal, \(X = \bar{X}\) is vertical).

3.4 Regression Lines के बीच का कोण (\(\theta\))

दोनों regression lines के बीच का कोण \(\theta\), correlation की तीव्रता को दर्शाता है:

\(\tan \theta\) का सूत्र:
\[ \tan \theta = \left( \frac{1 - r^2}{|r|} \right) \left( \frac{\sigma_x \sigma_y}{\sigma_x^2 + \sigma_y^2} \right) \]

दो मुख्य स्थितियाँ (Extreme Cases):

  1. स्थिति 1: Perfect Correlation (\(r = \pm 1\)) \(\implies \theta = 0^\circ\) (दोनों रेखाएँ पूरी तरह से एक-दूसरे के ऊपर संपाती / coincide होती हैं)।
  2. स्थिति 2: Zero Correlation (\(r = 0\)) \(\implies \theta = 90^\circ\) (दोनों रेखाएँ एक-दूसरे पर लंबवत / perpendicular होती हैं)।

3.5 Standard Error of Estimate (\(S_{yx}\) & \(S_{xy}\))

The Standard Error of Estimate measures the reliability or accuracy of the estimated regression equation by calculating the scatter of observed data points around the fitted regression line.

Formulas for Standard Error of Estimate:
\[ S_{yx} = \sqrt{\frac{\sum (Y - \hat{Y})^2}{n}} = \sigma_y \sqrt{1 - r^2} \] \[ S_{xy} = \sqrt{\frac{\sum (X - \hat{X})^2}{n}} = \sigma_x \sqrt{1 - r^2} \]

If \(r = \pm 1\), \(S_{yx} = 0\), which means all points lie exactly on the line and prediction is 100% error-free. If \(r = 0\), \(S_{yx} = \sigma_y\), meaning the regression line provides no improvement over using the simple mean \(\bar{Y}\).

3.5 Standard Error of Estimate (\(S_{yx}\) एवं \(S_{xy}\))

Standard Error of Estimate, regression line द्वारा किए गए पूर्वानुमानों के मानक विचलन या त्रुटि को मापता है:

\[ S_{yx} = \sigma_y \sqrt{1 - r^2}, \quad S_{xy} = \sigma_x \sqrt{1 - r^2} \]

3.6 Distinction Between Correlation and Regression

Basis Correlation Analysis Regression Analysis
Meaning Measures strength and direction of linear association. Establishes functional cause-and-effect relationship for prediction.
Symmetry Symmetric: \(r_{xy} = r_{yx}\). No distinction between variables. Asymmetric: \(b_{yx} \neq b_{xy}\). Strictly distinguishes dependent (\(Y\)) and independent (\(X\)).
Limits Bounded strictly between \(-1 \le r \le +1\). Regression coefficients \(b_{yx}, b_{xy}\) can exceed 1 in magnitude.
Origin & Scale Independent of both origin shift and scale change. Independent of origin shift, but dependent on scale change.
Prediction Cannot be used to forecast unknown values. Specifically used for forecasting and prediction.

3.7 Multiple Regression & Partial Correlation (Trivariate Regression)

In many agricultural, biological, and economic studies, a dependent variable \(X_1\) is influenced simultaneously by two or more independent variables \(X_2, X_3\). For example, crop yield (\(X_1\)) depends on fertilizer dose (\(X_2\)) and rainfall (\(X_3\)).

1. Multiple Regression Plane Equation

The linear regression equation of \(X_1\) on \(X_2\) and \(X_3\) represents a plane in 3D space:

\[ (X_1 - \bar{X}_1) = b_{12.3} (X_2 - \bar{X}_2) + b_{13.2} (X_3 - \bar{X}_3) \]

where \(b_{12.3}\) and \(b_{13.2}\) are Partial Regression Coefficients.

Partial Regression Coefficients Formulas:
\[ b_{12.3} = \left( \frac{\sigma_1}{\sigma_2} \right) \left( \frac{r_{12} - r_{13} r_{23}}{1 - r_{23}^2} \right) \] \[ b_{13.2} = \left( \frac{\sigma_1}{\sigma_3} \right) \left( \frac{r_{13} - r_{12} r_{23}}{1 - r_{23}^2} \right) \]

2. Multiple Correlation Coefficient (\(R_{1.23}\))

Measures the overall correlation between observed \(X_1\) and its joint linear estimate based on \(X_2\) and \(X_3\):

\[ R_{1.23}^2 = \frac{r_{12}^2 + r_{13}^2 - 2 r_{12} r_{13} r_{23}}{1 - r_{23}^2}, \quad (0 \le R_{1.23} \le 1) \]

3. Partial Correlation Coefficient (\(r_{12.3}\))

Measures the net linear correlation between \(X_1\) and \(X_2\) after eliminating the influence of \(X_3\):

\[ r_{12.3} = \frac{r_{12} - r_{13} r_{23}}{\sqrt{(1 - r_{13}^2)(1 - r_{23}^2)}}, \quad (-1 \le r_{12.3} \le +1) \]

3.7 Multiple Regression एवं Partial Correlation (Trivariate Regression)

जब एक dependent variable \(X_1\) को दो या अधिक independent variables \(X_2, X_3\) द्वारा predict किया जाता है, तो Multiple Regression Plane का उपयोग किया जाता है:

Multiple Regression Plane Equation:

\[ (X_1 - \bar{X}_1) = b_{12.3} (X_2 - \bar{X}_2) + b_{13.2} (X_3 - \bar{X}_3) \]
Partial Regression Coefficients:
\[ b_{12.3} = \left( \frac{\sigma_1}{\sigma_2} \right) \left( \frac{r_{12} - r_{13} r_{23}}{1 - r_{23}^2} \right), \quad b_{13.2} = \left( \frac{\sigma_1}{\sigma_3} \right) \left( \frac{r_{13} - r_{12} r_{23}}{1 - r_{23}^2} \right) \]

3.8 Solved Examples

Solved Example 1: Calculation of Bivariate Regression Lines

Problem: Calculate the two lines of regression for the following data:

X12345
Y25387

Predict \(Y\) when \(X = 6\), and predict \(X\) when \(Y = 10\).

Solution: \(N = 5\). \(\sum X = 15 \implies \bar{X} = 3\). \(\sum Y = 25 \implies \bar{Y} = 5\).

\(\sum X^2 = 55\), \(\sum Y^2 = 151\), \(\sum XY = 89\).

1) Regression Coefficient \(b_{yx}\):

\[ b_{yx} = \frac{5(89) - (15)(25)}{5(55) - (15)^2} = \frac{445 - 375}{275 - 225} = \frac{70}{50} = 1.4 \]

Regression Line of \(Y\) on \(X\): \((Y - 5) = 1.4 (X - 3) \implies \mathbf{Y = 1.4 X + 0.8}\).

For \(X = 6\): \(Y = 1.4(6) + 0.8 = \mathbf{9.2}\).

2) Regression Coefficient \(b_{xy}\):

\[ b_{xy} = \frac{5(89) - (15)(25)}{5(151) - (25)^2} = \frac{70}{755 - 625} = \frac{70}{130} = 0.538 \]

Regression Line of \(X\) on \(Y\): \((X - 3) = 0.538 (Y - 5) \implies \mathbf{X = 0.538 Y + 0.31}\).

For \(Y = 10\): \(X = 0.538(10) + 0.31 = \mathbf{5.69}\).

Solved Example 2: Finding Means and Correlation from Given Regression Lines

Problem: Given two regression lines: \(8X - 10Y + 66 = 0\) and \(40X - 18Y - 214 = 0\). Find: 1) Means \(\bar{X}\) and \(\bar{Y}\), 2) Correlation coefficient \(r\).

Solution:

1) Both lines intersect at \((\bar{X}, \bar{Y})\). Solving simultaneously:

\(8\bar{X} - 10\bar{Y} = -66\) ---(1), and \(40\bar{X} - 18\bar{Y} = 214\) ---(2).

Multiply Eq (1) by 5: \(40\bar{X} - 50\bar{Y} = -330\). Subtracting from Eq (2): \(32\bar{Y} = 544 \implies \mathbf{\bar{Y} = 17}\).

Substituting into Eq (1): \(8\bar{X} = 10(17) - 66 = 104 \implies \mathbf{\bar{X} = 13}\).

2) Assume line (1) is \(Y\) on \(X\): \(Y = 0.8 X + 6.6 \implies b_{yx} = 0.8\).

Assume line (2) is \(X\) on \(Y\): \(X = 0.45 Y + 5.35 \implies b_{xy} = 0.45\).

Since both slopes are positive, \(r = +\sqrt{b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy}} = \sqrt{0.8 \times 0.45} = \sqrt{0.36} = \mathbf{+0.6}\).

Solved Example 3: Multiple Regression Plane for Agricultural Yield

Problem: For an agricultural experiment with Crop Yield (\(X_1\)), Fertilizer (\(X_2\)), and Rainfall (\(X_3\)), we have: \(\bar{X}_1 = 60, \bar{X}_2 = 20, \bar{X}_3 = 10\), \(\sigma_1 = 10, \sigma_2 = 4, \sigma_3 = 3\), and \(r_{12} = 0.8, r_{13} = 0.6, r_{23} = 0.5\). Find the multiple regression plane equation of \(X_1\) on \(X_2\) and \(X_3\).

Solution:

1) Partial regression coefficient \(b_{12.3}\):

\[ b_{12.3} = \left(\frac{10}{4}\right) \left[\frac{0.8 - (0.6)(0.5)}{1 - (0.5)^2}\right] = 2.5 \left[\frac{0.5}{0.75}\right] = 1.67 \]

2) Partial regression coefficient \(b_{13.2}\):

\[ b_{13.2} = \left(\frac{10}{3}\right) \left[\frac{0.6 - (0.8)(0.5)}{1 - (0.5)^2}\right] = 3.33 \left[\frac{0.2}{0.75}\right] = 0.89 \]

3) Regression Plane Equation:

\[ (X_1 - 60) = 1.67 (X_2 - 20) + 0.89 (X_3 - 10) \implies \mathbf{X_1 = 17.7 + 1.67 X_2 + 0.89 X_3} \]

Q1. Sir Francis Galton introduced regression while studying heights of fathers and sons. [T/F]

Q2. The line of regression of Y on X and X on Y are identical if correlation coefficient r = 0. [T/F]
Answer: False

If r = 0, the line Y on X is horizontal (Y = ȳ) and X on Y is vertical (X = x̄), making them perpendicular.

Q3. The correlation coefficient r is the geometric mean of the two regression coefficients b_yx and b_xy. [T/F]
Answer: True

r = ± √(b_yx * b_xy).

Q4. One regression coefficient can be +0.8 and the other can be -0.5. [T/F]
Answer: False

Both regression coefficients must have the exact same sign matching r.

Q5. If b_yx = 2.5, then b_xy can be 0.8. [T/F]
Answer: False

b_yx * b_xy = 2.5 * 0.8 = 2.0 > 1, which means r = √2 > 1 (impossible since |r| ≤ 1). Thus if one is 2.5, the other MUST be ≤ 0.4.

Answer: False

Regression coefficients are completely independent of origin shift.

b_yx = (k / h) * b_vu where h and k are scale factors.

Substituting X = x̄ gives Y = ȳ on both lines.

Q9. If r = ±1, the angle between the two regression lines is 0°. [T/F]

The two lines coincide completely when correlation is perfect.

S_yx = σ_y √(1 - 1) = 0.

📝 Subjective Questions — 30 Questions

Q1. Define Linear Regression. Explain Sir Francis Galton's historical concept of regression.
Solution:

Linear Regression is the statistical method of modeling the functional relationship between a dependent variable Y and one or more independent variables X to predict Y. History: Introduced by Sir Francis Galton in 1885 during biometric studies on human inheritance (heights of fathers and sons). Galton observed that tall fathers tend to have tall sons, but the sons' average height reverted back toward the general population mean. He called this phenomenon 'Regression to Mediocrity'.

Q2. Derive the equations for the two lines of regression (Y on X and X on Y) using the Principle of Least Squares.
Solution:

1) Line of Y on X (Y = a + bX): Minimize S = Σ (y_i - a - b x_i)². Setting ∂S/∂a = 0 ⇒ Σ Y = n a + b Σ X. Setting ∂S/∂b = 0 ⇒ Σ XY = a Σ X + b Σ X². Solving gives b = b_yx = Cov(X,Y)/Var(X) = r (σ_y/σ_x) and a = ȳ - b_yx x̄. Hence line is (Y - ȳ) = b_yx (X - x̄). 2) Line of X on Y (X = c + dY): Minimize S = Σ (x_i - c - d y_i)². Solving normal equations gives d = b_xy = Cov(X,Y)/Var(Y) = r (σ_x/σ_y) and c = x̄ - b_xy ȳ. Hence line is (X - x̄) = b_xy (Y - ȳ).

Q3. State and prove the 5 fundamental properties of regression coefficients.
Solution:

1) Geometric Mean: r = ± √(b_yx * b_xy). Proof: b_yx * b_xy = [r (σ_y/σ_x)] * [r (σ_x/σ_y)] = r² ⇒ r = ± √(b_yx * b_xy). 2) Same Sign: Both b_yx and b_xy must have the same sign as r because σ_x > 0 and σ_y > 0. 3) Magnitude Reciprocity: If |b_yx| > 1, then |b_xy| < 1 because b_yx * b_xy = r² ≤ 1. 4) Arithmetic Mean Inequality: (b_yx + b_xy)/2 ≥ |r|. Proof: (√b_yx - √b_xy)² ≥ 0 ⇒ b_yx + b_xy ≥ 2 √(b_yx * b_xy) = 2|r|. 5) Change of Origin and Scale: Independent of origin, dependent on scale: b_yx = (k / h) b_vu.

Q4. Derive the formula for the angle θ between the two lines of regression.
Solution:

Slopes of regression lines: m_1 = b_yx = r (σ_y/σ_x) and m_2 = 1 / b_xy = (1/r) (σ_y/σ_x). Angle formula: tan θ = |(m_1 - m_2) / (1 + m_1 m_2)| = |[r (σ_y/σ_x) - (1/r) (σ_y/σ_x)] / [1 + (σ_y²/σ_x²)]| = [(1 - r²) / |r|] * [σ_x σ_y / (σ_x² + σ_y²)]. Special cases: 1) If r = ±1, tan θ = 0 ⇒ θ = 0° (lines coincide). 2) If r = 0, tan θ = ∞ ⇒ θ = 90° (lines perpendicular).

Q5. Define Standard Error of Estimate S_yx. Derive its expression in terms of r and σ_y.
Solution:

Standard Error of Estimate S_yx measures the dispersion of observed Y values around the fitted regression line: S_yx = √[Σ (y_i - ŷ_i)² / n]. Substituting ŷ_i = ȳ + b_yx (x_i - x̄): S_yx² = (1/n) Σ [(y_i - ȳ) - b_yx (x_i - x̄)]² = (1/n) Σ (y_i - ȳ)² - 2 b_yx Cov(X,Y) + b_yx² (1/n) Σ (x_i - x̄)² = σ_y² - 2 [r (σ_y/σ_x)] [r σ_x σ_y] + [r² (σ_y²/σ_x²)] σ_x² = σ_y² - 2 r² σ_y² + r² σ_y² = σ_y² (1 - r²). Thus S_yx = σ_y √(1 - r²).

Q6. Distinguish clearly between Correlation and Regression analysis.
Solution:

1) Meaning: Correlation measures strength of linear association; Regression models cause-and-effect functional prediction. 2) Variables: Correlation treats X and Y symmetrically; Regression treats them asymmetrically (Y dependent, X independent). 3) Coefficient Limits: r lies in [-1, +1]; regression coefficients b_yx, b_xy can exceed 1. 4) Origin & Scale: r is independent of both origin and scale; b_yx is independent of origin but dependent on scale. 5) Prediction: Correlation cannot predict values; Regression enables predicting Y for a given X.

Q7. Explain Trivariate Multiple Linear Regression. Write the equation of the regression plane and define partial regression coefficients.
Solution:

Trivariate Multiple Regression involves 3 variables (X_1, X_2, X_3) where X_1 is predicted from X_2 and X_3. Regression plane equation: X_1 - x̄_1 = b_12.3 (X_2 - x̄_2) + b_13.2 (X_3 - x̄_3). Here b_12.3 is the partial regression coefficient of X_1 on X_2 keeping X_3 constant: b_12.3 = (σ_1 / σ_2) * [(r_12 - r_13 r_23) / (1 - r_23²)]. Similarly b_13.2 is the partial regression coefficient of X_1 on X_3 keeping X_2 constant: b_13.2 = (σ_1 / σ_3) * [(r_13 - r_12 r_23) / (1 - r_23²)].

Q8. Explain Multiple Correlation Coefficient R_1.23 and Partial Correlation Coefficient r_12.3.
Solution:

1) Multiple Correlation R_1.23: Measures the overall linear association between X_1 and its combined joint linear estimate based on X_2 and X_3. Formula: R_1.23² = (r_12² + r_13² - 2 r_12 r_13 r_23) / (1 - r_23²). Range: 0 ≤ R_1.23 ≤ 1. 2) Partial Correlation r_12.3: Measures the net correlation between X_1 and X_2 after eliminating the linear influence of X_3. Formula: r_12.3 = (r_12 - r_13 r_23) / √[(1 - r_13²)(1 - r_23²)]. Range: -1 ≤ r_12.3 ≤ +1.

Q9. The two regression lines are 8X - 10Y + 66 = 0 and 40X - 18Y - 214 = 0. Find: 1) Mean of X and Y, 2) Correlation coefficient r, 3) Variance of Y if Var(X) = 9.
Solution:

1) Means: Solving 8x̄ - 10ȳ = -66 and 40x̄ - 18ȳ = 214. Multiply first by 5: 40x̄ - 50ȳ = -330. Subtract from second: 32ȳ = 544 ⇒ ȳ = 17. Then 8x̄ = 10(17) - 66 = 104 ⇒ x̄ = 13. Means are (x̄ = 13, ȳ = 17). 2) Slopes: Assume Y on X is 8X - 10Y + 66 = 0 ⇒ Y = 0.8 X + 6.6 ⇒ b_yx = 0.8. Assume X on Y is 40X - 18Y - 214 = 0 ⇒ X = 0.45 Y + 5.35 ⇒ b_xy = 0.45. r = √(0.8 * 0.45) = √0.36 = +0.6. 3) Variance of Y: b_yx = r (σ_y / σ_x) ⇒ 0.8 = 0.6 (σ_y / 3) ⇒ σ_y = 4 ⇒ Var(Y) = 16.

Q10. Given crop yield X_1, fertilizer X_2, and rainfall X_3 with means x̄_1 = 60, x̄_2 = 20, x̄_3 = 10, std devs σ_1 = 10, σ_2 = 4, σ_3 = 3, and correlations r_12 = 0.8, r_13 = 0.6, r_23 = 0.5. Calculate partial regression coefficients b_12.3 and b_13.2 and write the regression equation.
Solution:

1) b_12.3 = (10 / 4) * [(0.8 - 0.6 * 0.5) / (1 - 0.5²)] = 2.5 * [(0.8 - 0.3) / 0.75] = 2.5 * (0.5 / 0.75) = 1.67. 2) b_13.2 = (10 / 3) * [(0.6 - 0.8 * 0.5) / (1 - 0.5²)] = 3.33 * [(0.6 - 0.4) / 0.75] = 3.33 * (0.2 / 0.75) = 0.89. 3) Regression equation: X_1 - 60 = 1.67 (X_2 - 20) + 0.89 (X_3 - 10) ⇒ X_1 = 60 + 1.67 X_2 - 33.4 + 0.89 X_3 - 8.9 ⇒ X_1 = 17.7 + 1.67 X_2 + 0.89 X_3.
