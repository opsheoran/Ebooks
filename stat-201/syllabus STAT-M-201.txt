Course Code: STAT-M-201
Course Title: Statistical Methods-II
Credit Hours: 3+1
Course Type: Minor Course (Vocational)
Semester: III

	
Course Objectives:
1.	To introduce the concept of correlation and methods for measuring the linear association between two variables, including rank correlation.
2.	To familiarize students with the principle of least squares and its application in fitting various types of curves to data.
3.	To develop an understanding of linear regression analysis, including fitting regression lines and interpreting regression coefficients.
4.	To introduce basic concepts of multivariate analysis, including multiple regression and partial/multiple correlation for three variables.

Course Learning Outcomes:
 Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:
1.	Calculate and interpret Karl Pearson's and Spearman's rank correlation coefficients.
2.	Apply the principle of least squares to fit linear, parabolic, power, and exponential curves to bivariate data.
3.	Determine the equations of regression lines, interpret regression coefficients, and understand the relationship between correlation and regression.
4.	Fit a regression plane and calculate multiple and partial correlation coefficients for trivariate data.

Detailed Syllabus:
UNIT-I
Correlation: Concept and types of correlation: Positive, negative, and zero correlation. Methods of finding correlation: Scatter diagram, Karl Pearson’s Coefficient of correlation (r), its properties. Coefficient of correlation for a bivariate frequency distribution. Rank correlation: Derivation, merits, demerits, limits of rank correlation coefficient, handling tied or repeated ranks.
UNIT-II
Curve Fitting: Principle of least squares. Fitting of curves: Straight line: Y=a+bX., Second-degree parabola: Y=a+bX+cX^2., Power curves: Y=aXb, Exponential curves: Y=ab^X and Y=ae^(bX).
UNIT-III
Linear Regression: Two lines of regression: Regression of Y on X and X on Y. Regression coefficients: Properties, interpretation, and calculation. Angle between two regression lines. Standard error of estimate obtained from the regression line. Correlation coefficient between observed and estimated values. Distinction between correlation and regression.
UNIT-IV
Multivariate Data: Plane of regression: Regression in three variables. Properties of residuals: Mean, variance, and distribution. Variance of the residual. Multiple and partial correlation for three variables: Coefficient of multiple correlation and its properties. Coefficient of partial correlation and its properties terms of total and partial correlations. Coefficient of determination.

Practical:
1.	Karl Pearson’s Coefficient of Correlation: Compute Karl Pearson’s coefficient of correlation for a given bivariate frequency distribution.
2.	Spearman’s Rank Correlation Coefficient: Find Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient for given data.
3.	Fitting a Straight Line: Fit a straight line for the given data on pairs of observations.
4.	Fitting a Second-Degree Curve: Fit a second-degree curve for the given data.
5.	Fitting a Power Curve: Fit a curve of the type Y = aXᵇ for the given data on pairs of observations.
6.	Regression Lines: Obtain the regression lines for given data.
7.	Partial and Multiple Correlation Coefficients: Compute partial and multiple correlation coefficients for the given tri-variate data.
8.	Plane of Regression: Obtain the plane of regression for the given tri-variate data.
