About This Course

STAT-CC-201: Statistical Methods-II is an advanced course for undergraduate students, exploring how variables interact, how empirical curves fit mathematical data, and how multivariate models predict complex real-world relationships.

Designed specifically for students at CCS Haryana Agricultural University, Hisar, this e-book combines rigorous theoretical proofs with step-by-step practical calculations, interactive SVG charts, and comprehensive self-assessment modules.

Preface

While foundational statistics focuses on summarizing single variables, real-world scientific and agricultural investigations almost always involve complex relationships between multiple variables. This second volume, Statistical Methods-II (STAT-CC-201), builds directly upon the descriptive foundations laid in Stat-CC-101, guiding students into bivariate analysis, mathematical curve fitting, linear regression modeling, and multivariate relationships.

Tailored specifically for undergraduate students at CCS Haryana Agricultural University, Hisar, this web textbook combines theoretical rigor with practical problem-solving. Each method is grounded in empirical research—whether modeling fertilizer dosage against crop yield, determining rank agreement between agricultural judges, or predicting multi-factor biological growth.

Correlation is not causation, but it is the indispensable compass that reveals where secret relationships lie hidden in nature.

— Karl Pearson

Scope and Organisation

The course is structured into four comprehensive units designed to guide students from simple pair-wise associations to multi-variable predictive modeling:

Unit I: Correlation & Rank Correlation — Introduces bivariate data, scatter diagrams, Karl Pearson's coefficient of correlation ($r$) and its mathematical properties, and Spearman's rank correlation coefficient ($r_s$) with handling for tied ranks.

Unit II: Curve Fitting & Principle of Least Squares — Covers the mathematical fitting of empirical curves to bivariate data using the Principle of Least Squares. Students learn to fit straight lines ($Y = a + bX$), second-degree parabolas ($Y = a + bX + cX^2$), power curves ($Y = a X^b$), and exponential curves ($Y = a e^{bX}$).

Unit III: Linear Regression Analysis — Explores linear prediction models, the two lines of regression ($Y$ on $X$ and $X$ on $Y$), regression coefficients ($b_{yx}, b_{xy}$) and their five fundamental properties, the geometric angle between regression lines, and the standard error of estimate.

Unit IV: Multivariate Data Analysis — Extends bivariate models into trivariate and multivariate systems, covering trivariate regression planes, residual variance, the multiple correlation coefficient ($R_{1.23}$), and partial correlation coefficients ($r_{12.3}$).

Interactive Learning: Every chapter features toggleable step-by-step mathematical proofs, interactive SVG scatter plots and curve fitting visualizations, and complete exercise banks to test your comprehension.

Course Syllabus & Structure

The detailed unit-wise syllabus for STAT-CC-201: Statistical Methods-II as prescribed for B.Sc. II Year students at CCS HAU Hisar:

Unit Title Topics Covered
Unit I Correlation & Rank Correlation Bivariate distribution, scatter diagram, Karl Pearson's coefficient of correlation \(r\) and its properties, Spearman's rank correlation coefficient \(r_s\) (untied and tied ranks), limits of correlation.
Unit II Curve Fitting & Least Squares Principle of Least Squares, fitting of straight line \(Y = a + bX\), second-degree parabola \(Y = a + bX + cX^2\), power curve \(Y = a X^b\), and exponential curve \(Y = a e^{bX}\). Normal equations and parameter estimation.
Unit III Linear Regression Analysis Concept of regression, lines of regression (\(Y\) on \(X\) and \(X\) on \(Y\)), regression coefficients \(b_{yx}\) and \(b_{xy}\), 5 fundamental properties of regression coefficients, angle between regression lines, standard error of estimate.
Unit IV Multivariate Data Analysis Multivariate data, trivariate regression planes, residual variance, Multiple Correlation Coefficient \(R_{1.23}\) and its properties, Partial Correlation Coefficient \(r_{12.3}\) and its relationship with simple correlations.

A Note from the Author

Dear Students,

Welcome to Statistical Methods-II (STAT-CC-201). Having mastered univariate descriptive statistics in your previous course, you are now taking a major leap forward into modeling relationships between multiple variables.

In scientific research, no phenomenon exists in isolation. Crop yields depend on rainfall and nitrogen application; soil moisture correlates with ambient temperature; economic trends depend on multiple interconnected indicators. The tools presented in this textbook—correlation, least-squares curve fitting, linear regression, and multivariate models—provide the exact mathematical framework needed to analyze and predict these complex real-world systems.

To model relationships between variables is to decipher the underlying mechanics of natural and agricultural phenomena.

— Prof. O.P. Sheoran

How to Excel in STAT-CC-201

  • Master the Geometry: Pay close attention to scatter plots and regression line diagrams. Visualizing data relationships makes mathematical formulas far easier to remember and interpret.
  • Understand the 5 Properties: Linear regression coefficients ($b_{yx}, b_{xy}$) have critical mathematical properties (such as $r = \pm \sqrt{b_{yx} \cdot b_{xy}}$). Learn the proofs and understand why they hold.
  • Practice Calculation Steps: Multivariate calculations for $R_{1.23}$ and $r_{12.3}$ require structured calculation steps. Work through the data-based examples with pen and paper before checking the toggleable solutions.

It is my sincere hope that this digital textbook inspires you to apply statistical reasoning with confidence in your academic research and future careers.
