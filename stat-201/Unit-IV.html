4.1 Concept of Multivariate Data

In many practical phenomena, an outcome depends on more than one factor simultaneously. For example, crop yield (\(X_1\)) depends on rainfall (\(X_2\)) and fertilizer dose (\(X_3\)). Data collected simultaneously on three or more variables is called Multivariate Data. When three variables are studied, it is called Trivariate Analysis.

We use Yule's Notation, denoting variables as \(X_1, X_2, X_3\). Subscripts before the dot (.) represent primary variables, while subscripts after the dot represent control variables held constant.

4.1 Multivariate Data का परिचय

जब 3 या अधिक variables पर एक साथ data एकत्र किया जाता है, तो इसे Multivariate Data कहा जाता है। Agriculture में crop yield (\(X_1\)) न केवल rainfall (\(X_2\)) बल्कि fertilizer (\(X_3\)) पर भी निर्भर करता है।

4.2 Plane of Regression

For three variables \(X_1, X_2, X_3\), the linear regression of \(X_1\) on \(X_2\) and \(X_3\) is expressed as a 3D plane:

Plane of Regression Equation of X₁ on X₂ and X₃:
\[ (X_1 - ar{X}_1) = b_{12.3} (X_2 - ar{X}_2) + b_{13.2} (X_3 - ar{X}_3) \]

where \(b_{12.3}\) and \(b_{13.2}\) are the partial regression coefficients.

4.3 Properties of Residuals

The residual is defined as: \(e_{1.23} = X_1 - \hat{X}_{1.23}\).

Properties of Residuals

  1. Mean is zero: \(E[e_{1.23}] = 0\).
  2. Uncorrelated with Predictors: \( ext{Cov}(e_{1.23}, X_2) = 0\) and \( ext{Cov}(e_{1.23}, X_3) = 0\).
  3. Uncorrelated with Estimates: \( ext{Cov}(e_{1.23}, \hat{X}_{1.23}) = 0\).

4.3 Residuals के गुण (Properties)

Residual \(e_{1.23}\) का mean हमेशा zero होता है और यह predictor variables \(X_2\) व \(X_3\) से uncorrelated होता है।

4.4 Variance of Residuals

Variance of Residual \(S_{1.23}^2\):
\[ S_{1.23}^2 = \sigma_1^2 (1 - R_{1.23}^2) = \sigma_1^2 rac{R}{R_{11}} \]

where \(R\) is the determinant of the correlation matrix and \(R_{11}\) is the minor of \(r_{11}\).

4.4 Residuals का Variance

Residual variance \(S_{1.23}^2 = \sigma_1^2 (1 - R_{1.23}^2)\) unexplained variation को मापता है।

4.5 Multiple Correlation Coefficient (\(R_{1.23}\))

The Coefficient of Multiple Correlation \(R_{1.23}\) measures the simple correlation between \(X_1\) and its joint linear estimate \(\hat{X}_{1.23}\) based on \(X_2\) and \(X_3\).

Formula for Multiple Correlation:
\[ R_{1.23} = \sqrt{ rac{r_{12}^2 + r_{13}^2 - 2 r_{12} r_{13} r_{23}}{1 - r_{23}^2} } \]

Properties of Multiple Correlation

  • Bounded between 0 and 1: \(0 \le R_{1.23} \le 1\). (Cannot be negative!)
  • Always greater than or equal to any individual total correlation: \(R_{1.23} \ge |r_{12}|\) and \(R_{1.23} \ge |r_{13}|\).
  • \(R_{1.23} = 1\) iff all residuals are zero (perfect linear fit).

4.5 Multiple Correlation Coefficient (\(R_{1.23}\))

Multiple Correlation \(R_{1.23}\) हमेशा 0 और 1 के बीच होता है (\(0 \le R_{1.23} \le 1\)) और कभी negative नहीं होता।

4.6 Partial Correlation Coefficient (\(r_{12.3}\))

The Coefficient of Partial Correlation \(r_{12.3}\) measures the net linear correlation between \(X_1\) and \(X_2\) after eliminating the linear influence of \(X_3\).

Formula for Partial Correlation:
\[ r_{12.3} = rac{r_{12} - r_{13} r_{23}}{\sqrt{(1 - r_{13}^2)(1 - r_{23}^2)}} \]
Figure 4.2: Venn Diagram Representation of Partial and Multiple Correlation
Partial correlation \(r_{12.3}\) measures the overlap between \(X_1\) and \(X_2\) excluding \(X_3\).

4.6 Partial Correlation Coefficient (\(r_{12.3}\))

Partial Correlation \(r_{12.3}\), \(X_3\) के असर को हटाने के बाद \(X_1\) और \(X_2\) के बीच शुद्ध (net) correlation मापता है।

4.7 Coefficient of Determination (\(R_{1.23}^2\))

The Coefficient of Determination \(R_{1.23}^2\) represents the proportion of total variation in \(X_1\) that is explained by the regression plane on \(X_2\) and \(X_3\):

\[ R_{1.23}^2 = rac{ ext{Explained Variance}}{ ext{Total Variance}} \]

4.7 Coefficient of Determination

\(R_{1.23}^2\) यह मापता है कि \(X_1\) के कुल variance का कितना प्रतिशत \(X_2\) और \(X_3\) द्वारा समझा जा सकता है।

4.8 Solved Numerical Examples

Example 4.1: Calculation of Partial and Multiple Correlations

Given \(r_{12} = 0.6\), \(r_{13} = 0.7\), \(r_{23} = 0.5\). Calculate \(r_{12.3}\) and \(R_{1.23}\).

Solution:

\[ r_{12.3} = rac{0.6 - (0.7 imes 0.5)}{\sqrt{(1 - 0.49)(1 - 0.25)}} = rac{0.25}{\sqrt{0.51 imes 0.75}} = rac{0.25}{0.6185} = \mathbf{0.404} \] \[ R_{1.23} = \sqrt{ rac{0.36 + 0.49 - 2(0.6)(0.7)(0.5)}{1 - 0.25}} = \sqrt{ rac{0.43}{0.75}} = \sqrt{0.5733} = \mathbf{0.757} \]

Q2. Define Residual e_1.23 in trivariate linear regression. Prove that E[e_1.23] = 0.
Solution:

Residual e_1.23 is the difference between observed X_1 and estimated X̂_1.23: e_1.23 = X_1 - x̄_1 - b_12.3 (X_2 - x̄_2) - b_13.2 (X_3 - x̄_3). Taking expectation: E[e_1.23] = E[X_1 - x̄_1] - b_12.3 E[X_2 - x̄_2] - b_13.2 E[X_3 - x̄_3] = 0 - b_12.3 (0) - b_13.2 (0) = 0.

Q3. Prove that the residual e_1.23 is uncorrelated with predictor variables X_2 and X_3.
Solution:

Normal equations obtained by minimizing Σ e_1.23² with respect to b_12.3 and b_13.2 give: 1) Σ e_1.23 (X_2 - x̄_2) = 0 ⇒ Cov(e_1.23, X_2) = 0. 2) Σ e_1.23 (X_3 - x̄_3) = 0 ⇒ Cov(e_1.23, X_3) = 0. Hence, residual is orthogonal (uncorrelated) to predictors X_2 and X_3.

Q4. Derive the formula for the variance of residual S_1.23² = σ_1² (1 - R_1.23²).
Solution:

Variance S_1.23² = (1/n) Σ e_1.23² = (1/n) Σ e_1.23 [ (X_1 - x̄_1) - b_12.3 (X_2 - x̄_2) - b_13.2 (X_3 - x̄_3) ]. Since Cov(e_1.23, X_2) = 0 and Cov(e_1.23, X_3) = 0, S_1.23² = (1/n) Σ e_1.23 (X_1 - x̄_1) = Cov(e_1.23, X_1). Expressing in terms of determinants gives S_1.23² = σ_1² (R / R_11) = σ_1² (1 - R_1.23²).

Q5. Define Coefficient of Multiple Correlation R_1.23. State its properties and formula in terms of total correlations.
Solution:

R_1.23 is the simple correlation between actual observed X_1 and its predicted value X̂_1.23. Formula: R_1.23 = √[ (r_12² + r_13² - 2 r_12 r_13 r_23) / (1 - r_23²) ]. Properties: 1) 0 ≤ R_1.23 ≤ 1 (non-negative). 2) R_1.23 ≥ |r_12| and R_1.23 ≥ |r_13|. 3) R_1.23 = 1 iff all residuals are zero (perfect fit). 4) R_1.23 = 0 iff r_12 = 0 and r_13 = 0.

Q6. Define Coefficient of Partial Correlation r_12.3. Derive its formula in terms of total correlations.
Solution:

r_12.3 is the measure of linear relationship between X_1 and X_2 after eliminating the linear effect of X_3. Formula: r_12.3 = (r_12 - r_13 r_23) / √[(1 - r_13²)(1 - r_23²)]. Derivation: Let e_1.3 = X_1 - x̄_1 - b_13(X_3 - x̄_3) and e_2.3 = X_2 - x̄_2 - b_23(X_3 - x̄_3). The simple correlation between residuals e_1.3 and e_2.3 gives r_12.3 = Cov(e_1.3, e_2.3) / [S_1.3 S_2.3], which simplifies to the formula.

Q7. State and prove the properties of Partial Correlation Coefficient r_12.3.
Solution:

1) -1 ≤ r_12.3 ≤ +1 (bounded). 2) Symmetric in primary subscripts: r_12.3 = r_21.3. 3) If r_13 = 0 and r_23 = 0, then r_12.3 = r_12. 4) If r_12 = 1, then r_12.3 = 1. 5) Independent of origin and scale of all three variables.

Q8. Explain the Coefficient of Determination R_1.23² and its interpretation in variance decomposition.
Solution:

R_1.23² is the square of multiple correlation coefficient. It represents the proportion of total variance of X_1 explained by the regression plane on X_2 and X_3. Variance decomposition: Total Variance σ_1² = Explained Variance (σ_1² R_1.23²) + Residual Variance S_1.23² [σ_1² (1 - R_1.23²)]. Thus R_1.23² = (Explained Variance) / (Total Variance).

Q9. Given r_12 = 0.6, r_13 = 0.7, r_23 = 0.5. Compute the partial correlation r_12.3 and multiple correlation R_1.23.
Solution:

Partial r_12.3 = (0.6 - 0.7*0.5) / √[(1 - 0.49)(1 - 0.25)] = (0.6 - 0.35) / √[0.51 * 0.75] = 0.25 / √0.3825 = 0.25 / 0.6185 = 0.404. Multiple R_1.23 = √[ (0.36 + 0.49 - 2*0.6*0.7*0.5) / (1 - 0.25) ] = √[ (0.85 - 0.42) / 0.75 ] = √[ 0.43 / 0.75 ] = √0.5733 = 0.757.

Q10. In an agricultural experiment on crop yield (X_1), rainfall (X_2), and fertilizer (X_3), given σ_1 = 10, σ_2 = 8, σ_3 = 5, r_12 = 0.8, r_13 = 0.6, r_23 = 0.5. Calculate partial regression coefficients b_12.3, b_13.2 and residual variance S_1.23².

S_2.3 = 8 √(1-0.25) = 8 * 0.866 = 6.928. b_12.3 = r_12.3 (S_1.3 / S_2.3) = 0.7217 * (8 / 6.928) = 0.833. R_1.23² = (0.64 + 0.36 - 2*0.8*0.6*0.5)/(1 - 0.25) = (1.0 - 0.48)/0.75 = 0.52 / 0.75 = 0.6933. Q11. Explain Yule's notation for trivariate regression analysis.
Solution:

Yule's notation uses variables X_1, X_2, X_3. Primary subscripts (before dot) indicate the dependent and primary predictor variables. Secondary subscripts (after dot) indicate control variables held constant. E.g., b_12.3 is the regression coefficient of X_1 on X_2 keeping X_3 constant.

Q12. Write down the plane of regression equation of X_2 on X_1 and X_3.

The plane of regression of X_2 on X_1 and X_3 is: (X_2 - x̄_2) = b_21.3 (X_1 - x̄_1) + b_23.1 (X_3 - x̄_3), where b_21.3 and b_23.1 are partial regression coefficients.

Q13. Define partial regression coefficient b_12.3. How is it calculated from total correlation matrix?
Solution:

b_12.3 represents the rate of change of X_1 per unit change in X_2 while holding X_3 constant. In terms of correlation matrix determinants: b_12.3 = - (σ_1 / σ_2) * (R_12 / R_11), where R_ij is the cofactor of r_ij in the determinant R of correlation matrix.

Q14. Show that the multiple correlation coefficient R_1.23 is non-negative.
Solution:

R_1.23 is defined as the simple correlation coefficient between observed X_1 and its linear estimate X̂_1.23. By convention, multiple correlation measures total linear agreement and is non-negative: 0 ≤ R_1.23 ≤ 1.

Q15. Explain the relationship between multiple correlation R_1.23 and total correlations r_12, r_13.
Solution:

R_1.23² = (r_12² + r_13² - 2 r_12 r_13 r_23) / (1 - r_23²). Since residual variance S_1.23² ≤ S_1.2² ≤ σ_1², R_1.23 ≥ |r_12| and R_1.23 ≥ |r_13|.

Q16. Prove that if r_12 = 1, then R_1.23 = 1.
Solution:

Substituting r_12 = 1 into R_1.23² = (1 + r_13² - 2 r_13 r_23) / (1 - r_23²). Since r_12 = 1 implies r_13 = r_23, numerator = 1 + r_23² - 2 r_23² = 1 - r_23², so R_1.23² = (1 - r_23²)/(1 - r_23²) = 1 ⇒ R_1.23 = 1.

Q17. Distinguish between Total, Partial, and Multiple Correlation.
Solution:

Total correlation r_12 measures linear association between two variables ignoring all others. Partial correlation r_12.3 measures net association between two variables after removing the linear effect of a third variable. Multiple correlation R_1.23 measures joint association of one variable with a set of two or more predictor variables.

Q18. Explain the concept of orthogonality of residuals in multivariate regression.
Solution:

Residual e_1.23 = X_1 - X̂_1.23 is orthogonal to the predictor subspace spanned by X_2 and X_3, meaning Cov(e_1.23, X_2) = 0 and Cov(e_1.23, X_3) = 0. This ensures minimum residual variance.

Q19. State the condition under which partial correlation r_12.3 is equal to total correlation r_12.
Solution:

r_12.3 = (r_12 - r_13 r_23) / √[(1 - r_13²)(1 - r_23³)]. r_12.3 = r_12 if and only if r_13 = 0 and r_23 = 0, or if r_13 r_23 = r_12 (1 - √[(1-r_13²)(1-r_23²)]).

Q20. Given r_12 = 0.5, r_13 = 0.5, r_23 = 0.5. Compute r_12.3 and R_1.23.
Solution:

r_12.3 = (0.5 - 0.25) / (1 - 0.25) = 0.25 / 0.75 = 1/3 = 0.333. R_1.23² = (0.25 + 0.25 - 2*0.5*0.5*0.5) / (1 - 0.25) = (0.50 - 0.25) / 0.75 = 0.25 / 0.75 = 1/3 ⇒ R_1.23 = 1/√3 = 0.577.

S_1.23² = σ_1² (1 - R_1.23²). Since 0 ≤ R_1.23² ≤ 1, (1 - R_1.23²) ≤ 1. Therefore S_1.23² ≤ σ_1².

Q29. Explain why partial correlation is necessary when confounding variables exist.
Solution:

Confounding variables can create spurious correlation or hide genuine relationships between two variables. Partial correlation holds confounding variables constant to reveal true net relationship.
