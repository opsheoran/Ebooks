Primary & secondary data, survey questionnaire construction, SPSS/R computing workflows & NSS sampling

3.1 Introduction to Research Design

Suppose your teacher asks you to find out how many students in your college use public transport. You cannot simply guess. You need a plan. You must decide whom to ask, how many students to include, what questions to ask, and how to record the answers. This plan is called a research design. A good research design is like a road map. It tells you where to start, what to do, and how to reach your goal. Without a design, your study may become messy.
What is a Research Design?
A research design is the blueprint or conceptual structure of a study. It details how the investigation will be executed, answering: What do we want to study? Who will be studied? How will data be collected? How will data be analysed?

Why Do We Need a Research Design?

  • Focus: Keeps the investigation structured and aligned with core research objectives.
  • Resource Efficiency: Saves time, labor, and budget by avoiding unnecessary data collection.
  • Data Validity: Ensures the measurement tools yield reliable and accurate figures.
  • Replicability: Enables other scientists to audit and reproduce the exact methodology.

3.2 Primary and Secondary Data

Primary Data: Data collected first-hand by the researcher specifically for the current project (e.g. personal interviews, questionnaires, field experiments).

Secondary Data: Existing data previously gathered by other agencies for external purposes (e.g. Census of India, NSS reports, hospital archives).

Feature Primary Data Secondary Data
Collection Directly collected for the first time Extracted from existing publications
Cost & Time High expenditure, time-intensive Inexpensive, immediately accessible
Reliability Known quality, tailored to objectives Requires verification of source accuracy

3.3 Survey Questionnaire Construction

What is a Questionnaire?
A questionnaire is a structured instrument consisting of a series of written questions administered to respondents to collect standardized primary data.
Best Practices for Questionnaire Design
  • Keep the questionnaire concise and objective-driven.
  • Use simple, unambiguous wording; avoid double-barreled questions (asking two things at once).
  • Avoid leading questions that bias the respondent's answer.
  • Pre-test (pilot test) the instrument on a small group before full deployment.

3.4 Data Organisation

Editing: Inspecting raw response forms to correct obvious errors and handle missing entries.

Coding: Assigning numerical codes to categorical responses (e.g. Male=1, Female=2).

Tabulation: Systematically displaying data into structured rows and columns (frequency tables).

3.6 Overview of National Sample Survey (NSS)

NSS Stratified Multi-Stage Sampling

The National Statistical Office (NSO) conducts nationwide sample surveys using stratified multi-stage sampling:

Stage 1 (FSUs): Census villages in rural areas or Urban Frame Survey blocks in urban areas.

Stage 2 (SSUs): Households selected randomly from within the sampled villages/blocks.
