Unit III: Research Design and Data Generation

Study planning, primary vs secondary data collection, questionnaire design, data organization, SPSS & R analysis workflows & NSS methodology

3.1 Introduction to Research Design

Suppose your teacher asks you to find out how many students in your college use public transport. You cannot simply guess. You need a plan. You must decide whom to ask, how many students to include, what questions to ask, and how to record the answers. This plan is called a research design. A good research design is like a road map. It tells you where to start, what to do, and how to reach your goal. Without a design, your study may become messy. You may collect wrong data, ask the wrong people, or forget important details. In this unit, we learn how to plan a study, collect data, and prepare it for analysis.
What is Research Design?
A research design is the conceptual blueprint or operational plan within which research is conducted. It provides a structured framework for data collection, measurement, and analysis to answer specific research questions efficiently.

Why Do We Need a Research Design?

  • Structure & Direction: Keeps the investigation focused and prevents scope creep.
  • Resource Optimization: Saves time, money, and manpower by eliminating redundant data collection.
  • Internal & External Validity: Minimizes systematic bias and ensures findings can be generalized.
  • Reproducibility: Allows independent researchers to replicate the methodology.

8-Step Research Planning Workflow

  1. Choose a research topic and identify the knowledge gap.
  2. State clear, measurable objectives and testable hypotheses.
  3. Define the target population and eligibility criteria.
  4. Select an appropriate sampling technique and sample size.
  5. Construct and pre-test data collection instruments.
  6. Organize data through editing, coding, and tabulation.
  7. Perform statistical data analysis (SPSS / R / Python).
  8. Synthesize conclusions and publish research reports.
Example: Crop Yield Experiment in Haryana
A researcher at CCS HAU wants to compare 4 wheat varieties ($V_1, V_2, V_3, V_4$) across 3 fertilizer levels ($F_1, F_2, F_3$). Without a research design, soil heterogeneity would distort yield measurements. By using a Randomized Block Design (RBD), the researcher blocks by field slope and isolates true variety-fertilizer interaction effects.

3.1 रिसर्च डिजाइन का परिचय (Introduction to Research Design)

मान लीजिए आपके शिक्षक आपसे पूछते हैं कि आपके कॉलेज में कितने छात्र सार्वजनिक परिवहन का उपयोग करते हैं। आप केवल अनुमान नहीं लगा सकते। आपको एक योजना की आवश्यकता है। आपको यह तय करना होगा कि किससे पूछना है, कितने छात्रों को शामिल करना है, क्या प्रश्न पूछने हैं और उत्तरों को कैसे रिकॉर्ड करना है। इस योजना को रिसर्च डिजाइन (research design) कहा जाता है। एक अच्छा research design एक रोड मैप की तरह है। यह आपको बताता है कि कहाँ से शुरू करना है, क्या करना है और अपने लक्ष्य तक कैसे पहुँचना है। डिजाइन के बिना आपका अध्ययन अव्यवस्थित हो सकता है।
Research Design की परिभाषा (Definition)
एक research design अध्ययन की अवधारणात्मक रूपरेखा (conceptual blueprint) या परिचालन योजना है जिसके तहत शोध किया जाता है। यह डेटा संग्रह (data collection), मापन (measurement) और विश्लेषण (analysis) के लिए एक संरचित ढांचा प्रदान करता है।

रिसर्च डिजाइन की आवश्यकता क्यों है?

  • संरचना और दिशा (Structure & Direction): अध्ययन को केंद्रित और व्यवस्थित रखता है।
  • संसाधन अनुकूलन (Resource Optimization): अनावश्यक डेटा संग्रह को समाप्त करके समय और धन की बचत करता है।
  • आंतरिक और बाह्य वैधता (Validity): पक्षपात (bias) को कम करता है और परिणामों को सामान्य बनाने में मदद करता है।
  • पुनरुत्पादकता (Reproducibility): अन्य शोधकर्ताओं को अध्ययन को दोहराने की अनुमति देता है।

शोध योजना के 8 बुनियादी चरण (8-Step Research Workflow)

  1. शोध विषय चुनें और उद्देश्य स्पष्ट करें।
  2. स्पष्ट और मापने योग्य उद्देश्यों (objectives) को बताएं।
  3. लक्ष्य आबादी (target population) को परिभाषित करें।
  4. उपयुक्त नमूना विधि (sampling technique) और आकार चुनें।
  5. डेटा संग्रह उपकरण (questionnaire / schedule) तैयार करें।
  6. डेटा को संपादित (editing), कोडित (coding) और सारणीबद्ध (tabulation) करें।
  7. सांख्यिकीय विश्लेषण (SPSS / R) करें।
  8. निष्कर्ष प्रस्तुत करें और रिपोर्ट लिखें।

3.2 Primary and Secondary Data

When you collect information for the first time, it is called primary data. When you use information that someone else has already collected, it is called secondary data. Both types are useful. A student who counts the number of bicycles in the college parking lot is collecting primary data. A student who reads last year's attendance records is using secondary data. Knowing the difference helps you choose the right source for your study.

3.2 प्राथमिक और माध्यमिक डेटा (Primary and Secondary Data)

जब आप पहली बार किसी विशिष्ट उद्देश्य के लिए जानकारी एकत्र करते हैं, तो इसे प्राथमिक डेटा (primary data) कहा जाता है। जब आप उस जानकारी का उपयोग करते हैं जिसे किसी अन्य व्यक्ति ने पहले ही एकत्र किया है, तो इसे माध्यमिक डेटा (secondary data) कहा जाता है। दोनों प्रकार उपयोगी हैं।

प्राथमिक डेटा (Primary Data)

Primary data शोधकर्ता द्वारा पहली बार सीधे एकत्र किया गया डेटा है।

  • प्रत्यक्ष अवलोकन (Direct Observation): घटनाओं को घटित होते हुए देखना और रिकॉर्ड करना।
  • व्यक्तिगत साक्षात्कार (Personal Interview): आमने-सामने प्रश्न पूछना।
  • प्रश्नावली (Questionnaire): उत्तरदाताओं को दिए जाने वाले प्रश्नों का सेट।
  • अनुसूची (Schedule): प्रशिक्षित प्रगणक (enumerator) द्वारा भरे जाने वाले उत्तर।

माध्यमिक डेटा (Secondary Data)

Secondary data वह डेटा है जो पहले से प्रकाशित या अप्रकाशित रूप में उपलब्ध है (जैसे भारत की जनगणना, NSS रिपोर्ट, अस्पताल के रिकॉर्ड)।

3.3 Survey Questionnaire Construction

A questionnaire is one of the most common tools used to collect primary data. It is a list of questions given to people to answer. A good questionnaire is short, clear, and easy to answer. A bad questionnaire confuses people and gives wrong answers. In this section, we learn how to make a good questionnaire step by step.
Figure 3.2: 7-Step Questionnaire Construction Protocol
Step-by-step questionnaire engineering workflow from objective definition to pilot pre-testing.

What is a Questionnaire?

A questionnaire is a structured instrument consisting of a series of written items delivered to respondents to gather self-reported data. It is widely used in field surveys, health studies, and social research.

Detailed Steps in Constructing a Questionnaire

  1. Step 1: Define the Research Objective: Clearly specify what parameters need to be measured.
  2. Step 2: Identify the Target Audience: Consider literacy level, language, and cultural context.
  3. Step 3: Determine Question Formats: Select between open-ended, closed-ended, or rating scales.
  4. Step 4: Draft Clear Questions: Use simple, unambiguous language. Avoid technical jargon.
  5. Step 5: Sequence Logically: Begin with non-sensitive warmup questions, group related topics, and place sensitive questions at the end.
  6. Step 6: Conduct Pilot Pre-Testing: Test the draft on a small sample (15–30 respondents) to detect ambiguities and measure completion time.
  7. Step 7: Revise and Finalize: Incorporate feedback from pre-testing to produce the final survey instrument.

Question Types & Examples

1. Open-Ended Questions
Allow respondents to provide unrestricted answers in their own words.
Example: "What factors influenced your decision to join this college?"
2. Closed-Ended (Dichotomous) Questions
Offer exactly two mutually exclusive choices.
Example: "Do you own a smartphone? (a) Yes    (b) No"
3. Multiple-Choice Questions (MCQs)
Provide a list of predetermined response options.
Example: "Which mode of transport do you use most often? (a) Bus   (b) Bicycle   (c) Walking   (d) Two-wheeler"
4. Rating Scale (Likert Scale) Questions
Measure attitudes or intensity of agreement on an ordinal scale.
Example: "Rate your satisfaction with library facilities: 1 = Very Dissatisfied, 2 = Dissatisfied, 3 = Neutral, 4 = Satisfied, 5 = Very Satisfied."

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Avoid Leading Questions: Questions that suggest a preferred answer (e.g. "Don't you agree that our library is excellent?").
  • Avoid Double-Barreled Questions: Combining two separate issues into one question (e.g. "Are you satisfied with canteen food and Wi-Fi speed?").
  • Avoid Ambiguous Terms: Using vague words like "often" or "frequently" without operational definitions.

3.3 सर्वेक्षण प्रश्नावली निर्माण (Survey Questionnaire Construction)

प्रश्नावली (questionnaire) प्राथमिक डेटा (primary data) एकत्र करने के लिए सबसे आम उपकरणों में से एक है। यह लोगों को उत्तर देने के लिए दिए गए प्रश्नों की एक सूची है। एक अच्छी प्रश्नावली छोटी, स्पष्ट और उत्तर देने में आसान होती है।

Questionnaire क्या है?

एक questionnaire उत्तरदाताओं से स्व-रिपोर्ट किए गए डेटा को इकट्ठा करने के लिए उपयोग किया जाने वाला एक संरचित लिखित साधन है।

प्रश्नावली बनाने के 7 चरण (7 Steps)

  1. उद्देश्य तय करें (Define Purpose): क्या जानकारी चाहिए, यह स्पष्ट करें।
  2. लक्ष्य समूह चुनें (Target Group): उत्तरदाताओं की भाषा और पृष्ठभूमि का ध्यान रखें।
  3. प्रश्नों के प्रकार चुनें (Question Types): Open-ended या Closed-ended विकल्प तय करें।
  4. सरल प्रश्न लिखें (Draft Questions): सरल और स्पष्ट भाषा का प्रयोग करें।
  5. तार्किक क्रम में व्यवस्थित करें (Sequence): आसान प्रश्नों से शुरू करके संवेदनशील प्रश्नों को अंत में रखें।
  6. पायलट परीक्षण (Pre-Testing / Pilot Test): एक छोटे समूह (15-30 लोग) पर प्रश्नावली का परीक्षण करें।
  7. अंतिम रूप दें (Finalize): फीडबैक के आधार पर सुधार करें।

प्रश्नों के मुख्य प्रकार (Types of Questions)

  • Open-ended Questions: उत्तरदाता अपने शब्दों में स्वतंत्र उत्तर देते हैं।
  • Dichotomous Questions: केवल दो विकल्प होते हैं (जैसे हाँ / नहीं)।
  • Multiple-Choice Questions (MCQs): कई दिए गए विकल्पों में से चुनाव।
  • Likert Rating Scale: 1 से 5 पैमानों पर संतुष्टि या सहमति को मापना।

सावधानियां (Mistakes to Avoid)

  • Leading Questions से बचें: ऐसे प्रश्न न पूछें जो किसी खास उत्तर का सुझाव देते हों।
  • Double-barreled Questions से बचें: एक ही वाक्य में दो अलग बातें न पूछें।

3.4 Data Organisation

After collecting data, the next step is to organise it. Raw data is like a heap of stones. It has value, but it is hard to use. Organising data means cleaning it, classifying it, and putting it in tables. This makes the data easy to understand and analyse. In this section, we learn the basic steps of data organisation.

1. Editing Data

Editing is the systematic inspection of completed survey questionnaires or measurement forms to detect and correct errors, omissions, illegible entries, and inconsistencies.

  • Field Editing: Conducted by the interviewer immediately after the interview to expand abbreviated notes and clarify illegible writing.
  • Central Editing: Conducted at the central research office before data entry to resolve out-of-range values and logically inconsistent responses (e.g. respondent age entered as 150 years).

2. Coding Data

Coding is the process of converting qualitative or categorical responses into numerical codes to enable computer entry and statistical software processing.

Variable Name Response Categories Assigned Numeric Code
Gender Male / Female / Other 1 = Male, 2 = Female, 3 = Other
Education Level Illiterate / Primary / Secondary / Graduate 0 = Illiterate, 1 = Primary, 2 = Secondary, 3 = Graduate
Agreement Status Disagree / Neutral / Agree 1 = Disagree, 2 = Neutral, 3 = Agree

3. Classification of Data

Classification is the grouping of raw data into homogeneous classes based on shared characteristics.

  • Qualitative Classification: Grouping by descriptive attributes (e.g. Marital Status, Blood Group).
  • Quantitative Classification: Grouping by numerical measurements using class intervals (e.g. Income groups: ₹0–10,000, ₹10,001–20,000).
  • Chronological Classification: Grouping observations over time periods (years, months).
  • Spatial / Geographical Classification: Grouping by location (districts, states, regions).

4. Tabulation of Data

Tabulation is the orderly presentation of classified data in rows and columns. A standard statistical table contains:

  • Table Number & Title: Clear description of content.
  • Stub: Left-most column headers describing row categories.
  • Caption: Top column headers describing column categories.
  • Body: The inner cell matrix containing numerical data.
  • Footnotes & Source Notes: Explanations of abbreviations and data source attribution.

5. Frequency Distributions

A frequency distribution summarizes raw continuous data by showing the number of observations ($f$) falling into non-overlapping class intervals.

3.4 डेटा संगठन (Data Organisation)

डेटा एकत्र करने के बाद, अगला कदम इसे व्यवस्थित करना है। कच्चा डेटा (raw data) पत्थरों के ढेर की तरह है। डेटा को व्यवस्थित करने का अर्थ है इसे साफ करना, वर्गीकृत करना और तालिकाओं में रखना।

1. सम्पादन (Editing)

Editing का अर्थ है एकत्र किए गए डेटा की त्रुटियों और विसंगतियों को दूर करने के लिए जांच करना।

2. कोडिंग (Coding)

Coding उत्तरों को सांख्यिकीय सॉफ्टवेयर में प्रविष्टि के लिए संख्याओं में बदलने की प्रक्रिया है (जैसे Male = 1, Female = 2)।

3. वर्गीकरण (Classification)

Classification डेटा को समानताओं के आधार पर समूहों में व्यवस्थित करना है (जैसे आयु समूह: 0-20, 20-40)।

4. सारणीयन (Tabulation)

Tabulation डेटा को पंक्तियों (rows) और स्तंभों (columns) के रूप में व्यवस्थित रूप से प्रस्तुत करना है।

5. आवृत्ति वितरण (Frequency Distribution)

एक frequency distribution तालिका यह दर्शाती है कि प्रत्येक श्रेणी में कितनी आवृत्तियाँ (frequencies) आती हैं।

3.5 Data Analysis Workflows in SPSS and R

Today, most statistical work is done on computers. Two popular software packages are SPSS and R. SPSS is menu-based. You click buttons to do analysis. R is free software where you type commands. Both are useful for entering data, cleaning it, analysing it, and making graphs. In this section, we learn the basic steps of data analysis using these tools.

Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS)

SPSS is a widely used menu-driven software interface featuring two primary windows:

  • Variable View: Metadata specification defining Variable Name, Type, Width, Decimals, Label, Values (Codes), Missing values, and Measurement Scale (Nominal, Ordinal, Scale).

3.6 Overview of National Sample Survey (NSS)

India is a large country. It is not possible to survey every household every time we need data. The National Sample Survey solves this problem. It selects a small but representative group of households from across the country. Then it collects information from them. This information is used by the government to plan schools, hospitals, jobs, and welfare schemes.
Figure 3.4: NSS Stratified Multi-Stage Sampling Architecture
Sampling hierarchy of the National Sample Survey across rural villages and urban blocks.

What is the National Sample Survey?

The National Sample Survey (NSS) was established in 1950 by Professor P.C. Mahalanobis to conduct nationwide large-scale socio-economic sample surveys. It is administered by the National Statistical Office (NSO) under the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI), Government of India.

NSS Stratified Multi-Stage Sampling Design

  1. Stratification: The nation is divided into geographical strata based on districts. Rural and urban areas are stratified independently.
  2. First-Stage Units (FSUs): Census villages in rural sectors and Urban Frame Survey (UFS) blocks in urban sectors are selected using Probability Proportional to Size With Replacement (PPSWR) or Systematic Sampling.
  3. Second-Stage Units (SSUs): Households are listed within selected FSUs and sampled using Stratified Systematic Sampling to ensure representation across income and social strata.

Key NSS Survey Rounds & Domains

  • Household Consumer Expenditure Surveys (CES): Estimates poverty lines, monthly per capita expenditure (MPCE), and inflation weights.
  • Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS): Measures employment rates, labor force participation rate (LFPR), and worker-population ratio (WPR).
  • Health and Morbidity Surveys: Measures healthcare utilization, hospitalization expenses, and maternal-child health parameters.

3.6 राष्ट्रीय नमूना सर्वेक्षण (National Sample Survey - NSS)

भारत एक विशाल देश है। प्रत्येक परिवार का सर्वेक्षण करना संभव नहीं है। राष्ट्रीय नमूना सर्वेक्षण (NSS) इस समस्या का समाधान एक प्रतिनिधि नमूने के माध्यम से करता है।

NSS क्या है?

National Sample Survey (NSS) की स्थापना 1950 में प्रोफेसर पी.सी. महालनोबिस द्वारा की गई थी। अब इसे सांख्यिकी और कार्यक्रम कार्यान्वयन मंत्रालय (MoSPI) के तहत राष्ट्रीय सांख्यिकी कार्यालय (NSO) द्वारा संचालित किया जाता है।

NSS की नमूना पद्धति (Stratified Multi-Stage Sampling)

  1. Stratification (स्तरीकरण): देश को जिलों के आधार पर ग्रामीण और शहरी क्षेत्रों में विभाजित किया जाता है।
  2. First-Stage Units (प्रथम चरण की इकाइयां): ग्रामीण क्षेत्रों से गांव और शहरी क्षेत्रों से अर्बन ब्लॉक्स चुने जाते हैं।
  3. Second-Stage Units (द्वितीय चरण की इकाइयां): चुने गए गांवों और ब्लॉक्स से परिवारों (households) का चयन किया जाता है।

3.7 Solved Multiline Numerical Examples

Example 3.7.1: Neyman Optimum Allocation in Stratified Sampling

Problem: A population of $N = 10,000$ households is divided into 3 strata: Stratum 1 ($N_1 = 5,000, S_1 = 4$), Stratum 2 ($N_2 = 3,000, S_2 = 9$), Stratum 3 ($N_3 = 2,000, S_3 = 16$). Calculate the sample size per stratum ($n_1, n_2, n_3$) for a total sample of $n = 500$ using Neyman Optimum Allocation.

Step-by-Step Solution:

Step 1: Formula for Neyman Allocation:

$$ n_h = n \times \frac{N_h S_h}{\sum_{i=1}^k N_i S_i} $$

Step 2: Calculate products $N_h S_h$ for each stratum:

$$ N_1 S_1 = 5,000 \times 4 = 20,000 $$ $$ N_2 S_2 = 3,000 \times 9 = 27,000 $$ $$ N_3 S_3 = 2,000 \times 16 = 32,000 $$ $$ \sum_{i=1}^3 N_i S_i = 20,000 + 27,000 + 32,000 = 79,000 $$

Step 3: Calculate sample size allocations:

$$ n_1 = 500 \times \frac{20,000}{79,000} = 500 \times 0.25316 = 126.58 \approx 127 \text{ households} $$ $$ n_2 = 500 \times \frac{27,000}{79,000} = 500 \times 0.34177 = 170.88 \approx 171 \text{ households} $$ $$ n_3 = 500 \times \frac{32,000}{79,000} = 500 \times 0.40506 = 202.53 \approx 202 \text{ households} $$ $$ \text{Verification: } 127 + 171 + 202 = 500 \text{ total households.} $$

Example 3.7.3: Chi-Square Test of Independence for Survey Contingency Data

Problem: A survey evaluates whether public transport preference (Bus vs Train) is independent of gender. Observed data: Males (Bus = 60, Train = 40; Total = 100), Females (Bus = 40, Train = 60; Total = 100). Test independence at $\alpha = 0.05$ ($1$ degree of freedom critical value $\chi^2_{0.05, 1} = 3.841$).

Step-by-Step Solution:

$$ E_{11} = \frac{100 \times 100}{200} = 50, \quad E_{12} = 50, \quad E_{21} = 50, \quad E_{22} = 50 $$ $$ \chi^2 = \sum \frac{(O - E)^2}{E} = \frac{(60-50)^2}{50} + \frac{(40-50)^2}{50} + \frac{(40-50)^2}{50} + \frac{(60-50)^2}{50} $$ $$ \chi^2 = \frac{100}{50} + \frac{100}{50} + \frac{100}{50} + \frac{100}{50} = 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 8.00 $$

Since $\chi^2_{\text{cal}} = 8.00 > 3.841$, reject the null hypothesis. Transport preference is significantly associated with gender.

Example 3.7.4: Questionnaire Internal Consistency Reliability (Cronbach's Alpha)

Problem: A 5-item Likert survey ($k = 5$) is tested. The sum of item variances is $\sum \sigma^2_i = 4.8$ and the variance of total test scores is $\sigma^2_T = 16.0$. Calculate Cronbach's Alpha coefficient ($lpha$).

Step-by-Step Solution:

$$ \alpha = \frac{k}{k-1} \left( 1 - \frac{\sum \sigma^2_i}{\sigma^2_T} \right) $$ $$ \alpha = \frac{5}{5-1} \left( 1 - \frac{4.8}{16.0} \right) = \frac{5}{4} \times (1 - 0.30) = 1.25 \times 0.70 = 0.875 $$

Interpretation: $\alpha = 0.875 > 0.70$ indicates excellent questionnaire internal consistency reliability.

Example 3.7.5: Proportional Allocation in Stratified Sampling

Problem: A university has 3 faculties: Agriculture ($N_1 = 2,400$), Basic Sciences ($N_2 = 1,600$), Veterinary ($N_3 = 1,000$). Total population $N = 5,000$. Allocate a sample of $n = 250$ students proportionally.

Step-by-Step Solution:

$$ n_h = n \times \frac{N_h}{N} $$ $$ n_1 = 250 \times \frac{2,400}{5,000} = 250 \times 0.48 = 120 \text{ students} $$ $$ n_2 = 250 \times \frac{1,600}{5,000} = 250 \times 0.32 = 80 \text{ students} $$ $$ n_3 = 250 \times \frac{1,000}{5,000} = 250 \times 0.20 = 50 \text{ students} $$ $$ \text{Verification: } 120 + 80 + 50 = 250 \text{ total sample.} $$

Part E: Subjective and Analytical Questions (10 Questions with Detailed Solutions)

Q1. What is a research design? Why is it important?

Solution:

A research design is the operational plan or blueprint of a study. It details what will be studied, how data will be collected, and how analysis will be conducted. It is essential because it keeps the study organized, saves time and financial resources, reduces systematic bias, and ensures valid and reliable results.

Q2. Distinguish between primary and secondary data. Give two examples of each.

Solution:

Primary Data: Data collected first-hand by the investigator specifically for the present research. Examples: Household nutrition survey, clinical trial blood pressure recordings.

Secondary Data: Pre-existing data collected by another agency for a different primary purpose. Examples: Census of India report, NSS consumer expenditure survey.

Q3. Describe four methods of collecting primary data.

Solution:

  1. Observation: Recording events or behaviors directly as they occur.
  2. Personal Interview: Asking structured questions face-to-face.
  3. Questionnaire: Distributing self-administered written forms.
  4. Schedule: Employing trained enumerators to ask questions and record responses.
Q4. What are the steps in constructing a questionnaire?

Solution:

  1. Define clear research objectives.
  2. Identify the target respondent population.
  3. Select appropriate question formats (open vs closed).
  4. Draft clear, unambiguous questions avoiding leading wording.
  5. Sequence questions logically (easy to sensitive).
  6. Pre-test on a pilot sample and refine.
  7. Finalize and deploy.
Q5. What is the difference between open-ended and closed-ended questions? Give one example of each.

Solution:

Open-ended Questions: Allow respondents to answer freely in their own words. Example: "What improvements would you suggest for hostel facilities?"

Closed-ended Questions: Provide fixed response options. Example: "Do you use hostel Wi-Fi? (Yes / No)"

Q6. What are the steps in organising data after collection?

Solution:

  1. Editing: Inspecting forms for completeness and error correction.
  2. Coding: Assigning numerical codes to categorical responses.
  3. Classification: Grouping data into homogeneous classes.
  4. Tabulation: Displaying data in structured rows and columns.
  5. Frequency Distribution: Summarizing occurrence counts per category.

Q8. What is the National Sample Survey? Why is it important?

Solution:

The National Sample Survey (NSS) is a nation-wide socio-economic sample survey conducted by the Government of India (NSO under MoSPI). It is important because it provides reliable, representative nationwide data on employment, health, education, and expenditure at relatively low cost to guide national planning and welfare policies.

Q9. Explain stratified multi-stage sampling as used by NSS.

Solution:

In NSS sampling, the country is first divided into geographical strata (districts/sectors). In the first stage, Primary Sampling Units (villages in rural sectors, urban blocks in cities) are selected randomly within each stratum. In the second stage, sample households are selected from the chosen villages/blocks for detailed interview.
