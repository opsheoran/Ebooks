2.1 Introduction to Clinical Trials

When a doctor gives a patient a tablet for fever, the doctor knows the drug has already been tested. But before that tablet reached the market, someone had to answer important questions: Does the drug work? Is it safe? What is the right dose? These questions are answered through clinical trials.
What is a Clinical Trial?
A clinical trial is a research study carried out on human volunteers to test how safe and useful a new treatment, drug, vaccine, or medical device is. The treatment being tested is called the investigational product.

2.3 Phases of Clinical Trials

Phase Main Question Participants Focus
Phase 0 How does drug act in body? Very few (10) Microdosing & early PK
Phase I Is it safe? What is safe dose? 20–100 Safety & dose-finding
Phase II Does it work? 100–300 Efficacy & short-term safety
Phase III Is it better than standard care? 1,000–5,000 Large-scale confirmatory RCT
Phase IV Post-marketing surveillance Large numbers Long-term safety & rare ADRs

2.4 Randomized Controlled Trials (RCT)

Key Elements of RCT

Randomization: Allocating participants to groups by chance (computer/sealed envelopes) to balance known/unknown confounders.

Control Group: Comparison group receiving placebo or active standard care.

Blinding: Single-blind (subject), Double-blind (subject + investigator), Triple-blind (subject + investigator + statistician).

2.7 Sample Size Estimation

Sample Size Formula (Comparing Two Proportions)
$$ n = \frac{(Z_{\alpha/2} + Z_{\beta})^2 \times 2p(1-p)}{(p_1 - p_2)^2} $$

Where $Z_{\alpha/2}$ is significance level, $Z_{\beta}$ is power ($1-\beta$), and $p_1, p_2$ are expected success rates.

2.9 Chapter End Exercises and Self-Assessment Bank

Part A: Multiple Choice Questions

1. Phase I clinical trials mainly focus on:
a) Long-term side effects after marketing
b) Safety and dose-finding
c) Price negotiation
d) Quality of life
Correct Answer: b — Phase I evaluates safety and determines safe dosage range.
2. Neither participant nor investigator knows the treatment allocation in a:
a) Open-label trial
b) Single-blind trial
c) Double-blind trial
d) Phase 0 trial
Correct Answer: c — Double-blind trial masks both patient and investigator.
