Unit IV: Introduction to Survival Analysis

Censoring, survival & hazard functions, Kaplan-Meier estimation, log-rank tests & failure rate ageing classes

4.1 Introduction to Survival Analysis & Censoring

Suppose a doctor treats ten cancer patients with a new drug. After six months, some patients die, some are still alive, and one patient shifts to another city and cannot be traced. If we only count the patients who died, we ignore those who are still alive. We also cannot say that the missing patient died. We only know that he survived until he left. This kind of data, where we do not know the exact time of the event for everyone, needs special methods. Survival analysis is the branch of statistics that deals with such data.
What is Survival Analysis?
Survival analysis is a branch of biostatistics that models time-to-event data where incomplete observations occur.

Types of Censoring

Right Censoring: Occurs when the event has not occurred by study closure or the subject is lost to follow-up. We know survival $\geq t$.

Left Censoring: Occurs when the event occurred prior to study onset, but exact time is unobserved.

Interval Censoring: Occurs when the event is known to occur within a time window $(t_a, t_b]$.

4.2 Basic Survival Functions

Fundamental Formulas
$$ S(t) = P(T > t) = 1 - F(t) $$ $$ h(t) = \frac{f(t)}{S(t)} = -\frac{d}{dt}\ln S(t) $$ $$ H(t) = \int_0^t h(u)\,du \implies S(t) = \exp[-H(t)] $$

4.3 Life Table or Actuarial Estimator

Divides follow-up into fixed intervals $(t_{i-1}, t_i]$. Adjusts for censoring by half-interval assumption: $n_i' = n_i - c_i/2$.

$$ \hat{p}_i = 1 - \frac{d_i}{n_i'}, \quad \hat{S}(t_k) = \prod_{i=1}^k \hat{p}_i $$

4.4 Kaplan-Meier (Product-Limit) Estimator

Kaplan-Meier Estimator
$$ \hat{S}(t) = \prod_{t_i \leq t} \left(1 - \frac{d_i}{n_i}\right) $$

4.5 Total Time on Test (TTT)

Total Time on Test ($TTT = \sum t_i$). Straight-line TTT plot indicates constant hazard (exponential distribution). Concave plot indicates wear-out (increasing failure rate).

4.6 Log-Rank and Wilcoxon Tests

Log-Rank Test: Evaluates overall survival curve differences under proportional hazards assumption.

Wilcoxon Test: Gives greater weight to early survival differences.

4.7 Ageing Classes of Life Distributions

Class Name Property
IFR Increasing Failure Rate $h(t)$ increases with time $t$.
IFRA Increasing Failure Rate Average Average hazard increases.
NBU New Better than Used $S(x+t) \leq S(x)S(t)$.
NBUE New Better than Used in Expectation Expected residual life of new $\geq$ used.
Class Hierarchy
$$ \text{IFR} \implies \text{IFRA} \implies \text{NBU} \implies \text{NBUE} $$

4.10 Summary & References

Survival analysis provides specialized statistical tools for time-to-event data with censoring. Kaplan-Meier curves, log-rank tests, and failure rate ageing classes form the core framework of modern biostatistical reliability and survival research.
