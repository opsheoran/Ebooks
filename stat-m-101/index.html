About STAT-MDC-101: Bio-Statistics

STAT-MDC-101: Bio-Statistics is a comprehensive 3-credit course (2 Theory + 1 Practical) offered in Semester I at CCS Haryana Agricultural University, Hisar. It equips students with empirical tools to investigate disease outbreaks, evaluate clinical drug trials, design statistical research surveys, and model time-to-event survival data.

Integrated Digital Learning: Features interactive R/SPSS code snippets, step-by-step mathematical proofs, floating whiteboard for live lectures, and a built-in scientific calculator.

Preface

Bio-Statistics is the quantitative foundation of modern public health, clinical medicine, and agricultural biological research. This digital textbook on Bio-Statistics (STAT-MDC-101) has been crafted specifically for students at CCS Haryana Agricultural University, Hisar, seamlessly bridging mathematical statistical principles with practical epidemiological, clinical, and laboratory applications.

The course unfolds chronologically: beginning with disease surveillance and screening accuracy in Unit I, progressing to gold-standard clinical trials and ethics in Unit II, covering empirical survey design, SPSS/R computing, and NSS methodology in Unit III, and culminating in survival curves, Kaplan-Meier estimation, and reliability ageing classes in Unit IV.

Biostatistics is the science that turns raw biomedical observations into lifesaving public health action.

— Prof. O.P. Sheoran

Scope and Structure

Unit I: Epidemiology Fundamentals — Outbreak investigation, person-place-time triad, incidence/prevalence, odds ratios ($OR$), relative risk ($RR$), screening test metrics (sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV), and bias control.

Unit II: Clinical Trials — Phases I–IV, Randomized Controlled Trials (RCT), single/double-blind designs, ethical guidelines (Declaration of Helsinki, ICMR), multi-centre trial logistics, and sample size calculation.

Unit III: Research Design & Data Generation — Primary vs. secondary sources, questionnaire validation, SPSS & R data pipelines, and National Sample Survey (NSS) multi-stage sampling.

Unit IV: Survival Analysis — Right/Left/Interval censoring, survival $S(t)$ and hazard $h(t)$ functions, Kaplan-Meier product-limit estimator, total time on test, and failure rate ageing classes (IFR, IFRA, NBU, NBUE).

Course Syllabus & Structure

Detailed unit-wise syllabus for STAT-MDC-101: Bio-Statistics (3 Credits: 2 Theory + 1 Practical):

Unit Title Topics Covered
Unit I Epidemiology Fundamentals Outbreak investigation protocol, person-place-time triad, attack rate, prevalence ($P$), incidence ($I$), mortality rates (CDR, CFR), case-control ($OR$) & cohort studies ($RR, AR, AR\%$), screening test evaluation (sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV), selection/recall/attrition bias & confounding.
Unit II Clinical Trials Trial definitions & objectives, Phases I–IV trials, Randomized Controlled Trials (RCT), open-label vs single/double-blind, placebo control, ethics & informed consent (ICMR/Helsinki), multi-centre trials, sample size estimation.
Unit III Research Design & Data Generation Research design principles, primary vs secondary data collection, questionnaire construction & pre-testing, data editing, coding & tabulation, SPSS & R statistical workflows, National Sample Survey (NSS) sampling methodology.
Unit IV Survival Analysis Censoring mechanisms (Right, Left, Interval), survival function $S(t)$, cumulative distribution $F(t)$, hazard rate $h(t)$, actuarial life table, Kaplan-Meier product-limit estimator, total time on test, ageing classes (IFR, IFRA, NBU, NBUE) and dual classes.

Practical / Lab Work Component

  1. Calculating and interpreting incidence, prevalence, attack rates, and case fatality rates.
  2. Constructing 2x2 contingency tables and computing Odds Ratios ($OR$) and Relative Risk ($RR$).
  3. Evaluating screening test sensitivity, specificity, PPV, and NPV from field data.
  4. Designing randomized controlled trial protocols and estimating required sample sizes.
  5. Importing, cleaning, and analyzing primary/secondary biostatistical datasets in SPSS and R.
  6. Constructing actuarial life tables and plotting Kaplan-Meier survival curves.
  7. Classifying life distributions into failure rate ageing classes (IFR, IFRA, NBU, NBUE).
