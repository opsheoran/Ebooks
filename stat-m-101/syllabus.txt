 **STAT-MDC-101: Bio-Statistics (3 credits: 2 Theory + 1 Practical, Semester I)**:

---

## **Course Objectives & Outcomes**

* **Core Objectives:** Cover foundational principles of epidemiology (investigation and measurement), introduce clinical trial design/ethics, and teach data planning, generation, and statistical analysis using software.
* **Learning Outcomes:** Students will learn to differentiate epidemiological measures, design basic studies/trials, evaluate case-control/cohort studies, and utilize statistical software to compute key biostatistical metrics.

---

## **Theoretical Modules**

### **Unit-I: Epidemiology Fundamentals**

* Principles of epidemiological investigation and basic measures (prevalence, incidence, mortality rates).
* Design and analysis of epidemiological study types (case-control vs. cohort studies).
* Quantitative methodology applied in health screening.

### **Unit-II: Clinical Trials**

* Definitions, classification, and phases (Phases I–IV) of clinical trials.
* Concepts in Randomized Controlled Clinical Trials (RCT), including ethics, multi-centre trials, and sample size estimation/importance.

### **Unit-III: Research Design & Data Generation**

* Primary vs. secondary data collection, survey questionnaire construction, and data organization.
* Data analysis workflows in statistical packages (**SPSS** and **R**).
* Overview of National Sample Survey (NSS) sampling methods.

### **Unit-IV: Survival Analysis**

* Life table techniques and survival function estimation (Actuarial and Kaplan-Meier estimators).
* Total time on test, hazard function tests, and ageing classes (IFR, IFRA, NBU, NBUE) along with their dual classes and properties.

---

## **Practical / Lab Work**

The practical component focuses on applying statistical computing (**R / SPSS**) to real or simulated health datasets:

1. Calculating/interpreting incidence, prevalence, and mortality rates.
2. Designing basic case-control, cohort, and RCT study frameworks.
3. Plotting epidemic curves and evaluating screening test sensitivity/specificity.
4. Comparing multi-centre vs. single-centre trials and identifying study ethical considerations.
5. Estimating required sample sizes based on effect size and confidence levels.
6. Importing, managing, and analyzing primary/secondary data using R or SPSS.
7. Demonstrating simple random sampling, life table construction, and Kaplan-Meier survival curves.
8. Testing hypotheses regarding exponentiality and classifying failure distributions into ageing classes.
