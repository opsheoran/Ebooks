2.1 Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors

In linear algebra, when a square matrix multiplies a non-zero column vector, the vector usually changes both its direction and length. But for certain special vectors, multiplication by matrix $A$ only changes the length by a scalar factor $\lambda$. These vectors are called eigenvectors and $\lambda$ is called an eigenvalue.

Eigenvalue and Eigenvector Definition
Let matrix $A$ be a square matrix of order $n$. A non-zero column vector $x$ is called an eigenvector of matrix $A$ if: $$ A x = \lambda x $$ where scalar $\lambda$ is called the eigenvalue of matrix $A$ corresponding to vector $x$.
Characteristic Polynomial and Equation
Equation $(A - \lambda I)x = 0$ has a non-zero solution vector $x$ if and only if determinant is zero: $$ p(\lambda) = \det(A - \lambda I) = 0 $$ $p(\lambda)$ is called the characteristic polynomial of matrix $A$, and $\det(A - \lambda I) = 0$ is called the characteristic equation of matrix $A$.

Solved Examples for Section 2.1

Example 2.1.1: Hand Calculation of Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors for a 2x2 Matrix

Question: Find the eigenvalues and corresponding eigenvectors of matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 1 \\ 2 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$.

Solution:

Step 1: Write characteristic equation $\det(A - \lambda I) = 0$:

$$ \begin{vmatrix} 4-\lambda & 1 \\ 2 & 3-\lambda \end{vmatrix} = 0 $$ $$ (4-\lambda)(3-\lambda) - (1)(2) = 0 $$ $$ (12 - 4\lambda - 3\lambda + \lambda^2) - 2 = 0 $$ $$ \lambda^2 - 7\lambda + 10 = 0 $$ $$ (\lambda - 5)(\lambda - 2) = 0 $$

Eigenvalues are:

$$ \lambda_1 = 5 $$ $$ \lambda_2 = 2 $$

Step 2: Find eigenvector for eigenvalue $\lambda_1 = 5$ by solving system $(A - 5I)x = 0$:

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 4-5 & 1 \\ 2 & 3-5 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} $$ $$ \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 1 \\ 2 & -2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} $$

Writing row equations:

$$ -x_1 + x_2 = 0 \implies x_1 = x_2 $$ $$ 2x_1 - 2x_2 = 0 \implies x_1 = x_2 $$

Choosing free variable:

$$ x_2 = 1 $$

We get:

$$ x_1 = 1 $$ $$ \text{Eigenvector for } \lambda_1 = 5 \text{ is } x^{(1)} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} $$

Step 3: Find eigenvector for eigenvalue $\lambda_2 = 2$ by solving system $(A - 2I)x = 0$:

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 4-2 & 1 \\ 2 & 3-2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} $$ $$ \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 2 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} $$

Writing row equations:

$$ 2x_1 + x_2 = 0 $$ $$ \implies x_2 = -2x_1 $$

Choosing free variable:

$$ x_1 = 1 $$

We get:

$$ x_2 = -2 $$ $$ \text{Eigenvector for } \lambda_2 = 2 \text{ is } x^{(2)} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -2 \end{bmatrix} $$
Example 2.1.2: Hand Calculation for Upper Triangular 3x3 Matrix

Question: Find the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 2 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$.

Solution:

Step 1: Write characteristic equation $\det(A - \lambda I) = 0$:

$$ \begin{vmatrix} 1-\lambda & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 2-\lambda & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & 3-\lambda \end{vmatrix} = 0 $$ $$ (1-\lambda)(2-\lambda)(3-\lambda) = 0 $$

Eigenvalues are diagonal entries:

$$ \lambda_1 = 1 $$ $$ \lambda_2 = 2 $$ $$ \lambda_3 = 3 $$

Step 2: Eigenvector for $\lambda_1 = 1$ from $(A - I)x = 0$:

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ x_3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} $$

From Row 3 equation:

$$ 2x_3 = 0 $$ $$ \implies x_3 = 0 $$

From Row 2 equation:

$$ x_2 + 4x_3 = 0 $$ $$ \implies x_2 + 4(0) = 0 $$ $$ \implies x_2 = 0 $$

Variable $x_1$ is free. Choosing:

$$ x_1 = 1 $$

Corresponding eigenvector is:

$$ x^{(1)} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} $$

Step 3: Eigenvector for $\lambda_2 = 2$ from $(A - 2I)x = 0$:

$$ \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ x_3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} $$

From Row 2 equation:

$$ 4x_3 = 0 $$ $$ \implies x_3 = 0 $$

From Row 1 equation:

$$ -x_1 + 2x_2 + 3(0) = 0 $$ $$ \implies x_1 = 2x_2 $$

Choosing free variable:

$$ x_2 = 1 $$

We obtain:

$$ x_1 = 2 $$

Corresponding eigenvector is:

$$ x^{(2)} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} $$

Step 4: Eigenvector for $\lambda_3 = 3$ from $(A - 3I)x = 0$:

$$ \begin{bmatrix} -2 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & -1 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ x_3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} \implies -x_2 + 4x_3 = 0 \implies x_2 = 4x_3 $$ $$ -2x_1 + 2(4x_3) + 3x_3 = 0 \implies -2x_1 + 11x_3 = 0 \implies x_1 = 5.5 x_3 $$

Choosing free variable:

$$ x_3 = 2 $$

We obtain:

$$ x_2 = 8 $$ $$ x_1 = 11 $$

Corresponding eigenvector is:

$$ x^{(3)} = \begin{bmatrix} 11 \\ 8 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} $$
Example 2.1.3: Hand Calculation for 3x3 Matrix with Repeated Roots

Question: Find all eigenvalues and eigenvectors of matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 3 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$.

Solution:

Step 1: Expand characteristic determinant $\det(A - \lambda I) = 0$ by Row 1:

$$ \begin{vmatrix} 2-\lambda & 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 3-\lambda & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 2-\lambda \end{vmatrix} = 0 $$ $$ (2-\lambda)[(3-\lambda)(2-\lambda) - 2] - 2[1(2-\lambda) - 1] + 1[2 - (3-\lambda)] = 0 $$ $$ (2-\lambda)[\lambda^2 - 5\lambda + 4] - 2[1 - \lambda] + 1[\lambda - 1] = 0 $$ $$ -\lambda^3 + 7\lambda^2 - 11\lambda + 5 = 0 $$ $$ (\lambda - 5)(\lambda - 1)^2 = 0 $$

Eigenvalues are:

$$ \lambda_1 = 5 $$ $$ \lambda_2 = 1 $$ $$ \lambda_3 = 1 $$

Step 2: For $\lambda_1 = 5$, solve $(A - 5I)x = 0$:

$$ \begin{bmatrix} -3 & 2 & 1 \\ 1 & -2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & -3 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ x_3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} $$

Applying EROs: $R_1 \leftrightarrow R_2$, then $R_2 \to R_2 + 3R_1$ and $R_3 \to R_3 - R_1$:

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -2 & 1 \\ 0 & -4 & 4 \\ 0 & 4 & -4 \end{bmatrix} \implies \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -2 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} $$

From Row 2 equation:

$$ -4x_2 + 4x_3 = 0 $$ $$ \implies x_2 = x_3 $$

From Row 1 equation:

$$ x_1 - 2x_2 + x_3 = 0 $$ $$ x_1 - 2x_3 + x_3 = 0 $$ $$ x_1 - x_3 = 0 $$ $$ \implies x_1 = x_3 $$

Choosing $x_3 = 1$, eigenvector is:

$$ x^{(1)} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} $$

Step 3: For repeated eigenvalue $\lambda = 1$, solve $(A - I)x = 0$:

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ x_3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} \implies x_1 + 2x_2 + x_3 = 0 $$

This single equation has 2 free variables $x_2$ and $x_3$:

$$ \text{Choose } x_2 = 1, x_3 = 0 \implies x_1 = -2 \implies x^{(2)} = \begin{bmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} $$ $$ \text{Choose } x_2 = 0, x_3 = 1 \implies x_1 = -1 \implies x^{(3)} = \begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} $$
Example 2.1.4: Symmetric Matrix Orthogonal Eigenvector Verification

Question: Find eigenvalues of symmetric matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$ and verify hand-calculated orthogonality of its eigenvectors.

Solution:

Step 1: Characteristic equation $\det(A - \lambda I) = 0$:

$$ (2-\lambda)^2 - 1 = 0 $$ $$ 4 - 4\lambda + \lambda^2 - 1 = 0 $$ $$ \lambda^2 - 4\lambda + 3 = 0 $$ $$ (\lambda-3)(\lambda-1) = 0 $$

Roots are:

$$ \lambda_1 = 3 $$ $$ \lambda_2 = 1 $$

Step 2: For $\lambda_1 = 3$, solve $(A - 3I)x = 0$:

$$ \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} $$ $$ -x_1 + x_2 = 0 $$ $$ \implies x_1 = x_2 $$

Choosing $x_2 = 1$, eigenvector is:

$$ x^{(1)} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} $$

Step 3: For $\lambda_2 = 1$, solve $(A - I)x = 0$:

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} $$ $$ x_1 + x_2 = 0 $$ $$ \implies x_2 = -x_1 $$

Choosing $x_1 = 1$, eigenvector is:

$$ x^{(2)} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix} $$

Step 4: Compute dot product $(x^{(1)})^T x^{(2)}$:

$$ (x^{(1)})^T x^{(2)} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix} = 1(1) + 1(-1) = 0 $$

Eigenvectors $x^{(1)}$ and $x^{(2)}$ are orthogonal!

Example 2.1.5: Skew-Symmetric Matrix Complex Eigenvalues

Question: Find the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$.

Solution:

Step 1: Characteristic equation $\det(A - \lambda I) = 0$:

$$ \lambda^2 + 1 = 0 $$

Roots are purely imaginary:

$$ \lambda_1 = i $$ $$ \lambda_2 = -i $$

Step 2: For $\lambda_1 = i$, solve $(A - iI)x = 0$:

$$ \begin{bmatrix} -i & 1 \\ -1 & -i \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} $$ $$ -i x_1 + x_2 = 0 $$ $$ \implies x_2 = i x_1 $$

Choosing $x_1 = 1$, eigenvector is:

$$ x^{(1)} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ i \end{bmatrix} $$

Step 3: For $\lambda_2 = -i$, solve $(A + iI)x = 0$:

$$ \begin{bmatrix} i & 1 \\ -1 & i \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} $$ $$ i x_1 + x_2 = 0 $$ $$ \implies x_2 = -i x_1 $$

Choosing $x_1 = 1$, eigenvector is:

$$ x^{(2)} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -i \end{bmatrix} $$
Example 2.1.6: Standard Textbook 3x3 Matrix Hand Calculation

Question: Find eigenvalues of matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix} 8 & -6 & 2 \\ -6 & 7 & -4 \\ 2 & -4 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$.

Solution:

Step 1: Characteristic equation $\det(A - \lambda I) = -\lambda^3 + \text{Tr}(A)\lambda^2 - M\lambda + \det(A) = 0$.

$$ \text{Tr}(A) = 8 + 7 + 3 = 18 $$ $$ M = (21-16) + (24-4) + (56-36) = 5 + 20 + 20 = 45 $$ $$ \det(A) = 8(21-16) + 6(-18+8) + 2(24-14) = 40 - 60 + 20 = 0 $$ $$ -\lambda^3 + 18\lambda^2 - 45\lambda = 0 \implies \lambda(\lambda^2 - 18\lambda + 45) = 0 \implies \lambda(\lambda-3)(\lambda-15) = 0 $$

Eigenvalues are $\lambda_1 = 0, \lambda_2 = 3, \lambda_3 = 15$.

Theorem 2.1: Cayley-Hamilton Theorem
Statement: Every square matrix $A$ satisfies its own characteristic equation: $$ p(A) = c_n A^n + c_{n-1} A^{n-1} + \dots + c_0 I = 0 $$

Proof:

Step 1: Let $B(\lambda) = \text{adj}(A - \lambda I)$. $B(\lambda)$ is a polynomial in $\lambda$ of degree at most $n-1$ with matrix coefficients:

$$ B(\lambda) = B_{n-1} \lambda^{n-1} + \dots + B_1 \lambda + B_0 $$

Step 2: We know $(A - \lambda I) \text{adj}(A - \lambda I) = \det(A - \lambda I) I$. Substitute $B(\lambda)$ and characteristic polynomial $p(\lambda) = c_n \lambda^n + \dots + c_0$:

$$ (A - \lambda I)(B_{n-1} \lambda^{n-1} + \dots + B_0) = (c_n \lambda^n + \dots + c_0) I $$

Step 3: Equating coefficients of powers of $\lambda$ on both sides:

$$ -B_{n-1} = c_n I $$ $$ A B_{n-1} - B_{n-2} = c_{n-1} I $$ $$ A B_{n-2} - B_{n-3} = c_{n-2} I $$ $$ \dots $$ $$ A B_0 = c_0 I $$

Step 4: Multiply first equation by $A^n$, second by $A^{n-1}$, and so on, then add all equations. Left side cancels completely to zero:

$$ c_n A^n + c_{n-1} A^{n-1} + \dots + c_0 I = 0 $$

Thus $p(A) = 0$. Proof complete.

2.5 Matrix Diagonalization

A square matrix $A$ of order $n$ is diagonalizable if there exists a non-singular modal matrix $P$ such that $P^{-1} A P = D$, where $D$ is a diagonal matrix.

Theorem 2.3: Diagonalizability Condition Theorem
Statement: Matrix $A$ is diagonalizable if and only if it has $n$ linearly independent eigenvectors, which occurs if Geometric Multiplicity (GM) equals Algebraic Multiplicity (AM) for each eigenvalue.

Solved Examples for Section 2.5

Example 2.5.1: Diagonalizing a 2x2 Matrix

Question: Diagonalize matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 1 \\ 2 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$ and find modal matrix $P$.

Solution:

Eigenvalues $\lambda_1 = 5, \lambda_2 = 2$. Eigenvectors $x^{(1)} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}, x^{(2)} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -2 \end{bmatrix}$.

Modal matrix composed of eigenvectors:

$$ P = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -2 \end{bmatrix} $$

Determinant of modal matrix:

$$ \det(P) = 1(-2) - 1(1) = -3 $$

Inverse modal matrix:

$$ P^{-1} = \frac{1}{-3} \begin{bmatrix} -2 & -1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} $$ $$ P^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} 2/3 & 1/3 \\ 1/3 & -1/3 \end{bmatrix} $$

Diagonal matrix $D = P^{-1} A P = \begin{bmatrix} 5 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$.

Example 2.5.2: Non-Diagonalizable Matrix Example

Question: Show that matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$ is not diagonalizable.

Solution:

Eigenvalues are:

$$ \lambda_1 = 1 $$ $$ \lambda_2 = 1 $$

Algebraic Multiplicity $\text{AM} = 2$.

Solve system $(A - I)x = 0$ for eigenvectors:

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} $$ $$ x_2 = 0 $$

Only 1 linearly independent eigenvector exists:

$$ x^{(1)} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} $$

Geometric Multiplicity $\text{GM} = 1$.

Since $\text{GM} < \text{AM}$, matrix $A$ is not diagonalizable!

2.6 Quadratic Forms

A real quadratic form in $n$ variables is $Q(x) = X^T A X = \sum \sum a_{ij} x_i x_j$, where matrix $A$ is symmetric.

Classification of Quadratic Forms
  • Positive Definite: All eigenvalues of matrix $A$ are positive ($\lambda_i > 0$).
  • Positive Semi-Definite: All $\lambda_i \geq 0$ with at least one $\lambda_i = 0$.
  • Negative Definite: All eigenvalues of matrix $A$ are negative ($\lambda_i < 0$).
  • Indefinite: Matrix $A$ has both positive and negative eigenvalues.

Solved Examples for Section 2.6

Example 2.6.1: Matrix of Quadratic Form and Definiteness

Question: Find matrix $A$ of quadratic form $Q(x_1, x_2) = 2x_1^2 + 6x_1 x_2 + 5x_2^2$ and determine its definiteness.

Solution:

Matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 3 & 5 \end{bmatrix}$. Principal minors: $D_1 = 2 > 0$, $D_2 = \det(A) = 10 - 9 = 1 > 0$. By Sylvester's criterion, quadratic form is Positive Definite.

Example 2.6.2: Canonical Reduction of a 3-Variable Quadratic Form

Question: Reduce quadratic form $Q(x_1, x_2, x_3) = x_1^2 + 2x_2^2 + 3x_3^2 + 2x_1 x_2 + 4x_2 x_3$ to canonical sum of squares form.

Solution:

Complete squares: $Q = (x_1 + x_2)^2 + x_2^2 + 4x_2 x_3 + 3x_3^2 = (x_1 + x_2)^2 + (x_2 + 2x_3)^2 - x_3^2$.

Canonical Form (Sum of Squares):

$$ Q(y_1, y_2, y_3) = y_1^2 + y_2^2 - y_3^2 $$

Rank of Quadratic Form (Non-Zero Coefficients):

$$ r = 3 $$

Index of Quadratic Form (Positive Coefficients):

$$ s = 2 $$

Signature of Quadratic Form (Positive minus Negative Coefficients):

$$ \text{Signature} = s - (r - s) = 2 - 1 = 1 $$

Definiteness Classification:

$$ \text{Classification: Indefinite (contains both positive and negative squares)} $$

Part E: Subjective Questions (10 Questions)

Q1. State and prove Cayley-Hamilton Theorem for a square matrix $A$.
Answer Outline: Write $(A - \lambda I) \text{adj}(A - \lambda I) = \det(A - \lambda I)I$. Substitute matrix polynomial $\text{adj}(A - \lambda I) = \sum B_k \lambda^k$. Equate coefficients of $\lambda^k$, multiply by $A^k$, and sum to get $p(A) = 0$.
Q2. Prove that eigenvalues of a real symmetric matrix are all real.
Answer Outline: Let $A x = \lambda x$. Pre-multiply by $\overline{x}^T$ to get $\overline{x}^T A x = \lambda \overline{x}^T x$. Take conjugate transpose to get $\overline{x}^T A x = \overline{\lambda} \overline{x}^T x$. Subtracting yields $(\lambda - \overline{\lambda})\|x\|^2 = 0 \implies \lambda = \overline{\lambda}$.
Q3. Prove that eigenvectors corresponding to distinct eigenvalues of a real symmetric matrix are orthogonal.
Answer Outline: Let $A x_1 = \lambda_1 x_1$ and $A x_2 = \lambda_2 x_2$ with $\lambda_1 \neq \lambda_2$. Compute $x_2^T A x_1 = \lambda_1 x_2^T x_1$ and $(A x_2)^T x_1 = \lambda_2 x_2^T x_1$. Since $A$ is symmetric, $x_2^T A x_1 = (A x_2)^T x_1 \implies (\lambda_1 - \lambda_2) x_2^T x_1 = 0 \implies x_2^T x_1 = 0$.
Q4. Explain the procedure to diagonalize a square matrix $A$ of order $n$.
Answer Outline: Step 1: Find characteristic polynomial and eigenvalues $\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n$. Step 2: Find $n$ linearly independent eigenvectors $x^{(1)}, \dots, x^{(n)}$. Step 3: Form modal matrix $P = [x^{(1)} \dots x^{(n)}]$. Step 4: Compute $P^{-1} A P = D$.
Q5. State Sylvester's Criterion for positive definiteness of a real quadratic form.
Answer Outline: Quadratic form $X^T A X$ is Positive Definite iff all leading principal minors $D_1 = a_{11}, D_2 = \begin{vmatrix} a_{11} & a_{12} \\ a_{21} & a_{22} \end{vmatrix}, \dots, D_n = \det(A)$ are strictly positive ($D_k > 0$).
Q6. Define minimal polynomial of a matrix $A$ and state its relation to characteristic polynomial.
Answer Outline: Minimal polynomial $m(\lambda)$ is the unique monic polynomial of lowest degree such that $m(A) = 0$. $m(\lambda)$ divides characteristic polynomial $p(\lambda)$, and both have exact same set of distinct roots (eigenvalues).
Q7. Show that if $\lambda$ is an eigenvalue of $A$, then $f(\lambda)$ is an eigenvalue of polynomial matrix $f(A)$.
Answer Outline: Let $f(x) = c_k x^k + \dots + c_0$. Apply $f(A)$ to eigenvector $x$: $f(A)x = c_k A^k x + \dots + c_0 x = c_k \lambda^k x + \dots + c_0 x = f(\lambda) x$. Thus $f(\lambda)$ is an eigenvalue.
Q8. Explain Sylvester's Law of Inertia for quadratic forms.
Answer Outline: Under any real non-singular linear transformation $X = P Y$, a real quadratic form reduces to canonical sum of squares form. The rank $r$, index $s$ (number of positive terms), and signature $2s - r$ remain strictly invariant.
Q9. Distinguish between Algebraic Multiplicity (AM) and Geometric Multiplicity (GM).
Answer Outline: AM is the multiplicity of root $\lambda$ in characteristic equation $\det(A - \lambda I) = 0$. GM is the maximum number of linearly independent eigenvectors for $\lambda$, equal to $\text{nullity}(A - \lambda I)$. Always $1 \leq \text{GM} \leq \text{AM}$.
Q10. Prove that eigenvalues of an Orthogonal matrix have unit modulus ($|\lambda| = 1$).
Answer Outline: Let $Q x = \lambda x$. Take norm squared $\|Q x\|^2 = (Q x)^H (Q x) = x^H Q^T Q x = x^H I x = x^H x = \|x\|^2$. Also $\|Q x\|^2 = \|\lambda x\|^2 = |\lambda|^2 \|x\|^2$. Equating gives $|\lambda|^2 = 1 \implies |\lambda| = 1$.
