Unit II: Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors

Characteristic polynomial, Cayley-Hamilton Theorem, Minimal Polynomial, AM vs GM Multiplicities, Matrix Diagonalization $P^{-1}AP=D$, and Quadratic Form Canonical Reduction

2.1 Eigenvalues, Eigenvectors and Spectrum

Eigenvalues and eigenvectors capture the invariant directional axes of a matrix transformation. When a matrix $A$ operates on an eigenvector $x$, the vector's direction remains unchanged—it is merely scaled by the eigenvalue scalar $\lambda$. This section presents characteristic equations, spectral definitions, and geometric scaling properties.
Figure 2.1: Geometric Scaling of Eigenvector under Matrix Action
An eigenvector x is mapped by matrix A to a parallel vector scaled by factor λ without rotation.
Eigenvalue & Eigenvector Definition
Let $A \in M_{n \times n}(F)$. A non-zero vector $x \in F^n$ ($x eq 0$) is called an eigenvector of $A$ if there exists a scalar $\lambda \in F$ such that: $$ A x = \lambda x \iff (A - \lambda I) x = 0 $$ The scalar $\lambda$ is called the eigenvalue associated with $x$.
Characteristic Polynomial & Equation
The eigenvalues $\lambda$ are the roots of the characteristic equation: $$ p(\lambda) = \det(A - \lambda I) = 0 $$ For an $n \times n$ matrix, $p(\lambda) = (-1)^n \lambda^n + (-1)^{n-1} \text{tr}(A) \lambda^{n-1} + \dots + \det(A) = 0$.

2.1 आइगनमान और आइगनवेक्टर (Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors)

जब एक आव्यूह $A$ किसी गैर-शून्य वेक्टर $x$ पर क्रिया करता है और $Ax = \lambda x$ प्राप्त होता है, तो $\lambda$ को eigenvalue और $x$ को eigenvector कहते हैं।

2.2 Algebraic Properties of Eigenvalues

Key Spectral Theorems
  • Sum of Eigenvalues: $\sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i = \text{trace}(A) = \sum a_{ii}$.
  • Product of Eigenvalues: $\prod_{i=1}^n \lambda_i = \det(A)$.
  • Eigenvalues of $A^k$: If $\lambda$ is an eigenvalue of $A$, then $\lambda^k$ is an eigenvalue of $A^k$.
  • Real Symmetric Matrices: All eigenvalues of a real symmetric matrix ($A^T = A$) are strictly real numbers.
  • Orthogonal Vectors: Eigenvectors corresponding to distinct eigenvalues of a real symmetric matrix are mutually orthogonal.

2.2 आइगनमानों के बीजगणितीय गुण (Properties of Eigenvalues)

Eigenvalues का योग $\text{trace}(A)$ के बराबर होता है और गुणनफल $\det(A)$ के बराबर होता है।

2.3 Cayley-Hamilton Theorem

Theorem 2.1: Cayley-Hamilton Theorem
Every square matrix $A \in M_{n \times n}(F)$ satisfies its own characteristic equation: $$ p(A) = (-1)^n A^n + c_{n-1} A^{n-1} + \dots + c_1 A + c_0 I = 0 $$

Applications: Cayley-Hamilton Theorem provides a direct method to express higher powers $A^k$ ($k \ge n$) and matrix inverse $A^{-1}$ as linear combinations of $\{I, A, A^2, \dots, A^{n-1}\}$.

2.3 केली-हैमिल्टन प्रमेय (Cayley-Hamilton Theorem)

प्रत्येक वर्गाकार आव्यूह अपने स्वयं के लाक्षणिक समीकरण (characteristic equation) को संतुष्ट करता है: $p(A) = 0$। इसका उपयोग $A^{-1}$ और $A^k$ निकालने में होता है।

2.4 Minimal Polynomial & Multiplicities

For an eigenvalue $\lambda_i$, we distinguish two multiplicities:

  • Algebraic Multiplicity (AM): The multiplicity of $\lambda_i$ as a root of $p(\lambda) = 0$.
  • Geometric Multiplicity (GM): The dimension of the eigenspace $E_{\lambda_i} = \text{Null}(A - \lambda_i I)$, i.e. $GM = n - \text{rank}(A - \lambda_i I)$.
Theorem 2.2: Multiplicity Inequality & Minimal Polynomial
For every eigenvalue $\lambda_i$, $1 \le \text{GM}(\lambda_i) \le \text{AM}(\lambda_i)$.
The minimal polynomial $m(\lambda)$ is the unique monic polynomial of minimum degree such that $m(A) = 0$.

2.4 न्यूनतम बहुपद और बहुलताएं (Minimal Polynomial & Multiplicities)

Algebraic Multiplicity (AM) लाक्षणिक समीकरण में जड़ की आवृत्ति है, जबकि Geometric Multiplicity (GM) आइगनस्पेस का आयाम $\text{nullity}(A - \lambda I)$ है। $1 \le GM \le AM$।

2.5 Diagonalization of Matrices

A matrix $A_{n \times n}$ is diagonalizable if it is similar to a diagonal matrix $D$, i.e. there exists an invertible modal matrix $P$ such that $P^{-1} A P = D$.

Figure 2.2: Matrix Diagonalization Similarity Transformation
Similarity transformation P⁻¹AP = D constructing diagonal matrix D from eigenvalues and modal matrix P from eigenvectors.
Theorem 2.3: Diagonalization Criterion
An $n \times n$ matrix $A$ is diagonalizable if and only if:
  1. The characteristic polynomial splits completely over $F$.
  2. For every eigenvalue $\lambda_i$, $\text{GM}(\lambda_i) = \text{AM}(\lambda_i)$.

2.5 आव्यूह का विकर्णीकरण (Matrix Diagonalization)

आव्यूह $A$ विकर्णीय (diagonalizable) होता है यदि और केवल यदि उसके पास $n$ रेखीय रूप से स्वतंत्र eigenvectors हों ($GM = AM$)। $P^{-1} A P = D = \text{diag}(\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n)$।

2.6 Quadratic Forms & Classification

A quadratic form in $n$ variables is a homogeneous polynomial of degree 2: $Q(x) = x^T A x = \sum_{i,j} a_{ij} x_i x_j$ where $A$ is a real symmetric matrix.

Classification of Quadratic Forms
A real quadratic form $Q(x) = x^T A x$ is classified by its eigenvalues $\lambda_i$:
  • Positive Definite: $Q(x) > 0$ for all $x eq 0 \iff$ All $\lambda_i > 0$.
  • Positive Semi-Definite: $Q(x) \ge 0$ for all $x \iff$ All $\lambda_i \ge 0$.
  • Negative Definite: $Q(x) < 0$ for all $x eq 0 \iff$ All $\lambda_i < 0$.
  • Indefinite: $Q(x)$ takes both positive and negative values $\iff A$ has both positive and negative eigenvalues.

2.6 द्विघात रूप और वर्गीकरण (Quadratic Forms)

द्विघात रूप $Q(x) = x^T A x$ को eigenvalues के चिन्हों द्वारा वर्गीकृत किया जाता है (Positive Definite यदि सभी $\lambda_i > 0$)।

2.7 Solved Multiline Numerical Examples

Example 2.7.1: Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors Computation

Problem: Find the eigenvalues and corresponding eigenvectors of $A = egin{bmatrix} 4 & 1 \ 2 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$.

Step-by-Step Solution:

Step 1: Characteristic Equation:

$$ \det(A - \lambda I) = egin{vmatrix} 4-\lambda & 1 \ 2 & 3-\lambda \end{vmatrix} = (4-\lambda)(3-\lambda) - 2 = \lambda^2 - 7\lambda + 10 = 0 $$ $$ (\lambda - 5)(\lambda - 2) = 0 \implies \lambda_1 = 5, \quad \lambda_2 = 2 $$

Step 2: Eigenvector for $\lambda_1 = 5$:

$$ (A - 5I) x = egin{bmatrix} -1 & 1 \ 2 & -2 \end{bmatrix} egin{bmatrix} x_1 \ x_2 \end{bmatrix} = egin{bmatrix} 0 \ 0 \end{bmatrix} \implies -x_1 + x_2 = 0 \implies x^{(1)} = egin{bmatrix} 1 \ 1 \end{bmatrix} $$

Step 3: Eigenvector for $\lambda_2 = 2$:

$$ (A - 2I) x = egin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \ 2 & 1 \end{bmatrix} egin{bmatrix} x_1 \ x_2 \end{bmatrix} = egin{bmatrix} 0 \ 0 \end{bmatrix} \implies 2x_1 + x_2 = 0 \implies x^{(2)} = egin{bmatrix} 1 \ -2 \end{bmatrix} $$
Example 2.7.2: Inverse via Cayley-Hamilton Theorem

Problem: Verify Cayley-Hamilton Theorem for $A = egin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \ 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$ and compute $A^{-1}$.

Step-by-Step Solution:

$$ \det(A - \lambda I) = (1-\lambda)(4-\lambda) - 6 = \lambda^2 - 5\lambda - 2 = 0 $$

By Cayley-Hamilton Theorem: $A^2 - 5A - 2I = 0$.

$$ A^2 = egin{bmatrix} 7 & 10 \ 15 & 22 \end{bmatrix}, \quad 5A + 2I = egin{bmatrix} 7 & 10 \ 15 & 22 \end{bmatrix} \implies A^2 - 5A - 2I = 0 \text{ (Verified)} $$

Multiply by $A^{-1}$:

$$ A - 5I - 2A^{-1} = 0 \implies A^{-1} = \frac{1}{2}(A - 5I) = \frac{1}{2}egin{bmatrix} -4 & 2 \ 3 & -1 \end{bmatrix} = egin{bmatrix} -2 & 1 \ 1.5 & -0.5 \end{bmatrix} $$
