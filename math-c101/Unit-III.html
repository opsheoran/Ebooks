3.3 Subspaces and Direct Sums

A non-empty subset $W$ of a vector space $V$ is called a subspace if $W$ itself forms a vector space under the same operations as $V$.

Theorem 3.1: Subspace Test Theorem
Statement: A non-empty subset $W \subseteq V$ is a subspace of $V(F)$ if and only if for all vectors $u, v \in W$ and all scalars $a, b \in F$: $$ a u + b v \in W $$

Proof:

($\implies$) Suppose $W$ is a subspace of $V$. By vector space axioms, $W$ is closed under vector addition and scalar multiplication. Thus for $u, v \in W$, $a u \in W$ and $b v \in W \implies a u + b v \in W$.

($\impliedby$) Suppose $a u + b v \in W$ holds for all $u, v \in W$ and $a, b \in F$.

1. Take $a = 1, b = 1 \implies u + v \in W$ (Closure under addition).

2. Take $a = c, b = 0 \implies c u \in W$ (Closure under scalar multiplication).

3. Take $a = 0, b = 0 \implies 0 u + 0 v = 0 \in W$ (Contains zero vector).

4. Take $a = -1, b = 0 \implies -u \in W$ (Contains additive inverse).

Other associative and commutative properties hold naturally because $W \subseteq V$. Thus $W$ is a subspace. Proof complete.

Theorem 3.2: Dimension of Sum of Subspaces
Statement: If $U$ and $W$ are finite-dimensional subspaces of $V$, then: $$ \dim(U + W) = \dim U + \dim W - \dim(U \cap W) $$

Proof:

Let $\dim(U \cap W) = k$. Let $\{v_1, \dots, v_k\}$ be a basis for $U \cap W$.

Extend this basis to a basis of $U$: $\{v_1, \dots, v_k, u_1, \dots, u_m\}$, so $\dim U = k + m$.

Extend the same basis to a basis of $W$: $\{v_1, \dots, v_k, w_1, \dots, w_n\}$, so $\dim W = k + n$.

Now consider set $S = \{v_1, \dots, v_k, u_1, \dots, u_m, w_1, \dots, w_n\}$. We will show $S$ is a basis for $U + W$.

1. Any element in $U + W$ is $u + w$. Since $u$ is a combination of $\{v_i, u_j\}$ and $w$ is a combination of $\{v_i, w_l\}$, $S$ spans $U + W$.

2. Check linear independence: $\sum a_i v_i + \sum b_j u_j + \sum c_l w_l = 0 \implies \sum c_l w_l = -\sum a_i v_i - \sum b_j u_j \in U$.

Since $\sum c_l w_l \in W$ and also $\in U \implies \in U \cap W$. Therefore, coefficients $c_l = 0, b_j = 0, a_i = 0$. $S$ is linearly independent.

Number of elements in $S$ is $k + m + n = (k+m) + (k+n) - k = \dim U + \dim W - \dim(U \cap W)$. Proof complete.

Solved Examples for Section 3.3

Example 3.3.1: Subspace Verification of a Plane

Question: Show that $W = \{(x, y, z) \in \mathbb{R}^3 \mid 2x - y + 3z = 0\}$ is a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^3$.

Solution:

Let $u = (x_1, y_1, z_1)$ and $v = (x_2, y_2, z_2)$ belong to $W$. Then $2x_1 - y_1 + 3z_1 = 0$ and $2x_2 - y_2 + 3z_2 = 0$.

For any scalars $a, b \in \mathbb{R}$, $a u + b v = (ax_1+bx_2, ay_1+by_2, az_1+bz_2)$.

$$ 2(ax_1+bx_2) - (ay_1+by_2) + 3(az_1+bz_2) = a(2x_1-y_1+3z_1) + b(2x_2-y_2+3z_2) = a(0) + b(0) = 0 $$

Therefore, $a u + b v \in W$. By Subspace Test Theorem, $W$ is a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^3$.
