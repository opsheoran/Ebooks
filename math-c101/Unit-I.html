Unit I: Matrices and Systems of Equations

Hermitian, Skew-Hermitian, Unitary, Orthogonal & Nilpotent matrices, Echelon forms, PAQ Normal Form, Gauss-Jordan Inversion, Rouché-Capelli System Consistency, and Doolittle/Crout LU Decomposition

1.1 Types of Special Matrices

Matrices form the computational backbone of linear algebra. In physical science applications—ranging from quantum mechanical observables to electrical circuit networks—matrices possessing special algebraic symmetries play a crucial role. This section presents complex and real matrix structures, including Hermitian, Skew-Hermitian, Unitary, Orthogonal, Idempotent, Involutory, and Nilpotent matrices.
1. Conjugate Transpose (Hermitian Transpose $A^\dagger$ or $A^H$)
For a matrix $A = (a_{ij}) \in \mathbb{C}^{m \times n}$, the conjugate transpose $A^\Theta = A^H = (\overline{A})^T = \overline{(A^T)}$ is obtained by taking the complex conjugate of each entry and transposing the matrix.
Special Matrix Definitions
  • Hermitian Matrix: A square complex matrix $A$ satisfying $A^H = A$ (i.e. $a_{ij} = \overline{a_{ji}}$). Diagonal elements of a Hermitian matrix are strictly real numbers.
  • Skew-Hermitian Matrix: A square complex matrix $A$ satisfying $A^H = -A$ (i.e. $a_{ij} = -\overline{a_{ji}}$). Diagonal elements are either purely imaginary or zero.
  • Unitary Matrix: A square complex matrix $U$ satisfying $U^H U = U U^H = I$, implying $U^{-1} = U^H$. The column (and row) vectors of a Unitary matrix form an orthonormal basis in $\mathbb{C}^n$.
  • Orthogonal Matrix: A square real matrix $Q$ satisfying $Q^T Q = Q Q^T = I$, implying $Q^{-1} = Q^T$. Determinant $\det(Q) = \pm 1$.
  • Idempotent Matrix: A square matrix $A$ satisfying $A^2 = A$. Eigenvalues of an idempotent matrix are either $0$ or $1$.
  • Involutory Matrix: A square matrix $A$ satisfying $A^2 = I$, implying $A = A^{-1}$.
  • Nilpotent Matrix: A square matrix $A$ for which there exists a positive integer $k$ (index of nilpotency) such that $A^k = 0$ while $A^{k-1} eq 0$. All eigenvalues of a nilpotent matrix are zero.
Theorem 1.1: Decomposition into Hermitian and Skew-Hermitian Parts
Every square complex matrix $A \in \mathbb{C}^{n \times n}$ can be uniquely expressed as the sum of a Hermitian matrix $B$ and a Skew-Hermitian matrix $C$: $$ A = B + C \quad \text{where } B = \frac{1}{2}(A + A^H) \text{ and } C = \frac{1}{2}(A - A^H) $$

1.1 विशेष प्रकार के आव्यूह (Types of Special Matrices)

Matrices लीनियर अलजेब्रा की कम्प्यूटेशनल रीढ़ हैं। क्वॉन्टम मैकेनिक्स और इलेक्ट्रिकल सर्किट्स में विशेष सममिति (symmetry) वाले matrices का उपयोग होता है।
विशेष Matrices की परिभाषाएं
  • Hermitian Matrix: एक जटिल आव्यूह $A$ जिसके लिए $A^H = A$ हो। इसके मुख्य विकर्ण (main diagonal) के तत्व हमेशा वास्तविक (real) होते हैं।
  • Skew-Hermitian Matrix: जिसके लिए $A^H = -A$ हो। विकर्ण तत्व या तो शून्य होते हैं या शुद्ध काल्पनिक (purely imaginary)।
  • Unitary Matrix: $U^H U = I$ (यानी $U^{-1} = U^H$)।
  • Orthogonal Matrix: वास्तविक आव्यूह $Q$ जिसके लिए $Q^T Q = I$ (यानी $Q^{-1} = Q^T$)।
  • Idempotent Matrix: $A^2 = A$।
  • Involutory Matrix: $A^2 = I$।
  • Nilpotent Matrix: $A^k = 0$ किसी धनात्मक पूर्णांक $k$ के लिए।

1.2 Matrix Rank and Row Echelon Forms

The rank of a matrix is a fundamental structural invariant reflecting the maximum number of linearly independent row or column vectors.

Row Echelon Form (REF) & Reduced Row Echelon Form (RREF)
A matrix is in Row Echelon Form (REF) if:
  1. All non-zero rows are above any rows of all zeros.
  2. The leading entry (pivot) of a non-zero row is strictly to the right of the pivot entry of the row above it.
It is in Reduced Row Echelon Form (RREF) if additionally:
  1. The leading entry in each non-zero row is equal to $1$.
  2. Each column containing a leading $1$ has zeros everywhere else in that column.
Theorem 1.2: Rank Definition via Echelon Form
The rank of a matrix $A$, denoted $\text{rank}(A)$ or $ ho(A)$, equals the number of non-zero rows (or number of pivot columns) in its row echelon form.

1.2 आव्यूह की कोटि और रो एकलोन फॉर्म (Matrix Rank and Row Echelon Forms)

Matrix का Rank $ ho(A)$ उसके स्वतंत्र पंक्तियों या स्तंभों की अधिकतम संख्या को दर्शाता है। Elementary Row Operations से इसे Row Echelon Form (REF) में बदला जाता है।

1.3 Normal Form and PAQ Canonical Reduction

By applying both elementary row operations and elementary column operations, any matrix $A \in M_{m \times n}(F)$ of rank $r$ can be reduced to its canonical Normal Form:

Theorem 1.3: PAQ Theorem
For any matrix $A_{m \times n}$ of rank $r$, there exist non-singular matrices $P_{m \times m}$ and $Q_{n \times n}$ such that: $$ P A Q = egin{bmatrix} I_r & 0 \ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} $$ where $P$ is the product of elementary row matrices and $Q$ is the product of elementary column matrices.

1.3 सामान्य रूप और PAQ कैनोनिकल रिडक्शन (Normal Form and PAQ)

प्रारंभिक पंक्ति (ERO) और स्तंभ (ECO) संक्रियाओं द्वारा आव्यूह $A$ को Normal Form $egin{bmatrix} I_r & 0 \ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$ में बदला जाता है, जहाँ $P A Q = \text{Normal Form}$।

1.4 Inverse of Matrix via Elementary Operations

Gauss-Jordan inversion computes the inverse $A^{-1}$ of an $n \times n$ non-singular matrix by augmenting with the identity matrix $[A \mid I_n]$ and performing EROs until $[I_n \mid A^{-1}]$ is obtained.

1.4 प्रारंभिक संक्रियाओं द्वारा व्युत्क्रम (Inverse via Elementary Operations)

Gauss-Jordan विधि द्वारा संवर्धित आव्यूह $[A \mid I_n]$ को EROs द्वारा $[I_n \mid A^{-1}]$ में बदला जाता है।

1.5 Systems of Linear Equations

A linear system of $m$ equations in $n$ unknowns $AX = B$ is analyzed using the augmented matrix $[A \mid B]$.

Theorem 1.4: Rouché-Capelli Theorem
1. A system $AX = B$ is consistent (has at least one solution) if and only if: $$ \text{rank}(A) = \text{rank}([A \mid B]) = r $$ 2. If consistent:
  • If $r = n$ (number of unknowns), the system has a unique solution.
  • If $r < n$, the system has infinitely many solutions with $n - r$ free parameters.
3. If $\text{rank}(A) eq \text{rank}([A \mid B])$, the system is inconsistent (no solution).

1.5 रेखीय समीकरणों के निकाय (Systems of Linear Equations)

Rouché-Capelli प्रमेय के अनुसार $AX = B$ संगत (consistent) होगा यदि और केवल यदि $\text{rank}(A) = \text{rank}([A \mid B])$।

1.6 LU Decomposition and Applications

LU decomposition factors a square matrix $A$ into the product of a unit lower triangular matrix $L$ and an upper triangular matrix $U$ ($A = LU$).

Figure 1.2: LU Factorization & Triangular System Solving
Solving AX = B via LU decomposition through forward substitution LY = B followed by back substitution UX = Y.
Doolittle vs Crout LU Formats
  • Doolittle Algorithm: $L$ has 1s on diagonal ($l_{ii} = 1$).
  • Crout Algorithm: $U$ has 1s on diagonal ($u_{ii} = 1$).

1.6 LU विखंडन और अनुप्रयोग (LU Decomposition)

आव्यूह $A$ का विखंडन $A = LU$ (Lower Triangular $L$ और Upper Triangular $U$) के रूप में करके समीकरण $AX=B$ को दो सरल अंतरालों में हल किया जाता है: $LY = B$ और $UX = Y$।

1.7 Solved Multiline Numerical Examples

Example 1.7.1: PAQ Normal Form Reduction

Problem: Reduce the matrix $A = egin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 2 \ 2 & 3 & 5 \ 3 & 4 & 7 \end{bmatrix}$ to Normal Form and find non-singular matrices $P$ and $Q$ such that $PAQ = egin{bmatrix} I_r & 0 \ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$.

Step-by-Step Solution:

Step 1: Write $A = I_3 A I_3$:

$$ egin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 2 \ 2 & 3 & 5 \ 3 & 4 & 7 \end{bmatrix} = egin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \ 0 & 1 & 0 \ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} A egin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \ 0 & 1 & 0 \ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} $$

Step 2: Apply EROs ($R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1, R_3 \to R_3 - 3R_1$):

$$ egin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 2 \ 0 & 1 & 1 \ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = egin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \ -2 & 1 & 0 \ -3 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} A egin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \ 0 & 1 & 0 \ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} $$

Step 3: Apply ERO ($R_3 \to R_3 - R_2$):

$$ egin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 2 \ 0 & 1 & 1 \ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = egin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \ -2 & 1 & 0 \ -1 & -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} A egin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \ 0 & 1 & 0 \ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} $$

Step 4: Apply ECOs ($C_2 \to C_2 - C_1, C_3 \to C_3 - 2C_1$):

$$ egin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \ 0 & 1 & 1 \ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = egin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \ -2 & 1 & 0 \ -1 & -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} A egin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 & -2 \ 0 & 1 & 0 \ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} $$

Step 5: Apply ECO ($C_3 \to C_3 - C_2$):

$$ egin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \ 0 & 1 & 0 \ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = egin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \ -2 & 1 & 0 \ -1 & -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} A egin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 & -1 \ 0 & 1 & -1 \ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} $$

Conclusion: Normal Form is $egin{bmatrix} I_2 & 0 \ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$, Rank $r = 2$, with $P = egin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \ -2 & 1 & 0 \ -1 & -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$ and $Q = egin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 & -1 \ 0 & 1 & -1 \ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$.

Example 1.7.2: Doolittle LU Decomposition

Problem: Find the LU decomposition ($A = LU$) using Doolittle's method for $A = egin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 & 1 \ 4 & 3 & 3 \ 8 & 7 & 9 \end{bmatrix}$ and solve $AX = egin{bmatrix} 4 \ 10 \ 26 \end{bmatrix}$.

Step-by-Step Solution:

Set $L = egin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \ l_{21} & 1 & 0 \ l_{31} & l_{32} & 1 \end{bmatrix}$ and $U = egin{bmatrix} u_{11} & u_{12} & u_{13} \ 0 & u_{22} & u_{23} \ 0 & 0 & u_{33} \end{bmatrix}$.

$$ u_{11}=2, u_{12}=1, u_{13}=1 $$ $$ l_{21} = 4/2 = 2, \quad l_{31} = 8/2 = 4 $$ $$ u_{22} = 3 - (2)(1) = 1, \quad u_{23} = 3 - (2)(1) = 1 $$ $$ l_{32} = (7 - 4(1))/1 = 3 $$ $$ u_{33} = 9 - (4(1) + 3(1)) = 2 $$ $$ L = egin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \ 2 & 1 & 0 \ 4 & 3 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad U = egin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 & 1 \ 0 & 1 & 1 \ 0 & 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix} $$

Forward Substitution $LY = B \implies Y = egin{bmatrix} 4 \ 2 \ 4 \end{bmatrix}$. Back Substitution $UX = Y \implies X = egin{bmatrix} 1 \ 0 \ 2 \end{bmatrix}$.

Correct Answer: (b) — Hermitian matrix satisfies $A^H = A$.
Q2. The diagonal entries of a Skew-Hermitian matrix must be:
Correct Answer: (b) — Skew-Hermitian diagonal elements satisfy $a_{ii} = -\overline{a_{ii}} \implies \text{Re}(a_{ii}) = 0$.

Part B: Fill in the Blanks (20 Questions)

FIB 1. A matrix $A$ satisfying $A^2 = A$ is called ____________.
Answer: Idempotent — Idempotent matrices satisfy $A^2 = A$.

Part C: True / False Statements (20 Questions)

TF 1. The determinant of an orthogonal matrix is always $\pm 1$.
Answer: True — Since $Q^T Q = I \implies \det(Q)^2 = 1 \implies \det(Q) = \pm 1$.

Part E: Subjective Questions

Q1. State and prove the Rouché-Capelli Theorem for consistency of $AX = B$.
A system $AX = B$ is consistent iff $\text{rank}(A) = \text{rank}([A \mid B])$. If rank $r = n$ (unknowns), unique solution; if $r < n$, infinitely many solutions.
