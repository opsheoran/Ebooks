Unit I: Matrices and Systems of Equations

Hermitian, Skew-Hermitian, Unitary, Orthogonal & Nilpotent matrices, Echelon forms, PAQ Normal Form, Gauss-Jordan Inversion, Rouché-Capelli System Consistency, and Doolittle/Crout LU Decomposition

1.1 Types of Special Matrices

Matrices are rectangular arrays of numbers arranged in rows and columns. In linear algebra, we deal with both real numbers and complex numbers. Matrices having special properties are very helpful in mathematics and physics problems.

1. Conjugate Transpose of a Matrix ($A^H$ or $A^\dagger$)
Let matrix $A = (a_{ij})$ be a matrix of order $m \times n$ with complex entries. If we take the complex conjugate of every element of matrix $A$ and then take its transpose, we get the conjugate transpose matrix $A^H = (\overline{A})^T$.
Definitions of Special Matrices
  • Hermitian Matrix: A square matrix $A$ is called a Hermitian matrix if $A^H = A$. This means element $a_{ij} = \overline{a_{ji}}$. The main diagonal elements of a Hermitian matrix are always real numbers.
  • Skew-Hermitian Matrix: A square matrix $A$ is called a Skew-Hermitian matrix if $A^H = -A$. This means element $a_{ij} = -\overline{a_{ji}}$. The main diagonal elements of a Skew-Hermitian matrix are either zero or purely imaginary numbers.
  • Unitary Matrix: A square matrix $U$ is called a Unitary matrix if $U^H U = I$ or $U U^H = I$. This means the inverse matrix $U^{-1}$ is equal to its conjugate transpose $U^H$.
  • Orthogonal Matrix: A real square matrix $Q$ is called an Orthogonal matrix if $Q^T Q = I$ or $Q Q^T = I$. This means $Q^{-1} = Q^T$. The determinant of an orthogonal matrix is always $+1$ or $-1$.
  • Idempotent Matrix: A square matrix $A$ is called an Idempotent matrix if $A^2 = A$.
  • Involutory Matrix: A square matrix $A$ is called an Involutory matrix if $A^2 = I$. This means $A^{-1} = A$.
  • Nilpotent Matrix: A square matrix $A$ is called a Nilpotent matrix if there exists a positive integer $k$ such that $A^k = 0$. The smallest positive integer $k$ is called the index of nilpotency.
Theorem 1.1: Unique Decomposition into Hermitian and Skew-Hermitian Matrices
Statement: Every square complex matrix $A$ can be uniquely expressed as the sum of a Hermitian matrix $B$ and a Skew-Hermitian matrix $C$: $$ A = B + C \quad \text{where } B = \frac{1}{2}(A + A^H) \text{ and } C = \frac{1}{2}(A - A^H) $$

Proof:

Step 1 (Existence): Let matrix $A$ be any square complex matrix. We can write matrix $A$ as:

$$ A = \frac{1}{2}(2A) = \frac{1}{2}(A + A^H + A - A^H) = \frac{1}{2}(A + A^H) + \frac{1}{2}(A - A^H) $$

Let $B = \frac{1}{2}(A + A^H)$ and $C = \frac{1}{2}(A - A^H)$.

Now let us check $B^H$:

$$ B^H = \left(\frac{1}{2}(A + A^H)\right)^H = \frac{1}{2}(A^H + (A^H)^H) = \frac{1}{2}(A^H + A) = B $$

Since $B^H = B$, matrix $B$ is a Hermitian matrix.

Now let us check $C^H$:

$$ C^H = \left(\frac{1}{2}(A - A^H)\right)^H = \frac{1}{2}(A^H - (A^H)^H) = \frac{1}{2}(A^H - A) = -\frac{1}{2}(A - A^H) = -C $$

Since $C^H = -C$, matrix $C$ is a Skew-Hermitian matrix.

Thus, $A = B + C$ where $B$ is Hermitian and $C$ is Skew-Hermitian.

Step 2 (Uniqueness): Suppose matrix $A$ can also be written as $A = B_1 + C_1$, where $B_1^H = B_1$ and $C_1^H = -C_1$.

Taking conjugate transpose on both sides:

$$ A^H = (B_1 + C_1)^H = B_1^H + C_1^H = B_1 - C_1 $$

Now add $A$ and $A^H$:

$$ A + A^H = (B_1 + C_1) + (B_1 - C_1) = 2 B_1 \implies B_1 = \frac{1}{2}(A + A^H) = B $$

Now subtract $A^H$ from $A$:

$$ A - A^H = (B_1 + C_1) - (B_1 - C_1) = 2 C_1 \implies C_1 = \frac{1}{2}(A - A^H) = C $$

Thus $B_1 = B$ and $C_1 = C$. This proves that the representation is unique. Hence proved.

Solved Examples for Section 1.1

Example 1.1.1: Verification of Hermitian Matrix

Question: Show that the matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 2+i \\ 2-i & 5 \end{bmatrix}$ is a Hermitian matrix, and check its diagonal elements.

Solution:

Step 1: Take the complex conjugate matrix $\overline{A}$ of matrix $A$ by changing sign of $i$:

$$ \overline{A} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 2-i \\ 2+i & 5 \end{bmatrix} $$

Step 2: Take the transpose of matrix $\overline{A}$ to find matrix $A^H$:

$$ A^H = (\overline{A})^T = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 2+i \\ 2-i & 5 \end{bmatrix} $$

Step 3: Compare matrix $A^H$ with matrix $A$. We see that $A^H = A$. Therefore, matrix $A$ is a Hermitian matrix.

Step 4: Check diagonal elements $a_{11} = 3$ and $a_{22} = 5$. Both are real numbers.

Example 1.1.2: Verification of Skew-Hermitian Matrix

Question: Show that the matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1+i \\ -1+i & 2i \end{bmatrix}$ is a Skew-Hermitian matrix.

Solution:

Step 1: Find complex conjugate matrix $\overline{A}$:

$$ \overline{A} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1-i \\ -1-i & -2i \end{bmatrix} $$

Step 2: Take transpose to find matrix $A^H$:

$$ A^H = (\overline{A})^T = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -1-i \\ 1-i & -2i \end{bmatrix} $$

Step 3: Find $-A$ by multiplying every entry of matrix $A$ by $-1$:

$$ -A = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -(1+i) \\ -(-1+i) & -(2i) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -1-i \\ 1-i & -2i \end{bmatrix} $$

Step 4: We see that $A^H = -A$. Therefore, matrix $A$ is a Skew-Hermitian matrix. The diagonal elements are $0$ and $2i$ (purely imaginary).

Example 1.1.3: Verification of Unitary Matrix

Question: Show that the matrix $U = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & i \\ i & 1 \end{bmatrix}$ is a Unitary matrix.

Solution:

Step 1: Find conjugate transpose matrix $U^H$:

$$ U^H = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -i \\ -i & 1 \end{bmatrix} $$

Step 2: Multiply matrix $U^H$ and matrix $U$:

$$ U^H U = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -i \\ -i & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & i \\ i & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{2} \begin{bmatrix} 1(1) + (-i)(i) & 1(i) + (-i)(1) \\ (-i)(1) + 1(i) & (-i)(i) + 1(1) \end{bmatrix} $$ $$ = \frac{1}{2} \begin{bmatrix} 1 - i^2 & i - i \\ -i + i & -i^2 + 1 \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{2} \begin{bmatrix} 1 - (-1) & 0 \\ 0 & -(-1) + 1 \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{2} \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = I $$

Since $U^H U = I$, matrix $U$ is a Unitary matrix.

Example 1.1.4: Decomposition of a Matrix into Hermitian and Skew-Hermitian Parts

Question: Express the matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix} 2+i & 3 \\ 1-i & 4i \end{bmatrix}$ as $A = B + C$, where matrix $B$ is Hermitian and matrix $C$ is Skew-Hermitian.

Solution:

Step 1: Find matrix $A^H$:

$$ A^H = \begin{bmatrix} 2-i & 1+i \\ 3 & -4i \end{bmatrix} $$

Step 2: Calculate Hermitian part matrix $B = \frac{1}{2}(A + A^H)$:

$$ B = \frac{1}{2} \left( \begin{bmatrix} 2+i & 3 \\ 1-i & 4i \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 2-i & 1+i \\ 3 & -4i \end{bmatrix} \right) = \frac{1}{2} \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 4+i \\ 4-i & 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 2 + 0.5i \\ 2 - 0.5i & 0 \end{bmatrix} $$

Step 3: Calculate Skew-Hermitian part matrix $C = \frac{1}{2}(A - A^H)$:

$$ C = \frac{1}{2} \left( \begin{bmatrix} 2+i & 3 \\ 1-i & 4i \end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix} 2-i & 1+i \\ 3 & -4i \end{bmatrix} \right) = \frac{1}{2} \begin{bmatrix} 2i & 2-i \\ -2-i & 8i \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} i & 1 - 0.5i \\ -1 - 0.5i & 4i \end{bmatrix} $$

Step 4: Verify $B + C = \begin{bmatrix} 2+i & 3 \\ 1-i & 4i \end{bmatrix} = A$.

Example 1.1.5: Idempotent and Nilpotent Verification

Question: (a) Show that matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -2 \\ 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix}$ is Idempotent. (b) Show that matrix $N = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$ is Nilpotent with index 3.

Solution:

(a) Multiply matrix $A$ with matrix $A$:

$$ A^2 = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -2 \\ 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -2 \\ 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 4-2 & -4+2 \\ 2-1 & -2+1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -2 \\ 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix} = A $$

Since $A^2 = A$, matrix $A$ is Idempotent.

(b) Calculate $N^2$ and $N^3$ for matrix $N$:

$$ N^2 = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} $$ $$ N^3 = N^2 N = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = 0 $$

Since $N^3 = 0$ and $N^2 \neq 0$, matrix $N$ is Nilpotent of index 3.

Example 1.1.6: Verification of Orthogonal Matrix

Question: Show that the matrix $Q = \frac{1}{3} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 2 \\ 2 & 1 & -2 \\ -2 & 2 & -1 \end{bmatrix}$ is an Orthogonal matrix.

Solution:

Step 1: Take transpose matrix $Q^T$:

$$ Q^T = \frac{1}{3} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & -2 \\ 2 & 1 & 2 \\ 2 & -2 & -1 \end{bmatrix} $$

Step 2: Multiply $Q^T$ and $Q$:

$$ Q^T Q = \frac{1}{9} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & -2 \\ 2 & 1 & 2 \\ 2 & -2 & -1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 2 \\ 2 & 1 & -2 \\ -2 & 2 & -1 \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{9} \begin{bmatrix} 1+4+4 & 2+2-4 & 2-4+2 \\ 2+2-4 & 4+1+4 & 4-2-2 \\ 2-4+2 & 4-2-2 & 4+4+1 \end{bmatrix} $$ $$ = \frac{1}{9} \begin{bmatrix} 9 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 9 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 9 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = I $$

Since $Q^T Q = I$, matrix $Q$ is an Orthogonal matrix.

1.1 विशेष प्रकार के आव्यूह (Types of Special Matrices)

आव्यूह पंक्ति (rows) और स्तंभ (columns) में व्यवस्थित संख्याओं की एक आयताकार व्यवस्था (rectangular array) होती है।

Conjugate Transpose ($A^H$): Matrix $A$ के प्रत्येक अवयव का complex conjugate लेकर उसका transpose लेने पर Conjugate Transpose matrix $A^H = (\overline{A})^T$ प्राप्त होता है।

Hermitian Matrix: यदि $A^H = A$ हो, तो matrix $A$ को Hermitian Matrix कहते हैं। इसके मुख्य विकर्ण (main diagonal) के अवयव हमेशा वास्तविक संख्याएं (real numbers) होते हैं।

Skew-Hermitian Matrix: यदि $A^H = -A$ हो, तो matrix $A$ को Skew-Hermitian Matrix कहते हैं। इसके मुख्य विकर्ण के अवयव शून्य या केवल काल्पनिक (purely imaginary) होते हैं।

Unitary Matrix: यदि $U^H U = I$ हो, तो matrix $U$ को Unitary Matrix कहते हैं।

Orthogonal Matrix: यदि $Q^T Q = I$ हो, तो matrix $Q$ को Orthogonal Matrix कहते हैं।

Idempotent Matrix: यदि $A^2 = A$ हो, तो matrix $A$ को Idempotent Matrix कहते हैं।

Involutory Matrix: यदि $A^2 = I$ हो, तो matrix $A$ को Involutory Matrix कहते हैं।

Nilpotent Matrix: यदि $A^k = 0$ हो, तो matrix $A$ को Nilpotent Matrix कहते हैं।

प्रमेय 1.1 का प्रमाण (Theorem 1.1 Proof in Hinglish)

कथन: प्रत्येक वर्ग Matrix $A$ को अद्वितीय रूप से एक Hermitian Matrix $B$ और एक Skew-Hermitian Matrix $C$ के योग के रूप में लिखा जा सकता है: $A = B + C$, जहाँ $B = \frac{1}{2}(A + A^H)$ और $C = \frac{1}{2}(A - A^H)$।

प्रमाण:

चरण 1 (अस्तित्व): Matrix $A$ को हम इस प्रकार लिख सकते हैं:

$$ A = \frac{1}{2}(2A) = \frac{1}{2}(A + A^H + A - A^H) = \frac{1}{2}(A + A^H) + \frac{1}{2}(A - A^H) $$

मान लीजिए $B = \frac{1}{2}(A + A^H)$ तथा $C = \frac{1}{2}(A - A^H)$।

अब $B^H = \left(\frac{1}{2}(A + A^H)\right)^H = \frac{1}{2}(A^H + A) = B$ (Hermitian)।

तथा $C^H = \left(\frac{1}{2}(A - A^H)\right)^H = \frac{1}{2}(A^H - A) = -C$ (Skew-Hermitian)।

चरण 2 (अद्वितीयता): यदि $A = B_1 + C_1$ हो, तो $A^H = B_1 - C_1$। जोड़ने पर $2B_1 = A + A^H \implies B_1 = B$ तथा घटाने पर $2C_1 = A - A^H \implies C_1 = C$। अतः रूप अद्वितीय है। सिद्ध हुआ।

1.2 Matrix Rank and Row Echelon Forms

The rank of a matrix tells us the maximum number of linearly independent rows or columns present in the matrix. We use elementary row operations to reduce a matrix into simple echelon forms.

Elementary Row Operations (EROs)
There are three types of elementary row operations on a matrix:
  1. Interchanging any two rows: $R_i \leftrightarrow R_j$.
  2. Multiplying any row by a non-zero constant $k$: $R_i \to k R_i$.
  3. Adding $k$ times row $j$ to row $i$: $R_i \to R_i + k R_j$.
Row Echelon Form (REF) & Reduced Row Echelon Form (RREF)
A matrix is in Row Echelon Form (REF) if:
  1. All completely zero rows are placed at the bottom of the matrix.
  2. The first non-zero entry (leading entry or pivot) in a row appears strictly to the right of the pivot in the row above it.
A matrix is in Reduced Row Echelon Form (RREF) if additionally:
  1. Every leading entry (pivot) is equal to $1$.
  2. Each column containing a pivot has zeros in all other positions of that column.
Theorem 1.2: Invariance of Matrix Rank under Elementary Row Operations
Statement: Performing elementary row operations on a matrix does not change its rank. The rank $\rho(A)$ of matrix $A$ is equal to the number of non-zero rows in its Row Echelon Form.

Proof:

Let matrix $A$ have row vectors $r_1, r_2, \dots, r_m$. A linear combination of rows is $a_1 r_1 + a_2 r_2 + \dots + a_m r_m = 0$.

1. Interchanging two rows $R_i \leftrightarrow R_j$ only changes the order of row vectors. The subspace spanned by rows remains identical.

2. Multiplying row $r_i$ by non-zero constant $k$ gives $k r_i$. Since $k \neq 0$, the row vector $k r_i$ spans the exact same line as $r_i$.

3. Replacing row $r_i$ by $r_i + k r_j$ creates a linear combination. Any vector that was a combination of original rows can still be written using new rows by subtracting $k r_j$.

Therefore, row space dimension remains unchanged under all three operations. Hence, rank $\rho(A)$ is invariant under EROs. Proof complete.

Solved Examples for Section 1.2

Example 1.2.1: Finding Rank of a 3x3 Matrix via Echelon Form

Question: Find the rank of matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 & 4 & 7 \\ 3 & 6 & 10 \end{bmatrix}$ using elementary row operations.

Solution:

Step 1: Perform operations $R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1$ and $R_3 \to R_3 - 3R_1$ to make entries below $a_{11}$ equal to 0:

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 - 2(1) & 4 - 2(2) & 7 - 2(3) \\ 3 - 3(1) & 6 - 3(2) & 10 - 3(3) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} $$

Step 2: Perform operation $R_3 \to R_3 - R_2$:

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 - 0 & 0 - 0 & 1 - 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} $$

Step 3: Count non-zero rows in this Row Echelon Form. Row 1 and Row 2 are non-zero. Row 3 is completely zero. Number of non-zero rows is 2. Therefore, rank of matrix $A$ is $\rho(A) = 2$.

Example 1.2.2: RREF Reduction of a 3x4 Matrix

Question: Reduce matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 & 2 & 0 & 2 \\ 4 & 0 & 4 & 8 \end{bmatrix}$ to Reduced Row Echelon Form (RREF) and find its rank.

Solution:

Step 1: Perform $R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1$ and $R_3 \to R_3 - 4R_1$:

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 4 & -4 & -4 \\ 0 & 4 & -4 & -4 \end{bmatrix} $$

Step 2: Perform $R_2 \to \frac{1}{4} R_2$, then $R_3 \to R_3 - 4R_2$:

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 & -1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} $$

Step 3: Make entry above pivot equal to 0 by $R_1 \to R_1 + R_2$:

$$ \text{RREF} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 & -1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} $$

Number of non-zero rows is 2. Therefore, rank of matrix $A$ is 2.

Example 1.2.3: Parameter-Dependent Matrix Rank

Question: Find the rank of matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & k \\ 1 & 4 & k^2 \end{bmatrix}$ for different real values of parameter $k$.

Solution:

Step 1: Perform $R_2 \to R_2 - R_1$ and $R_3 \to R_3 - R_1$:

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & k-1 \\ 0 & 3 & k^2-1 \end{bmatrix} $$

Step 2: Perform $R_3 \to R_3 - 3R_2$:

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & k-1 \\ 0 & 0 & (k^2-1) - 3(k-1) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & k-1 \\ 0 & 0 & (k-1)(k-2) \end{bmatrix} $$

Conclusions:

  • Case 1: If $k = 1$ or $k = 2$, bottom row entry becomes 0. Number of non-zero rows is 2 $\implies \rho(A) = 2$.
  • Case 2: If $k \neq 1$ and $k \neq 2$, all 3 rows are non-zero $\implies \rho(A) = 3$.

1.2 आव्यूह की कोटि और रो एकलोन फॉर्म (Matrix Rank and Row Echelon Forms)

Elementary Row Operations (EROs):

  1. दो पंक्तियों की आपस में अदला-बदली: $R_i \leftrightarrow R_j$।
  2. किसी पंक्ति को अ-शून्य संख्या $k$ से गुणा करना: $R_i \to k R_i$।
  3. किसी पंक्ति में दूसरी पंक्ति का $k$ गुना जोड़ना: $R_i \to R_i + k R_j$।

Rank ($\rho(A)$): Row Echelon Form में अ-शून्य पंक्तियों (non-zero rows) की संख्या को Matrix का Rank कहते हैं।

1.3 Normal Form and PAQ Canonical Reduction

By applying both elementary row operations and elementary column operations, we can reduce any matrix $A$ of rank $r$ into its canonical Normal Form $\begin{bmatrix} I_r & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$.

Theorem 1.3: PAQ Theorem
Statement: For any matrix $A$ of order $m \times n$ and rank $r$, there exist non-singular matrix $P$ of order $m \times m$ and non-singular matrix $Q$ of order $n \times n$ such that: $$ P A Q = \begin{bmatrix} I_r & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} $$

Proof:

Step 1: Write matrix $A$ as $A_{m \times n} = I_m A I_n$.

Step 2: Apply elementary row operations on matrix $A$ on the left side and simultaneously on matrix $I_m$ on the right side. This gives row echelon form $A_1 = P_1 A I_n$, where $P_1$ is the product of row operation matrices.

Step 3: Apply elementary column operations on matrix $A_1$ on the left side and simultaneously on matrix $I_n$ on the right side to clear non-pivot entries. This reduces matrix $A_1$ into Normal Form $\begin{bmatrix} I_r & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$.

Step 4: Accumulate column operations into matrix $Q$. Since row and column operations preserve non-singularity, matrix $P$ and matrix $Q$ are both non-singular matrices. Proof complete.

Solved Examples for Section 1.3

Example 1.3.1: PAQ Reduction of a 3x3 Matrix

Question: Reduce matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 3 & 5 \\ 3 & 4 & 7 \end{bmatrix}$ to Normal Form and find non-singular matrix $P$ and matrix $Q$ such that $PAQ = \begin{bmatrix} I_2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$.

Solution:

Step 1: Write $A = I_3 A I_3$:

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 3 & 5 \\ 3 & 4 & 7 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} A \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} $$

Step 2: Apply EROs $R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1, R_3 \to R_3 - 3R_1$:

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ -2 & 1 & 0 \\ -3 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} A \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} $$

Step 3: Apply ERO $R_3 \to R_3 - R_2$:

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ -2 & 1 & 0 \\ -1 & -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} A \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} $$

Step 4: Apply ECOs $C_2 \to C_2 - C_1, C_3 \to C_3 - 2C_1$, then $C_3 \to C_3 - C_2$:

$$ \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ -2 & 1 & 0 \\ -1 & -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} A \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} $$

Result: $P = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ -2 & 1 & 0 \\ -1 & -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$, $Q = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$, Normal Form $\begin{bmatrix} I_2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$. Rank $r = 2$.

1.3 सामान्य रूप और PAQ रिडक्शन (Normal Form and PAQ)

पंक्ति संक्रियाओं (EROs) और स्तंभ संक्रियाओं (ECOs) का उपयोग करके किसी भी matrix $A$ को Normal Form $\begin{bmatrix} I_r & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$ में बदला जा सकता है।

1.4 Inverse of Matrix via Elementary Operations

Gauss-Jordan method computes the inverse matrix $A^{-1}$ of a non-singular matrix $A$ by setting up augmented matrix $[A \mid I_n]$ and applying row operations until it becomes $[I_n \mid A^{-1}]$.

Theorem 1.4: Invertibility Condition
Statement: A square matrix $A$ of order $n$ is invertible if and only if $\text{rank}(A) = n$.

Proof:

($\implies$) Suppose matrix $A$ is invertible. Then $A A^{-1} = I_n$. Determinant $\det(A A^{-1}) = \det(A)\det(A^{-1}) = \det(I_n) = 1$. This implies $\det(A) \neq 0$. Since determinant is non-zero, all $n$ rows are linearly independent $\implies \text{rank}(A) = n$.

($\impliedby$) Suppose $\text{rank}(A) = n$. Then matrix $A$ can be reduced to identity matrix $I_n$ using elementary row operations $E_k \dots E_1 A = I_n$. Let $P = E_k \dots E_1$. Then $P A = I_n \implies P = A^{-1}$. Thus $A$ is invertible. Proof complete.

Solved Examples for Section 1.4

Example 1.4.1: Gauss-Jordan Inversion of a 3x3 Matrix

Question: Find the inverse of matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & 4 \\ 5 & 6 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$ using Gauss-Jordan method.

Solution:

Step 1: Write augmented matrix $[A \mid I_3]$:

$$ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|ccc} 1 & 2 & 3 & 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 4 & 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 5 & 6 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{array}\right] $$

Step 2: Apply $R_3 \to R_3 - 5R_1$:

$$ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|ccc} 1 & 2 & 3 & 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 4 & 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -4 & -15 & -5 & 0 & 1 \end{array}\right] $$

Step 3: Apply $R_3 \to R_3 + 4R_2$:

$$ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|ccc} 1 & 2 & 3 & 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 4 & 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & -5 & 4 & 1 \end{array}\right] $$

Step 4: Clear entries above pivot ($R_2 \to R_2 - 4R_3$, $R_1 \to R_1 - 3R_3$, then $R_1 \to R_1 - 2R_2$):

$$ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|ccc} 1 & 0 & 0 & -24 & 18 & 5 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 & 20 & -15 & -4 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & -5 & 4 & 1 \end{array}\right] $$

Therefore, inverse matrix $A^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} -24 & 18 & 5 \\ 20 & -15 & -4 \\ -5 & 4 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$.

1.4 प्रारंभिक संक्रियाओं द्वारा व्युत्क्रम (Inverse via EROs)

Gauss-Jordan विधि में $[A \mid I_n]$ को row operations द्वारा $[I_n \mid A^{-1}]$ में बदला जाता है।

1.5 Systems of Linear Equations

A system of $m$ linear equations in $n$ variables $AX = B$ is analyzed using augmented matrix $[A \mid B]$.

Theorem 1.5: Rouché-Capelli Theorem
Statement:
  1. The linear system $AX = B$ is consistent (has solutions) if and only if: $$ \text{rank}(A) = \text{rank}([A \mid B]) $$
  2. If $\text{rank}(A) = \text{rank}([A \mid B]) = n$ (number of variables), the system has a unique solution.
  3. If $\text{rank}(A) = \text{rank}([A \mid B]) = r < n$, the system has infinitely many solutions depending on $n - r$ free parameters.
  4. If $\text{rank}(A) \neq \text{rank}([A \mid B])$, the system is inconsistent (no solution).

Proof:

Let $A = [C_1, C_2, \dots, C_n]$ where $C_j$ are column vectors. The system $AX = B$ can be written as:

$$ x_1 C_1 + x_2 C_2 + \dots + x_n C_n = B $$

This equation states that vector $B$ is a linear combination of columns $C_1, \dots, C_n$.

Vector $B$ belongs to column space of $A$ if and only if adding $B$ to matrix $A$ does not increase the rank of column space. Thus $\text{rank}(A) = \text{rank}([A \mid B])$.

If rank $r = n$, all variables are pivot variables $\implies$ Unique solution. If $r < n$, $n - r$ variables are free variables $\implies$ Infinitely many solutions. Proof complete.

Solved Examples for Section 1.5

Example 1.5.1: Non-Homogeneous System with Unique Solution

Question: Solve the system of linear equations: $x + y + z = 6$, $2x + y - z = 1$, $x + 2y + 2z = 9$.

Solution:

Step 1: Write augmented matrix $[A \mid B]$:

$$ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 1 & 1 & 6 \\ 2 & 1 & -1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 2 & 9 \end{array}\right] $$

Step 2: Apply $R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1$ and $R_3 \to R_3 - R_1$:

$$ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 1 & 1 & 6 \\ 0 & -1 & -3 & -11 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 & 3 \end{array}\right] $$

Step 3: Apply $R_3 \to R_3 + R_2$:

$$ \left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 1 & 1 & 6 \\ 0 & -1 & -3 & -11 \\ 0 & 0 & -2 & -8 \end{array}\right] $$

Step 4: Check ranks: $\text{rank}(A) = 3 = \text{rank}([A \mid B]) = n = 3 \implies$ Unique solution.

Step 5: Back substitution:
$-2z = -8 \implies z = 4$.
$-y - 3(4) = -11 \implies -y - 12 = -11 \implies y = 1$.
$x + 1 + 4 = 6 \implies x = 1$.
Solution: $x = 1, y = 1, z = 4$.

1.5 रेखीय समीकरणों के निकाय (Systems of Linear Equations)

Rouché-Capelli प्रमेय के अनुसार $AX = B$ की संगति (consistency) की जांच की जाती है।

1.6 LU Decomposition and Applications

LU decomposition factors a square matrix $A$ into a lower triangular matrix $L$ and an upper triangular matrix $U$ ($A = LU$).

Theorem 1.6: Doolittle LU Factorization Theorem
Statement: If all principal leading minors of square matrix $A$ are non-zero, then matrix $A$ can be uniquely factored into $A = L U$, where $L$ is a unit lower triangular matrix ($l_{ii} = 1$) and $U$ is an upper triangular matrix.

Proof:

During Gaussian elimination without row interchanges, we use row multipliers $m_{ij} = a_{ij}^{(k)} / a_{kk}^{(k)}$ to create zeros below pivot $a_{kk}$.

Matrix operations correspond to lower triangular elimination matrices $E_k \dots E_1 A = U$.

Taking inverse gives $A = (E_1^{-1} \dots E_k^{-1}) U = L U$, where $L = E_1^{-1} \dots E_k^{-1}$ contains lower triangular multipliers $m_{ij}$ with ones on diagonal. Proof complete.

Solved Examples for Section 1.6

Example 1.6.1: Doolittle LU Factorization and System Solution

Question: Factor matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 & 1 \\ 4 & 3 & 3 \\ 8 & 7 & 9 \end{bmatrix}$ into $A = LU$ using Doolittle method ($l_{ii}=1$) and solve system $A X = B$ for vector $B = \begin{bmatrix} 4 \\ 10 \\ 26 \end{bmatrix}$.

Solution:

Step 1: Set $L = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ l_{21} & 1 & 0 \\ l_{31} & l_{32} & 1 \end{bmatrix}$ and $U = \begin{bmatrix} u_{11} & u_{12} & u_{13} \\ 0 & u_{22} & u_{23} \\ 0 & 0 & u_{33} \end{bmatrix}$.

Step 2: Compute elements row by row:
$u_{11}=2, u_{12}=1, u_{13}=1$.
$l_{21} = 4/2 = 2, \quad l_{31} = 8/2 = 4$.
$u_{22} = 3 - 2(1) = 1, \quad u_{23} = 3 - 2(1) = 1$.
$l_{32} = (7 - 4(1))/1 = 3$.
$u_{33} = 9 - (4(1) + 3(1)) = 2$.

$$ L = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 2 & 1 & 0 \\ 4 & 3 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad U = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix} $$

Step 3: Forward substitution $L Y = B \implies \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 2 & 1 & 0 \\ 4 & 3 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} y_1 \\ y_2 \\ y_3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 4 \\ 10 \\ 26 \end{bmatrix} \implies y_1 = 4, y_2 = 2, y_3 = 4$.

Step 4: Back substitution $U X = Y \implies \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ x_3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 4 \\ 2 \\ 4 \end{bmatrix} \implies x_3 = 2, x_2 = 0, x_1 = 1$.
