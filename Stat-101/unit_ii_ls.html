2.1 Introduction to Curve Fitting

2.1 Curve Fitting का परिचय

In statistics and experimental sciences, we often collect a set of \( n \) paired observations — written as \( (X_1,Y_1), (X_2,Y_2), \dots, (X_n,Y_n) \). When we plot these points on a graph, they form what is called a scatter diagram.

Statistics और experimental sciences में, हम अक्सर \( n \) paired observations का एक set collect करते हैं — जिसे \( (X_1,Y_1), (X_2,Y_2), \dots, (X_n,Y_n) \) के रूप में लिखते हैं। जब इन points को graph पर plot करते हैं, तो वे एक scatter diagram बनाते हैं।

Curve fitting is the mathematical process of finding one equation — a "curve" — that best describes the general pattern of this scattered data. The curve could be a straight line or a more complex curved shape.

Curve fitting वह mathematical process है जिसमें हम एक ऐसी equation — यानी एक "curve" — ढूँढते हैं जो scattered data के general pattern को सबसे अच्छी तरह describe करे। यह curve straight line भी हो सकती है या कोई complex curved shape भी।

Why do we fit curves?

Curves Fit क्यों करते हैं?

  • Interpolation & Extrapolation: To estimate the value of \( Y \) for a given \( X \) — either within the data range (interpolation) or outside it (extrapolation).
  • Data Summarisation: To replace a big, complex data table with a single compact mathematical formula.
  • Trend Analysis: To understand the underlying law or pattern that links the two variables — physical, biological, or economic.
  • Interpolation & Extrapolation: किसी दिए गए \( X \) के लिए \( Y \) की value estimate करने के लिए — चाहे data range के अंदर हो (interpolation) या बाहर (extrapolation)।
  • Data Summarisation: बड़ी और complex data table को एक single mathematical formula से replace करने के लिए।
  • Trend Analysis: दो variables के बीच के underlying law या pattern को समझने के लिए — चाहे वो physical, biological, या economic हो।

2.2 The Principle of Least Squares

2.2 Principle of Least Squares

The key challenge in curve fitting is deciding what "best fit" actually means. For this, we use the Principle of Least Squares.

Curve fitting में सबसे बड़ी challenge यह decide करना है कि "best fit" का मतलब क्या है। इसके लिए हम Principle of Least Squares का use करते हैं।

Say we fit a curve \( Y = f(X) \) to our data. For any input \( X_i \), there are two \( Y \) values:
1. The observed value \( y_i \) — the actual data point.
2. The estimated value \( \hat{y}_i = f(X_i) \) — the value our curve predicts.

मान लीजिए हम data पर curve \( Y = f(X) \) fit करते हैं। किसी भी input \( X_i \) के लिए दो \( Y \) values होती हैं:
1. Observed value \( y_i \) — असल data point।
2. Estimated value \( \hat{y}_i = f(X_i) \) — हमारे curve की predicted value।

The gap between the actual and predicted value is called the residual or error for the \( i \)-th observation: \( e_i = y_i - \hat{y}_i \).

Actual और predicted value के बीच के अंतर को \( i \)-th observation का residual या error कहते हैं: \( e_i = y_i - \hat{y}_i \)।

The Core Definition

मुख्य Definition

The Principle of Least Squares says: the best-fitting curve is the one that minimises the sum of the squares of all residuals.

Principle of Least Squares कहता है: best-fitting curve वह है जो सभी residuals के squares के sum को minimise करे।

\[ S = \sum_{i=1}^{n} e_i^2 = \sum_{i=1}^{n} (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2 \longrightarrow \text{minimum} \]

Why Do We Square the Errors?

Errors का Square क्यों करते हैं?

  • Sign Cancellation: If we just add raw errors, the positive ones (points above the curve) and negative ones (points below) will cancel out. A very bad curve could still show a total error of zero!
  • Absolute Values are Difficult: We could use \( |e_i| \), but the absolute value function has sharp corners at zero and is hard to differentiate in calculus.
  • Penalty for Large Errors: Squaring puts a heavy penalty on big outliers. This forces the fitted curve to represent all data points fairly.
  • Sign Cancellation: अगर raw errors सीधे जोड़ दें, तो positive errors (curve के ऊपर के points) और negative errors (curve के नीचे के points) एक-दूसरे को cancel कर देंगे। एक बहुत बुरा curve भी zero total error दिखा सकता है!
  • Absolute Values मुश्किल होते हैं: \( |e_i| \) use कर सकते हैं, लेकिन absolute value function को zero पर differentiate करना calculus में मुश्किल होता है।
  • बड़े Errors पर Penalty: Square करने से बड़े outliers को भारी penalty मिलती है। इससे fitted curve सभी data points को सही represent करता है।

2.3 Fitting a Straight Line: \( Y = a + bX \)

2.3 Straight Line Fit करना: \( Y = a + bX \)

A straight line is the simplest curve. We use it when the variables seem to change at a constant rate. Here, \( a \) is the Y-intercept (where the line crosses the Y-axis) and \( b \) is the slope (rate of change).

Straight line सबसे simple curve है। इसे तब use करते हैं जब variables एक constant rate से बदलते हों। यहाँ \( a \) Y-intercept है (जहाँ line Y-axis को cross करती है) और \( b \) slope है (change की rate)।

Step-by-Step Derivation of the Normal Equations

Normal Equations का Step-by-Step Derivation

Let the best-fit line be \( \hat{y}_i = a + bx_i \). The sum of squared errors we need to minimise is:

मान लें best-fit line \( \hat{y}_i = a + bx_i \) है। Minimise करने के लिए squared errors का sum है:

\[ S = \sum_{i=1}^{n} (y_i - a - bx_i)^2 \]

To find the minimum, take the partial derivative of \( S \) with respect to \( a \) and \( b \), then set both equal to zero.

Minimum ढूँढने के लिए, \( S \) का partial derivative \( a \) और \( b \) के respect में लें, फिर दोनों को zero के बराबर रखें।

Step 1: Differentiate \( S \) with respect to \( a \)

Step 1: \( S \) को \( a \) के respect में Differentiate करें

\[ \frac{\partial S}{\partial a} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} 2(y_i - a - bx_i) \cdot (-1) = 0 \]

Divide by −2 and distribute the summation:

−2 से divide करें और summation को distribute करें:

\[ \sum_{i=1}^{n} y_i - \sum_{i=1}^{n} a - \sum_{i=1}^{n} bx_i = 0 \]

Since adding constant \( a \) for \( n \) terms gives \( na \), we get Normal Equation 1:

Constant \( a \) को \( n \) बार जोड़ने पर \( na \) मिलता है, इसलिए Normal Equation 1 मिलती है:

\[ \sum Y = na + b \sum X \]

Step 2: Differentiate \( S \) with respect to \( b \)

Step 2: \( S \) को \( b \) के respect में Differentiate करें

\[ \frac{\partial S}{\partial b} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} 2(y_i - a - bx_i) \cdot (-x_i) = 0 \]

Divide by −2, multiply \( x_i \) inside, and distribute:

−2 से divide करें, \( x_i \) को अंदर multiply करें, और distribute करें:

\[ \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i y_i - a \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i - b \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i^2 = 0 \]

Rearranging gives Normal Equation 2:

Rearrange करने पर Normal Equation 2 मिलती है:

\[ \sum XY = a \sum X + b \sum X^2 \]

2.4 Fitting a Second-Degree Parabola: \( Y = a + bX + cX^2 \)

2.4 Second-Degree Parabola Fit करना: \( Y = a + bX + cX^2 \)

When the scatter plot shows a single bend — either curving up or down — a straight line is not enough. We need to fit a second-degree polynomial, also called a parabola. This model has three unknown constants: \( a \), \( b \), and \( c \).

जब scatter plot में एक "bend" (घुमाव) दिखे — ऊपर की तरफ या नीचे की तरफ — तो straight line काम नहीं आती। हमें एक second-degree polynomial, यानी parabola, fit करना होता है। इस model में तीन unknown constants होते हैं: \( a \), \( b \), और \( c \)।

Derivation of Normal Equations

Normal Equations का Derivation

The sum of squares is \( S = \sum (y_i - a - bx_i - cx_i^2)^2 \). Since we have three unknowns, we set three partial derivatives to zero: \( \frac{\partial S}{\partial a}=0 \), \( \frac{\partial S}{\partial b}=0 \), and \( \frac{\partial S}{\partial c}=0 \). This gives us three normal equations.

Sum of squares \( S = \sum (y_i - a - bx_i - cx_i^2)^2 \) है। तीन unknowns होने की वजह से तीन partial derivatives को zero रखते हैं: \( \frac{\partial S}{\partial a}=0 \), \( \frac{\partial S}{\partial b}=0 \), और \( \frac{\partial S}{\partial c}=0 \)। इससे तीन normal equations मिलती हैं।

\[ \sum Y = na + b \sum X + c \sum X^2 \quad \text{(Normal Eq I)} \] \[ \sum XY = a \sum X + b \sum X^2 + c \sum X^3 \quad \text{(Normal Eq II)} \] \[ \sum X^2Y = a \sum X^2 + b \sum X^3 + c \sum X^4 \quad \text{(Normal Eq III)} \]

2.5 The "Change of Origin" Shortcut

2.5 "Change of Origin" Shortcut

Solving a 3×3 system with terms like \( X^4 \) can be very tedious — especially when \( X \) values are large numbers (like years). If the \( X \) values are equally spaced by an interval \( h \), we can shift the origin to the middle value of \( X \). This simplifies our calculations greatly.

\( X^4 \) जैसे terms वाली 3×3 equations solve करना बहुत थका देने वाला हो सकता है — खासकर जब \( X \) values बड़ी हों (जैसे years)। अगर \( X \) values interval \( h \) के साथ equally spaced हों, तो हम origin को \( X \) की middle value पर shift कर सकते हैं। इससे calculations बहुत आसान हो जाती हैं।

Define a new variable \( U \):

एक नया variable \( U \) define करें:

If \( n \) is odd:

यदि \( n \) odd है:

\[ U = \frac{X - \text{middle term}}{h} \]

If \( n \) is even:

यदि \( n \) even है:

\[ U = \frac{X - (\text{mean of two middle terms})}{h/2} \]

Since the \( U \) values are symmetric around zero (like −2, −1, 0, 1, 2), the sums of odd powers become zero:

चूँकि \( U \) values zero के आस-पास symmetric हो जाती हैं (जैसे −2, −1, 0, 1, 2), odd powers के sums zero बन जाते हैं:

\[ \sum U = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \sum U^3 = 0 \]

This makes the normal equations much simpler!

इससे normal equations काफी आसान हो जाती हैं!

2.6 Fitting a Power Curve: \( Y = aX^b \)

2.6 Power Curve Fit करना: \( Y = aX^b \)

The power curve is used when one variable grows as a power of another. The equation \( Y = aX^b \) is non-linear. To apply the least squares method, we first convert it into a straight line using logarithms.

Power curve तब use होता है जब एक variable दूसरे की power के रूप में बढ़े। Equation \( Y = aX^b \) non-linear है। Least squares लगाने के लिए पहले इसे logarithms की help से straight line में convert करते हैं।

Linearisation via Logarithms

Logarithms से Linearisation

Start with:

शुरू करते हैं:

\[ Y = aX^b \]

Take the common logarithm \( \log_{10} \) of both sides:

दोनों तरफ common logarithm \( \log_{10} \) लेते हैं:

\[ \log Y = \log a + b \log X \]

Now substitute to map this to a standard straight line \( V = A + BU \):

अब इसे standard straight line \( V = A + BU \) में map करने के लिए variables substitute करें:

  • Let \( V = \log Y \)
  • Let \( U = \log X \)
  • Let \( A = \log a \)
  • Let \( B = b \)

The normal equations for this linear form are:

इस linear form के लिए normal equations हैं:

\[ \sum V = nA + B \sum U \] \[ \sum UV = A \sum U + B \sum U^2 \]

Once \( A \) and \( B \) are calculated, recover the original parameters:

\( A \) और \( B \) calculate होने के बाद, original parameters वापस पाएं:

\[ a = \text{antilog}(A) \quad \text{or} \quad a = 10^A \] \[ b = B \]

2.7 Fitting Exponential Curves: \( Y = ab^X \) and \( Y = ae^{bX} \)

2.7 Exponential Curves Fit करना: \( Y = ab^X \) और \( Y = ae^{bX} \)

Exponential curves model situations where growth or decay happens very rapidly — like compound interest, bacterial growth, or radioactive decay. Here, the independent variable \( X \) sits in the exponent.

Exponential curves उन situations को model करते हैं जहाँ growth या decay बहुत तेज़ी से होती है — जैसे compound interest, bacterial growth, या radioactive decay। यहाँ independent variable \( X \) exponent में होता है।

(A) Fitting Base-b Exponential: \( Y = ab^X \)

(A) Base-b Exponential Fit करना: \( Y = ab^X \)

Take the common logarithm \( \log_{10} \) of both sides:

दोनों तरफ common logarithm \( \log_{10} \) लें:

\[ \log Y = \log a + X \log b \]

Now map to the linear form \( V = A + BX \):

अब linear form \( V = A + BX \) में map करें:

  • Let \( V = \log Y \)
  • Let \( A = \log a \)
  • Let \( B = \log b \)
  • Notice that \( X \) is NOT transformed. It stays as \( X \).

After calculating \( A \) and \( B \) via normal equations, recover the original parameters: \( a = 10^A \) and \( b = 10^B \).

Normal equations से \( A \) और \( B \) calculate होने के बाद, original parameters recover करें: \( a = 10^A \) और \( b = 10^B \)।

(B) Fitting Natural Exponential: \( Y = ae^{bX} \)

(B) Natural Exponential Fit करना: \( Y = ae^{bX} \)

Since the base is Euler's number \( e \), use the natural logarithm \( \ln \):

Base Euler's number \( e \) है, इसलिए natural logarithm \( \ln \) use करते हैं:

\[ \ln Y = \ln a + bX \ln e \implies \ln Y = \ln a + bX \quad (\text{since } \ln e = 1) \]

Map to \( V = A + BX \):

\( V = A + BX \) में map करें:

  • Let \( V = \ln Y \)
  • Let \( A = \ln a \)
  • Let \( B = b \)

📝 Subjective Questions (Detailed Solutions) — 20 Questions

Q1. Explain the Principle of Least Squares in curve fitting. Why is the sum of squared errors minimised rather than the sum of absolute errors?

Solution: The Principle of Least Squares says the best-fitting curve is the one that minimises the sum of the squares of all residuals (the gaps between the actual and predicted values). We square the errors for three reasons. First, raw positive and negative errors cancel out — so their plain sum is not useful. Second, absolute values have sharp corners and cannot be differentiated easily in calculus. Third, squaring puts a heavier penalty on large errors, so the curve is forced to fit all the data fairly.

Q2. Derive the normal equations for fitting a straight line \( Y = a + bX \).

Solution: Let the line be \( \hat{Y}_i = a + bX_i \). The error for the \( i \)-th point is \( e_i = Y_i - a - bX_i \). The sum of squared errors is \( S = \sum (Y_i - a - bX_i)^2 \). To minimise \( S \), take partial derivatives with respect to \( a \) and \( b \) and set them to zero:
\( \frac{\partial S}{\partial a} = -2 \sum (Y_i - a - bX_i) = 0 \implies \sum Y = na + b\sum X \)   (Equation 1)
\( \frac{\partial S}{\partial b} = -2 \sum X_i(Y_i - a - bX_i) = 0 \implies \sum XY = a\sum X + b\sum X^2 \)   (Equation 2)

Q3. State the normal equations for fitting a second-degree parabola.

Solution: For \( Y = a + bX + cX^2 \), there are three unknown constants \( a, b, c \). Differentiating the squared error sum with respect to each gives three normal equations:
1) \( \sum Y = na + b\sum X + c\sum X^2 \)
2) \( \sum XY = a\sum X + b\sum X^2 + c\sum X^3 \)
3) \( \sum X^2Y = a\sum X^2 + b\sum X^3 + c\sum X^4 \)

Q4. Explain the "Change of Origin" shortcut and how it simplifies curve fitting calculations.

Solution: When \( X \) values are equally spaced by an interval \( h \), computing sums like \( \sum X^3 \) and \( \sum X^4 \) with large numbers is very tedious. We shift the origin to the middle \( X \) value by defining a new variable \( U \). For an odd number of items, \( U = (X - \text{middle})/h \). This makes \( U \) values symmetric around zero (like −2, −1, 0, 1, 2), which means \( \sum U = 0 \) and \( \sum U^3 = 0 \). This removes terms from the normal equations, turning a complex 3×3 system into three much simpler equations.

Q5. Outline the steps to fit a power curve \( Y = aX^b \).

Solution:
1) Take logarithms on both sides to linearise: \( \log Y = \log a + b \log X \).
2) Substitute: \( V = \log Y \), \( A = \log a \), \( U = \log X \). The equation becomes \( V = A + bU \).
3) Write normal equations: \( \sum V = nA + b\sum U \) and \( \sum UV = A\sum U + b\sum U^2 \).
4) Build a computation table for \( U, V, U^2, UV \) and solve for \( A \) and \( b \).
5) Recover original \( a \) as \( a = \text{antilog}(A) \). Parameter \( b \) stays the same.

Q6. How do you linearise \( Y = ae^{bX} \)? Write the normal equations.

Solution: Take natural log on both sides: \( \ln Y = \ln a + bX \). Since \( \ln e = 1 \), it simplifies nicely. Let \( V = \ln Y \) and \( A = \ln a \). The linear form is \( V = A + bX \). Normal equations:
1) \( \sum V = nA + b\sum X \)
2) \( \sum XV = A\sum X + b\sum X^2 \)
After solving for \( A \) and \( b \), recover \( a = e^A \).

Q7. Set up the normal equations to fit a straight line for the points (1, 2), (2, 5), (3, 9).

Solution: Here \( n = 3 \). Let \( Y = a + bX \).
\( \sum X = 6,\quad \sum Y = 16,\quad \sum X^2 = 14,\quad \sum XY = 39 \)
Normal equations:
\( 16 = 3a + 6b \)
\( 39 = 6a + 14b \)

Q8. When fitting a parabola using change of origin (\( \sum U = 0, \sum U^3 = 0 \)), how do the normal equations simplify?

Solution: The original normal equations are:
\( \sum Y = na + b\sum U + c\sum U^2 \)
\( \sum UY = a\sum U + b\sum U^2 + c\sum U^3 \)
\( \sum U^2Y = a\sum U^2 + b\sum U^3 + c\sum U^4 \)
Setting \( \sum U = 0 \) and \( \sum U^3 = 0 \):
1) \( \sum Y = na + c\sum U^2 \)
2) \( \sum UY = b\sum U^2 \implies b = \frac{\sum UY}{\sum U^2} \) (found directly!)
3) \( \sum U^2Y = a\sum U^2 + c\sum U^4 \)
Now \( b \) is directly solved, and \( a, c \) come from the 2×2 system of equations 1 and 3.

Q9. Why is curve fitting important in trend analysis?

Solution: Curve fitting replaces scattered, noisy data points with one clean mathematical function. This function shows the underlying trend — for example, whether a population is growing linearly or exponentially. Once the trend is expressed as a formula, researchers can easily estimate missing data values (interpolation) or make predictions beyond the data range (extrapolation).

Q10. A student wants to fit \( Y = aX + b/X \). Derive the normal equations for this curve.

Solution: Error: \( E_i = Y_i - aX_i - \frac{b}{X_i} \). Sum of squares: \( S = \sum (Y_i - aX_i - \frac{b}{X_i})^2 \).
Differentiate w.r.t. \( a \): \( \frac{\partial S}{\partial a} = -2\sum X_i(Y_i - aX_i - \frac{b}{X_i}) = 0 \implies \sum X_iY_i = a\sum X_i^2 + nb \)
Differentiate w.r.t. \( b \): \( \frac{\partial S}{\partial b} = -2\sum \frac{1}{X_i}(Y_i - aX_i - \frac{b}{X_i}) = 0 \implies \sum \frac{Y_i}{X_i} = na + b\sum \frac{1}{X_i^2} \)
These are the two normal equations.

Q11. Define Scatter Diagram and Approximating Curve.

Solution: A Scatter Diagram is a graph where each paired observation \( (X_i, Y_i) \) is plotted as a point. It gives a visual picture of how the two variables relate. An Approximating Curve is a smooth curve drawn through the scatter diagram that follows the general trend of the data, while keeping total deviations as small as possible.

Q12. Prove that the normal equation for intercept \( a \) in a straight line ensures the sum of residuals is zero.

Solution: The normal equation for \( a \) comes from: \( \frac{\partial S}{\partial a} = -2 \sum (Y_i - a - bX_i) = 0 \). Dividing by −2 gives \( \sum (Y_i - \hat{Y}_i) = 0 \). Since \( (Y_i - \hat{Y}_i) \) is exactly the residual \( e_i \), we have \( \sum e_i = 0 \). So for any least-squares straight line, the sum of all residuals is always zero.

Q13. Transform \( Y = ab^X \) into a linear form and write the equations to recover \( a \) and \( b \).

Solution: Take base-10 log: \( \log_{10} Y = \log_{10} a + X\log_{10} b \). Let \( V = \log Y \), \( A = \log a \), \( B = \log b \). The linear form is \( V = A + BX \). After solving normal equations for \( A \) and \( B \), recover: \( a = 10^A \) and \( b = 10^B \).

Q14. Given: \( \sum X = 15, \sum Y = 204, \sum X^2 = 55, \sum XY = 748 \) for 5 data points. Fit a straight line.

Solution: \( n = 5 \). Normal equations:
\( 204 = 5a + 15b \)  (Eq 1)
\( 748 = 15a + 55b \)  (Eq 2)
Multiply Eq 1 by 3: \( 612 = 15a + 45b \). Subtract from Eq 2: \( 136 = 10b \implies b = 13.6 \).
Put in Eq 1: \( 204 = 5a + 204 \implies a = 0 \).
Straight line: \( Y = 13.6X \).

Q15. What is the difference between fitting \( Y = ab^X \) and \( Y = aX^b \)?

Solution:
1) \( Y = ab^X \) is an exponential curve. Variable \( X \) is in the exponent. Linearised form: \( \log Y = \log a + X\log b \). Only \( Y \) is transformed; \( X \) stays as it is.
2) \( Y = aX^b \) is a power curve. Variable \( X \) is raised to a constant power. Linearised form: \( \log Y = \log a + b\log X \). Both \( X \) and \( Y \) must be transformed into log variables.

Q16. Can the sum of absolute errors \( \sum |y_i - \hat{y}_i| \) be used for curve fitting? Why or why not?

Solution: Technically yes, but it is not used in standard curve fitting. The absolute value function creates sharp "V" corners. At zero, it cannot be differentiated with standard calculus. This means we cannot simply set derivatives to zero to find the minimum. Squaring the errors, on the other hand, creates a smooth parabolic function that is easy to differentiate — leading cleanly to the Normal Equations.

Q17. For X = 2001, 2002, 2003, 2004, 2005, apply the change of origin shortcut to find U.

Solution: Here \( n = 5 \) (odd). Interval \( h = 1 \) year. Middle term is 2003. Using \( U = (X - 2003)/1 \):
X = 2001 → U = −2;   X = 2002 → U = −1;   X = 2003 → U = 0;   X = 2004 → U = 1;   X = 2005 → U = 2.
Check: \( \sum U = −2−1+0+1+2 = 0 \). ✓

Q18. What is the determinant form for the equation of the line of best fit?

Solution: Eliminating constants \( a \) and \( b \) from the line \( Y = a + bX \) and the two normal equations, the line of best fit can be written neatly in determinant form as: \[ \begin{vmatrix} Y & X & 1 \\ \sum y_i & \sum x_i & n \\ \sum x_iy_i & \sum x_i^2 & \sum x_i \end{vmatrix} = 0 \]

Q19. What sign will the second-order derivatives \( \frac{\partial^2 S}{\partial a^2} \) have, and what does this prove?

Solution: The second-order derivatives are positive. In calculus, a positive second derivative at a critical point means the function is concave up — which confirms that the normal equation solution gives a true minimum of the total error \( S \), not a maximum.

Q20. Why is building a computation table important when fitting curves?

Solution: A computation table neatly organises all necessary sums — like \( \sum X, \sum Y, \sum X^2, \sum XY \) — that go into the normal equations. Curve fitting involves many products and powers across multiple data points. Trying to compute them randomly leads to arithmetic mistakes. A proper table with one column per required term and a totals row at the bottom keeps things accurate and makes it simple to substitute values directly into the normal equations.

📝 Subjective Questions (Detailed Solutions) — 20 Questions

Q1. Curve fitting में Principle of Least Squares explain करें। Absolute errors की जगह squared errors का sum minimise क्यों करते हैं?

Solution: Principle of Least Squares कहता है कि best-fitting curve वह है जो सभी residuals (actual और predicted values का अंतर) के squares के sum को minimise करे। Errors का square तीन कारणों से करते हैं: पहला, raw positive और negative errors एक-दूसरे को cancel कर देते हैं — तो plain sum काम का नहीं। दूसरा, absolute values को calculus में differentiate करना मुश्किल होता है, जबकि squares smooth होते हैं। तीसरा, square करने से बड़े errors पर भारी penalty लगती है, जिससे curve सभी data को fairly represent करता है।

Q2. Straight line \( Y = a + bX \) fit करने के लिए normal equations derive करें।

Solution: मान लें line \( \hat{Y}_i = a + bX_i \) है। \( i \)-th point का error \( e_i = Y_i - a - bX_i \) है।
Squared errors का sum: \( S = \sum (Y_i - a - bX_i)^2 \)।
\( S \) minimise करने के लिए \( a \) और \( b \) के respect में partial derivatives को zero रखें:
\( \frac{\partial S}{\partial a} = 0 \implies \sum Y = na + b\sum X \)   (Equation 1)
\( \frac{\partial S}{\partial b} = 0 \implies \sum XY = a\sum X + b\sum X^2 \)   (Equation 2)

Q3. Second-degree parabola fit करने की normal equations लिखें।

Solution: Parabola \( Y = a + bX + cX^2 \) के लिए तीन unknown constants \( a, b, c \) होते हैं। Squared error sum को तीनों के respect में differentiate करने पर तीन normal equations मिलती हैं:
1) \( \sum Y = na + b\sum X + c\sum X^2 \)
2) \( \sum XY = a\sum X + b\sum X^2 + c\sum X^3 \)
3) \( \sum X^2Y = a\sum X^2 + b\sum X^3 + c\sum X^4 \)

Q4. "Change of Origin" shortcut क्या है और यह calculations को कैसे आसान बनाता है?

Solution: जब \( X \) values interval \( h \) के साथ equally spaced हों, तो बड़े numbers के साथ \( \sum X^3 \), \( \sum X^4 \) calculate करना बहुत मुश्किल हो जाता है। हम एक नया variable \( U \) define करके origin को \( X \) की middle value पर shift करते हैं। Odd items के लिए \( U = (X - \text{middle})/h \)। इससे \( U \) values zero के आस-पास symmetric बन जाती हैं (−2, −1, 0, 1, 2), यानी \( \sum U = 0 \) और \( \sum U^3 = 0 \)। Normal equations से terms हट जाते हैं और complex 3×3 system आसान equations में बदल जाता है।

Q5. Power curve \( Y = aX^b \) fit करने के steps बताएं।

Solution:
1) दोनों तरफ logarithm लेकर linearise करें: \( \log Y = \log a + b \log X \)।
2) Variables substitute करें: \( V = \log Y \), \( A = \log a \), \( U = \log X \)। Equation \( V = A + bU \) बन जाती है।
3) Normal equations बनाएं: \( \sum V = nA + b\sum U \) और \( \sum UV = A\sum U + b\sum U^2 \)।
4) Computation table बनाकर \( A \) और \( b \) solve करें।
5) \( a = \text{antilog}(A) \) से original \( a \) recover करें। \( b \) वही रहता है।

Q6. Exponential curve \( Y = ae^{bX} \) को linearise कैसे करते हैं? Normal equations लिखें।

Solution: दोनों तरफ natural log \( \ln \) लें: \( \ln Y = \ln a + bX \) (क्योंकि \( \ln e = 1 \))।
मान लें \( V = \ln Y \) और \( A = \ln a \)। Linear form \( V = A + bX \) है।
Normal equations:
1) \( \sum V = nA + b\sum X \)
2) \( \sum XV = A\sum X + b\sum X^2 \)
Solve करने के बाद \( a = e^A \) से recover करें।

Q7. Points (1, 2), (2, 5), (3, 9) के लिए straight line की normal equations set up करें।

Solution: \( n = 3 \)। मान लें \( Y = a + bX \)।
\( \sum X = 6, \quad \sum Y = 16, \quad \sum X^2 = 14, \quad \sum XY = 39 \)
Normal equations:
\( 16 = 3a + 6b \)
\( 39 = 6a + 14b \)

Q8. Change of origin use करने पर (\( \sum U = 0, \sum U^3 = 0 \)) parabola की normal equations कैसे simplify होती हैं?

Solution: Original normal equations:
\( \sum Y = na + b\sum U + c\sum U^2 \)
\( \sum UY = a\sum U + b\sum U^2 + c\sum U^3 \)
\( \sum U^2Y = a\sum U^2 + b\sum U^3 + c\sum U^4 \)
\( \sum U = 0 \) और \( \sum U^3 = 0 \) रखने पर:
1) \( \sum Y = na + c\sum U^2 \)
2) \( \sum UY = b\sum U^2 \implies b = \frac{\sum UY}{\sum U^2} \) (directly मिल जाता है!)
3) \( \sum U^2Y = a\sum U^2 + c\sum U^4 \)
अब \( b \) direct मिलता है, और \( a, c \) equations 1 और 3 के 2×2 system से निकलते हैं।

Q9. Trend analysis में curve fitting क्यों important है?

Solution: Curve fitting scattered data points को एक clean mathematical function से replace कर देता है। यह function data को control करने वाला underlying trend दिखाता है — जैसे population linearly बढ़ रही है या exponentially। एक बार formula मिलने पर, missing data को interpolate और future predictions के लिए extrapolate करना आसान हो जाता है।

Q10. एक student \( Y = aX + b/X \) fit करना चाहता है। इस curve के लिए normal equations derive करें।

Solution: Error: \( E_i = Y_i - aX_i - \frac{b}{X_i} \)। Sum of squares: \( S = \sum (Y_i - aX_i - \frac{b}{X_i})^2 \)।
\( a \) के respect में differentiate: \( \sum X_iY_i = a\sum X_i^2 + nb \)
\( b \) के respect में differentiate: \( \sum \frac{Y_i}{X_i} = na + b\sum \frac{1}{X_i^2} \)
ये दो normal equations हैं।

Q11. Scatter Diagram और Approximating Curve define करें।

Solution: Scatter Diagram एक graph है जिसमें paired observations \( (X_i, Y_i) \) को points के रूप में plot किया जाता है। यह visually दो variables का relationship और spread दिखाता है। Approximating Curve एक smooth curve है — जैसे straight line या parabola — जो scatter diagram के trend को follow करे और overall deviations minimise करे।

Q12. Prove करें कि straight line में intercept \( a \) की normal equation residuals का sum zero बनाती है।

Solution: \( a \) की normal equation: \( \frac{\partial S}{\partial a} = -2 \sum (Y_i - a - bX_i) = 0 \)।
−2 से divide करने पर: \( \sum (Y_i - \hat{Y}_i) = 0 \)।
चूँकि \( (Y_i - \hat{Y}_i) \) exactly residual \( e_i \) है, इसलिए \( \sum e_i = 0 \)।
यानी least-squares straight line के लिए सभी residuals का sum हमेशा zero होता है। ∎

Q13. \( Y = ab^X \) को linear form में transform करें और \( a \) तथा \( b \) recover करने की equations लिखें।

Solution: Base-10 logarithm लें: \( \log_{10} Y = \log_{10} a + X\log_{10} b \)।
मान लें \( V = \log Y \), \( A = \log a \), \( B = \log b \)। Linear form \( V = A + BX \) है।
Normal equations solve करने के बाद:
\( a = 10^A \) (A का antilog)
\( b = 10^B \) (B का antilog)

Q14. 5 data points के लिए: \( \sum X = 15, \sum Y = 204, \sum X^2 = 55, \sum XY = 748 \)। Straight line fit करें।

\( 204 = 5a + 15b \)  (Eq 1)
\( 748 = 15a + 55b \)  (Eq 2)

Q15. \( Y = ab^X \) और \( Y = aX^b \) fit करने में क्या अंतर है?

Solution:
1) \( Y = ab^X \) एक exponential curve है। \( X \) exponent में है। Linearise करने पर: \( \log Y = \log a + X\log b \)। केवल \( Y \) transform होता है, \( X \) वैसे ही रहता है।
2) \( Y = aX^b \) एक power curve है। \( X \) एक constant power पर है। Linearise करने पर: \( \log Y = \log a + b\log X \)। यहाँ \( X \) और \( Y \) दोनों को log variables में transform करना पड़ता है।

Q16. क्या curve fitting के लिए absolute errors का sum \( \sum |y_i - \hat{y}_i| \) use हो सकता है? क्यों या क्यों नहीं?

Solution: Technically हाँ, लेकिन standard curve fitting में नहीं होता। Absolute value function mathematically sharp "V" corners बनाता है। Zero पर इसे standard calculus से differentiate नहीं कर सकते। Errors का square करने से एक smooth parabolic function बनता है जिसे easily differentiate किया जा सकता है — जो सीधे Normal Equations देता है।

Q17. X = 2001, 2002, 2003, 2004, 2005 के लिए change of origin shortcut apply करके U निकालें।

X = 2001 → U = −2;   X = 2002 → U = −1;   X = 2003 → U = 0;   X = 2004 → U = 1;   X = 2005 → U = 2।
Check: \( \sum U = −2−1+0+1+2 = 0 \). ✓

Q18. Line of best fit की equation का determinant form क्या है?

Solution: Line equation \( Y = a + bX \) और दोनों normal equations से constants \( a \) और \( b \) eliminate करने पर, line of best fit को determinant form में लिखा जा सकता है: \[ \begin{vmatrix} Y & X & 1 \\ \sum y_i & \sum x_i & n \\ \sum x_iy_i & \sum x_i^2 & \sum x_i \end{vmatrix} = 0 \]

Solution: Second-order derivatives positive होंगे। Calculus में, critical point पर positive second derivative confirm करता है कि function वहाँ concave up है। इसका मतलब है कि normal equations से मिला solution total error \( S \) के लिए एक सच्चा minimum देता है, maximum नहीं।

Q20. Curves fit करते समय computation table बनाना क्यों ज़रूरी है?

Solution: Computation table उन सभी ज़रूरी sums को organize करती है — जैसे \( \sum X, \sum Y, \sum X^2, \sum XY \) — जो normal equations में जाते हैं। Curve fitting में multiple data points के products और powers के sums होते हैं। इन्हें बिना table के calculate करने में arithmetic errors की संभावना बहुत ज़्यादा होती है। एक proper table हर required term के लिए एक column और नीचे totals row के साथ accuracy ensure करती है और values को directly normal equations में substitute करना easy बनाती है।
