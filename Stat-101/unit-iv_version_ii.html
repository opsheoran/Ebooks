4.1 Skewness: Introduction & Interpretation

4.1 Skewness: परिचय और Interpretation

Measures of central tendency tell us about the concentration of values around a central point, and measures of dispersion give us the spread. However, neither tells us about the symmetry of the distribution. This is where Skewness comes in. Skewness means 'lack of symmetry'. When a frequency distribution is not symmetrical about the mean, it is called a skewed distribution.

Central tendency के measures हमें central point के आस-पास values के concentration के बारे में बताते हैं, और dispersion spread के बारे में बताता है। लेकिन, कोई भी distribution की symmetry के बारे में नहीं बताता। यहीं पर Skewness काम आता है। Skewness का मतलब है 'symmetry की कमी' (lack of symmetry)। जब कोई frequency distribution mean के दोनों ओर symmetrical नहीं होता, तो उसे skewed distribution कहते हैं।

Types of Skewness

Skewness के प्रकार

  • Symmetrical (Zero Skewness): The left half and right half are mirror images. Mean = Median = Mode. The curve is bell-shaped.
  • Positively Skewed: The right tail is longer. The extreme values are on the higher side. Here, Mean > Median > Mode.
  • Negatively Skewed: The left tail is longer. The extreme values are on the lower side. Here, Mean < Median < Mode.
  • Symmetrical (Zero Skewness): Left half और right half एक-दूसरे की mirror images होते हैं। Mean = Median = Mode. Curve bell-shaped होता है।
  • Positively Skewed: Right tail लंबी होती है। Extreme values higher side पर होती हैं। यहाँ, Mean > Median > Mode होता है।
  • Negatively Skewed: Left tail लंबी होती है। Extreme values lower side पर होती हैं। यहाँ, Mean < Median < Mode होता है।

4.2 Coefficients of Skewness

4.2 Skewness के Coefficients

To compare skewness between different distributions, we use relative measures called Coefficients of Skewness. They are pure numbers independent of units.

अलग-अलग distributions के बीच skewness compare करने के लिए, हम relative measures use करते हैं जिन्हें Coefficients of Skewness कहते हैं। ये pure numbers होते हैं जो units से independent होते हैं।

1. Karl Pearson's Coefficient of Skewness (\(S_k\))

1. Karl Pearson's Coefficient of Skewness (\(S_k\))

It is based on the relationship between Mean, Median, and Mode.

यह Mean, Median, और Mode के relationship पर आधारित है।

\[ S_k = \frac{\text{Mean} - \text{Mode}}{\text{Standard Deviation} (\sigma)} \]

If Mode is ill-defined, using the empirical relation (Mode = 3 Median - 2 Mean), the formula becomes:

अगर Mode ill-defined है, तो empirical relation (Mode = 3 Median - 2 Mean) का use करके, formula बन जाता है:

\[ S_k = \frac{3(\text{Mean} - \text{Median})}{\sigma} \]

Interpretation: \(S_k\) usually lies between -3 and +3. \(S_k = 0\) means perfect symmetry.

Interpretation: \(S_k\) आमतौर पर -3 और +3 के बीच होता है। \(S_k = 0\) का मतलब perfect symmetry है।

2. Bowley's Coefficient of Skewness (\(S_Q\))

2. Bowley's Coefficient of Skewness (\(S_Q\))

It is based on Quartiles and is very useful for open-ended distributions.

यह Quartiles पर आधारित है और open-ended distributions के लिए बहुत useful है।

\[ S_Q = \frac{Q_3 + Q_1 - 2\text{Median}}{Q_3 - Q_1} \]

Interpretation: \(S_Q\) always lies between -1 and +1.

Interpretation: \(S_Q\) हमेशा -1 और +1 के बीच होता है।

3. Kelly's Coefficient of Skewness (\(S_P\))

3. Kelly's Coefficient of Skewness (\(S_P\))

It is based on Percentiles or Deciles, ignoring the extreme 10% on both sides.

यह Percentiles या Deciles पर आधारित है, जो दोनों sides के extreme 10% को ignore करता है।

\[ S_P = \frac{P_{90} + P_{10} - 2P_{50}}{P_{90} - P_{10}} \quad \text{or} \quad \frac{D_9 + D_1 - 2D_5}{D_9 - D_1} \]

Example 1: Calculating Pearson's Skewness (Farm Survey)

Example 1: Pearson's Skewness की गणना (Farm Survey)

In a farm survey, the Mean yield is 45 q/ha, the Median is 42 q/ha, and the Standard Deviation is 12 q/ha. Calculate the coefficient of skewness.

एक farm survey में, Mean yield 45 q/ha, Median 42 q/ha और Standard Deviation 12 q/ha है। Coefficient of skewness निकालें।

Solution:

\[ S_k = \frac{3(45 - 42)}{12} = \frac{3(3)}{12} = \frac{9}{12} = +0.75 \]

Since \(S_k = +0.75 > 0\), the distribution of yield is positively skewed. The tail is on the right side.

चूंकि \(S_k = +0.75 > 0\) है, yield का distribution positively skewed है। Tail right side पर है।

Example 2: Grouped Data से Bowley's Coefficient

Since \(S_Q = -0.105\) (slightly negative), the distribution shows slight negative skewness.

चूंकि \(S_Q = -0.105\) (slightly negative) है, distribution में slight negative skewness है।

4.3 Kurtosis: Concept & Coefficient

4.3 Kurtosis: Concept और Coefficient

While skewness measures the lack of symmetry, Kurtosis measures the degree of peakedness or flatness of a distribution curve relative to a normal curve. It describes the shape of the tails and the central peak.

जहाँ skewness symmetry की कमी को मापता है, वहीं Kurtosis एक normal curve के मुक़ाबले distribution curve के peakedness (नुकीलापन) या flatness (चपटापन) को मापता है। यह tails और central peak के shape को describe करता है।

Types of Kurtosis

Kurtosis के प्रकार

  • Mesokurtic: A normal, bell-shaped curve with standard peakedness. Matches normal distribution.
  • Leptokurtic: A curve that is more peaked than the normal curve, with thicker tails. Contains more extreme values.
  • Platykurtic: A curve that is flatter than the normal curve, with thinner tails. More uniform distribution.
  • Mesokurtic: एक normal, bell-shaped curve जिसमें standard peakedness होती है। Normal distribution को match करता है।
  • Leptokurtic: एक curve जो normal curve से ज़्यादा peaked (नुकीला) होता है, और इसकी tails मोटी होती हैं। ज़्यादा extreme values होती हैं।
  • Platykurtic: एक curve जो normal curve से ज़्यादा flat (चपटा) होता है, और इसकी tails पतली होती हैं। ज़्यादा uniform distribution होती है।

Coefficient of Kurtosis (\(\beta_2\))

Coefficient of Kurtosis (\(\beta_2\))

Kurtosis is measured using the second and fourth central moments (\(\mu_2\) and \(\mu_4\)).

Kurtosis को second और fourth central moments (\(\mu_2\) और \(\mu_4\)) का उपयोग करके मापा जाता है।

\[ \beta_2 = \frac{\mu_4}{\mu_2^2} \quad \text{and} \quad \gamma_2 = \beta_2 - 3 \]

Interpretation:

Interpretation:

  • If \(\beta_2 = 3\) (or \(\gamma_2 = 0\)): Mesokurtic (Normal).
  • If \(\beta_2 > 3\) (or \(\gamma_2 > 0\)): Leptokurtic (More peaked).
  • If \(\beta_2 < 3\) (or \(\gamma_2 < 0\)): Platykurtic (Flatter).
  • अगर \(\beta_2 = 3\) (या \(\gamma_2 = 0\)): Mesokurtic (Normal)।
  • अगर \(\beta_2 > 3\) (या \(\gamma_2 > 0\)): Leptokurtic (ज़्यादा peaked)।
  • अगर \(\beta_2 < 3\) (या \(\gamma_2 < 0\)): Platykurtic (ज़्यादा flat)।

Example 1: Computing Kurtosis from Raw Data

Example 1: Raw Data से Kurtosis की गणना

Given: Central moments: \(\mu_1 = 0, \mu_2 = 16, \mu_3 = -64, \mu_4 = 256\)

Given: Central moments: \(\mu_1 = 0, \mu_2 = 16, \mu_3 = -64, \mu_4 = 256\)

Solution:

\[ \beta_2 = \frac{\mu_4}{\mu_2^2} = \frac{256}{(16)^2} = \frac{256}{256} = 1 \] \[ \gamma_2 = \beta_2 - 3 = 1 - 3 = -2 \]

Since \(\beta_2 = 1 < 3\) and \(\gamma_2 = -2 < 0\), the distribution is Platykurtic. It is flatter than the normal distribution.

चूंकि \(\beta_2 = 1 < 3\) और \(\gamma_2 = -2 < 0\) है, distribution Platykurtic है। यह normal distribution से flatter है।

4.4 Theory of Attributes & Dichotomy

4.4 Theory of Attributes और Dichotomy

In statistics, a characteristic that can be measured numerically (like height, weight, yield) is called a Variable. A characteristic that cannot be measured numerically but can only be observed as present or absent (like gender, literacy, employment, soil type) is called an Attribute. The statistics of attributes deals with counting the number of individuals possessing or not possessing certain qualitative traits.

Statistics में, जिस characteristic को numerically मापा जा सकता है (जैसे height, weight, yield), उसे Variable कहते हैं। जिस characteristic को numerically नहीं मापा जा सकता बल्कि केवल present या absent के रूप में observe किया जा सकता है (जैसे gender, literacy, employment, soil type), उसे Attribute कहते हैं। Attributes की statistics उन individuals की गिनती से संबंधित है जिनमें कुछ qualitative traits मौजूद हैं या नहीं।

Dichotomy of Data

Dichotomy of Data (डेटा का द्विभाजन)

Dividing the universe (total population) into two mutually exclusive groups—those possessing a specific attribute and those lacking it—is called Dichotomy.

Universe (total population) को दो mutually exclusive groups में बाँटना—जिनके पास कोई specific attribute है और जिनके पास नहीं है—Dichotomy कहलाता है।

Symbolic Notations

Symbolic Notations

  • Presence: Capital letters A, B, C are used to denote the presence of attributes. E.g., A = Educated, B = Employed.
  • Absence: Corresponding Greek letters \(\alpha, \beta, \gamma\) (or lowercase letters a, b, c) are used to denote the absence of attributes. E.g., \(\alpha\) = Uneducated, \(\beta\) = Unemployed.
  • Combination: Combinations are denoted by combining letters. E.g., AB = Educated and Employed. \(\alpha B\) = Uneducated but Employed.
  • Frequencies: The number of individuals possessing a specific attribute is placed in brackets to denote its class frequency. E.g., (A) = number of educated people, (AB) = number of educated and employed people, \(N\) = Total number of observations.
  • Presence: Attributes की presence दिखाने के लिए Capital letters A, B, C का use किया जाता है। जैसे, A = Educated, B = Employed.
  • Absence: Attributes की absence दिखाने के लिए corresponding Greek letters \(\alpha, \beta, \gamma\) (या lowercase a, b, c) का use किया जाता है। जैसे, \(\alpha\) = Uneducated, \(\beta\) = Unemployed.
  • Combination: Letters को combine करके combinations दिखाए जाते हैं। जैसे, AB = Educated and Employed. \(\alpha B\) = Uneducated but Employed.
  • Frequencies: किसी specific attribute वाले individuals की संख्या को brackets में रखा जाता है ताकि class frequency दिखाई जा सके। जैसे, (A) = educated लोगों की संख्या, (AB) = educated और employed लोगों की संख्या, \(N\) = Total observations की संख्या।

4.5 Class Frequencies & Order

4.5 Class Frequencies और Order

Any group formed by specific attributes is called a Class. The number of observations falling in a class is its Class Frequency. The order of a class depends on the number of attributes defining it.

Specific attributes से बने किसी भी group को Class कहते हैं। किसी class में आने वाले observations की संख्या उसकी Class Frequency होती है। किसी class का order उसे define करने वाले attributes की संख्या पर निर्भर करता है।

  • Order 0: Involves no attribute. It is the total population denoted by \(N\).
  • Order 1: Frequencies involving only one attribute. E.g., (A), (B), (\(\alpha\)), (\(\beta\)). There are \(2 \times n\) such frequencies for \(n\) attributes.
  • Order 2: Frequencies involving two attributes. E.g., (AB), (\(\alpha B\)), (A\(\beta\)), (\(\alpha\beta\)).
  • Order 0: इसमें कोई attribute नहीं होता। यह total population होती है जिसे \(N\) से denote करते हैं।
  • Order 1: केवल एक attribute वाली frequencies। जैसे, (A), (B), (\(\alpha\)), (\(\beta\))। \(n\) attributes के लिए ऐसी \(2 \times n\) frequencies होती हैं।
  • Order 2: दो attributes वाली frequencies। जैसे, (AB), (\(\alpha B\)), (A\(\beta\)), (\(\alpha\beta\))।

Ultimate Class Frequencies

Ultimate Class Frequencies

Frequencies of the highest order in a given problem are called Ultimate Class Frequencies. For \(n\) attributes, there are \(2^n\) ultimate class frequencies. If we know these, we can find any other class frequency.

किसी problem में highest order की frequencies को Ultimate Class Frequencies कहते हैं। \(n\) attributes के लिए, \(2^n\) ultimate class frequencies होती हैं। अगर हमें ये पता हों, तो हम कोई भी दूसरी class frequency निकाल सकते हैं।

4.6 Consistency of Data

4.6 Data की Consistency (संगति)

A set of class frequencies is said to be consistent if they do not conflict with each other. Since frequencies represent counts of items, no class frequency (whether observed or derived) can be negative.

Class frequencies का एक set consistent कहा जाता है अगर वे एक-दूसरे के साथ conflict नहीं करते। चूँकि frequencies items की गिनती को दर्शाती हैं, कोई भी class frequency (चाहे observed हो या derived) negative नहीं हो सकती।

Conditions for Consistency (2 Attributes)

Consistency के लिए Conditions (2 Attributes)

For a set of data with two attributes A and B to be consistent, all ultimate class frequencies must be greater than or equal to zero.

दो attributes A और B वाले data set के consistent होने के लिए, सभी ultimate class frequencies zero या उससे बड़ी होनी चाहिए।

\[ 1. \quad (AB) \ge 0 \] \[ 2. \quad (A\beta) \ge 0 \implies (A) - (AB) \ge 0 \implies (AB) \le (A) \] \[ 3. \quad (\alpha B) \ge 0 \implies (B) - (AB) \ge 0 \implies (AB) \le (B) \] \[ 4. \quad (\alpha\beta) \ge 0 \implies N - (A) - (B) + (AB) \ge 0 \implies (AB) \ge (A) + (B) - N \]

Therefore, the required condition is:

इसलिए, ज़रूरी condition है:

\[ (A) + (B) - N \le (AB) \le \text{minimum of } \{ (A), (B) \} \]

Example 1: Checking Consistency (Consistent Case)

Example 1: Consistency Check (Consistent Case)

Given \(N=500\), \((A)=300\), \((B)=250\), \((AB)=150\). Is this data consistent?

दिया गया है \(N=500\), \((A)=300\), \((B)=250\), \((AB)=150\)। क्या यह data consistent है?

Solution:

Check the conditions:

Conditions को check करें:

\[ (A) + (B) - N = 300 + 250 - 500 = 50 \] \[ \min\{(A), (B)\} = \min\{300, 250\} = 250 \] \[ 50 \le 150 \le 250 \quad \checkmark \]

All ultimate frequencies will be non-negative:

सभी ultimate frequencies non-negative होंगी:

\[ (A\beta) = (A) - (AB) = 300 - 150 = 150 \ge 0 \quad \checkmark \] \[ (\alpha B) = (B) - (AB) = 250 - 150 = 100 \ge 0 \quad \checkmark \] \[ (\alpha\beta) = N - (A) - (B) + (AB) = 500 - 300 - 250 + 150 = 100 \ge 0 \quad \checkmark \]

The data is Consistent ✓

Data Consistent है ✓

Example 2: Checking Consistency (Inconsistent Case)

Example 2: Consistency Check (Inconsistent Case)

Given \(N=1000\), \((A)=600\), \((B)=500\), \((AB)=50\). Is this data consistent?

दिया गया है \(N=1000\), \((A)=600\), \((B)=500\), \((AB)=50\)। क्या यह data consistent है?

Solution:

Check the boundary condition:

Boundary condition को check करें:

\[ (A) + (B) - N = 600 + 500 - 1000 = 100 \] \[ (AB) = 50 < 100 \implies (AB) < (A) + (B) - N \]

This violates the consistency condition. Let's compute (\(α\beta\)):

यह consistency condition को violate करता है। आइए (\(α\beta\)) की गणना करें:

\[ (\alpha\beta) = 1000 - 600 - 500 + 50 = -50 < 0 \]

Since a negative frequency is impossible, the given data is Inconsistent ✗

चूंकि negative frequency असंभव है, दिया गया data Inconsistent है ✗

4.7 Independence and Association of Attributes

4.7 Attributes की Independence और Association

1. Independence

1. Independence (स्वतंत्रता)

Two attributes A and B are said to be independent if the presence or absence of one does not affect the presence or absence of the other. Mathematically, they are independent if the expected frequency equals the observed frequency.

दो attributes A और B को independent कहा जाता है अगर एक की presence या absence दूसरे की presence या absence को प्रभावित नहीं करती। Mathematically, वे independent हैं यदि expected frequency observed frequency के बराबर हो।

\[ \text{Condition for Independence:} \quad (AB) = \frac{(A) \times (B)}{N} \]

2. Association

2. Association (साहचर्य)

If attributes A and B are not independent, they are said to be associated. Association can be of two types:

अगर attributes A और B independent नहीं हैं, तो उन्हें associated कहा जाता है। Association दो प्रकार का हो सकता है:

  • Positive Association: The presence of A promotes the presence of B. (e.g., Education and Employment).
    Condition: \( (AB) > \frac{(A)(B)}{N} \)
  • Negative Association (Dissociation): The presence of A suppresses the presence of B. (e.g., Vaccination and Disease).
    Condition: \( (AB) < \frac{(A)(B)}{N} \)
  • Positive Association: A की presence B की presence को बढ़ावा देती है। (जैसे, Education और Employment)।
    Condition: \( (AB) > \frac{(A)(B)}{N} \)
  • Negative Association (Dissociation): A की presence B की presence को दबाती है। (जैसे, Vaccination और Disease)।
    Condition: \( (AB) < \frac{(A)(B)}{N} \)

Example 1: Independence Test (Education & Employment)

4.8 Yule's Coefficient of Association & Colligation

4.8 Yule's Coefficient of Association और Colligation

To measure the degree and direction of association between two attributes, G.U. Yule proposed two coefficients.

दो attributes के बीच association की degree और direction मापने के लिए, G.U. Yule ने दो coefficients दिए।

1. Yule's Coefficient of Association (\(Q\))

1. Yule's Coefficient of Association (\(Q\))

\[ Q = \frac{(AB)(\alpha\beta) - (A\beta)(\alpha B)}{(AB)(\alpha\beta) + (A\beta)(\alpha B)} \]

Properties:

  • \(Q\) lies between -1 and +1.
  • If \(Q = +1\), complete positive association.
  • If \(Q = -1\), complete negative association.
  • If \(Q = 0\), attributes are independent.

Properties:

  • \(Q\) -1 और +1 के बीच होता है।
  • अगर \(Q = +1\), तो complete positive association.
  • अगर \(Q = -1\), तो complete negative association.
  • अगर \(Q = 0\), तो attributes independent हैं।

2. Yule's Coefficient of Colligation (\(Y\))

2. Yule's Coefficient of Colligation (\(Y\))

Another measure which is numerically smaller than \(Q\) but serves a similar purpose.

एक और measure जो numerically \(Q\) से छोटा होता है लेकिन समान उद्देश्य पूरा करता है।

\[ Y = \frac{1 - \sqrt{\frac{(A\beta)(\alpha B)}{(AB)(\alpha\beta)}}}{1 + \sqrt{\frac{(A\beta)(\alpha B)}{(AB)(\alpha\beta)}}} = \frac{\sqrt{(AB)(\alpha\beta)} - \sqrt{(A\beta)(\alpha B)}}{\sqrt{(AB)(\alpha\beta)} + \sqrt{(A\beta)(\alpha B)}} \]

Relationship between Q and Y

Q और Y के बीच Relationship

\[ Q = \frac{2Y}{1 + Y^2} \]

Example 1: Computing Yule's Q from Contingency Table

Example 1: Contingency Table से Yule's Q की गणना

Using the Education & Employment data: (AB)=240, (Aβ)=80, (αB)=40, (αβ)=140

Education & Employment data का उपयोग करते हुए: (AB)=240, (Aβ)=80, (αB)=40, (αβ)=140

Solution:

\[ Q = \frac{(AB)(\alpha\beta) - (A\beta)(\alpha B)}{(AB)(\alpha\beta) + (A\beta)(\alpha B)} \] \[ Q = \frac{(240)(140) - (80)(40)}{(240)(140) + (80)(40)} \] \[ Q = \frac{33600 - 3200}{33600 + 3200} = \frac{30400}{36800} = \frac{304}{368} = 0.826 \]

Q = 0.826 indicates strong positive association between education and employment.

Q = 0.826 education और employment के बीच strong positive association को indicate करता है।

Example 2: Computing Yule's Y and Verifying the Relationship

Example 2: Yule's Y की गणना और Relationship का Verification

From Example 1, compute Y and verify Q = 2Y/(1+Y²)

Example 1 से, Y की गणना करें और Q = 2Y/(1+Y²) को verify करें

Solution:

\[ Y = \frac{\sqrt{(AB)(\alpha\beta)} - \sqrt{(A\beta)(\alpha B)}}{\sqrt{(AB)(\alpha\beta)} + \sqrt{(A\beta)(\alpha B)}} \] \[ Y = \frac{\sqrt{(240)(140)} - \sqrt{(80)(40)}}{\sqrt{(240)(140)} + \sqrt{(80)(40)}} \] \[ Y = \frac{\sqrt{33600} - \sqrt{3200}}{\sqrt{33600} + \sqrt{3200}} \] \[ Y = \frac{183.3 - 56.6}{183.3 + 56.6} = \frac{126.7}{239.9} = 0.528 \]

Verification:

Verification:

\[ Q = \frac{2Y}{1+Y^2} = \frac{2(0.528)}{1 + (0.528)^2} = \frac{1.056}{1 + 0.279} = \frac{1.056}{1.279} = 0.826 \quad \checkmark \]

The relationship is confirmed! Q = 0.826 matches our earlier calculation.

Relationship confirm हुई! Q = 0.826 हमारी पहली गणना को match करता है।

📝 Subjective Questions (Detailed Solutions)

Q1. Define Skewness and list the three types of distributions based on skewness.

Solution: Skewness refers to the lack of symmetry in a statistical distribution. It indicates whether the data is concentrated on one side.
1) Symmetrical: Left and right tails are equal (Mean = Median = Mode).
2) Positively Skewed: Right tail is longer (Mean > Median > Mode).
3) Negatively Skewed: Left tail is longer (Mean < Median < Mode).

Q2. What is Kurtosis? Mention the three types of kurtotic curves.

Solution: Kurtosis measures the degree of peakedness or flatness of a distribution curve relative to a normal curve.
1) Leptokurtic: Highly peaked with thick tails (\(\beta_2 > 3\)).
2) Mesokurtic: Normal peakedness (\(\beta_2 = 3\)).
3) Platykurtic: Flat-topped with thin tails (\(\beta_2 < 3\)).

Q3. A distribution has Mean = 50, Median = 48, and Standard Deviation = 10. Calculate Karl Pearson’s coefficient of skewness.

Solution:
Karl Pearson's coefficient (using Median):
\(S_k = \frac{3(\text{Mean} - \text{Median})}{\sigma}\)
\(S_k = \frac{3(50 - 48)}{10}\)
\(S_k = \frac{3(2)}{10} = \frac{6}{10} = 0.6\)
The distribution is positively skewed.

Q4. For a distribution, \(Q_1 = 30\), \(Q_2 = 45\), and \(Q_3 = 70\). Calculate Bowley’s coefficient of skewness.

Solution:
Bowley's coefficient: \(S_Q = \frac{Q_3 + Q_1 - 2Q_2}{Q_3 - Q_1}\)
\(S_Q = \frac{70 + 30 - 2(45)}{70 - 30}\)
\(S_Q = \frac{100 - 90}{40}\)
\(S_Q = \frac{10}{40} = 0.25\).

Q5. Differentiate between Variables and Attributes with examples.

Solution:
Variable: A characteristic that can be measured numerically. E.g., Height (cm), Weight (kg), Yield (quintals).
Attribute: A qualitative characteristic that cannot be measured numerically but only observed as present or absent. E.g., Gender (Male/Female), Literacy (Literate/Illiterate), Blindness.

Q6. What do you mean by Dichotomy of data?

Solution: Dichotomy means dividing the entire universe (total population) into exactly two mutually exclusive classes based on a single attribute. One class possesses the attribute (denoted by A), and the other lacks it (denoted by \(\alpha\)). Therefore, \(N = (A) + (\alpha)\).

Q7. Explain the order of class frequencies in theory of attributes.

Solution: The order of a class frequency is determined by the number of attributes used to specify it.
- Order 0: \(N\) (Total population, no attributes).
- Order 1: (A), (B), (\(\alpha\)), (\(\beta\)) (One attribute).
- Order 2: (AB), (A\(\beta\)), (\(\alpha\)B), (\(\alpha\beta\)) (Two attributes).

Q8. Write down the relationship between frequencies of various orders for two attributes A and B.

Solution:
\(N = (A) + (\alpha) = (B) + (\beta)\)
\((A) = (AB) + (A\beta)\)
\((B) = (AB) + (\alpha B)\)
\((\alpha) = (\alpha B) + (\alpha\beta)\)
\((\beta) = (A\beta) + (\alpha\beta)\)
\(N = (AB) + (A\beta) + (\alpha B) + (\alpha\beta)\).

Q9. What is the condition for two attributes A and B to be completely independent?

Solution: Two attributes A and B are independent if the presence of one does not influence the presence of the other. The mathematical condition is that the observed frequency (AB) is equal to its expected frequency.
Condition: \((AB) = \frac{(A) \times (B)}{N}\).

Q10. Define Consistency of data in theory of attributes.

Solution: A given set of class frequencies is said to be consistent if they do not conflict with each other. Since frequencies represent counts, no class frequency (especially the ultimate class frequencies) can be negative. So, if any calculated ultimate class frequency is < 0, the data is inconsistent.

Q11. State the necessary conditions for consistency for two attributes A and B.

Solution: For consistency, all ultimate class frequencies must be non-negative:
1) \((AB) \ge 0\)
2) \((A\beta) \ge 0 \implies (AB) \le (A)\)
3) \((\alpha B) \ge 0 \implies (AB) \le (B)\)
4) \((\alpha\beta) \ge 0 \implies (AB) \ge (A) + (B) - N\).
Combined: \((A) + (B) - N \le (AB) \le \min((A), (B))\).

Q12. Write the formula for Yule's Coefficient of Association (\(Q\)).

Solution: Yule's Coefficient of Association is given by:

\[ Q = \frac{(AB)(\alpha\beta) - (A\beta)(\alpha B)}{(AB)(\alpha\beta) + (A\beta)(\alpha B)} \]
It ranges from -1 to +1.

Q13. Define Yule's Coefficient of Colligation (\(Y\)).

Solution: Yule's Coefficient of Colligation is another measure of association, which is numerically smaller than \(Q\).

\[ Y = \frac{1 - \sqrt{\frac{(A\beta)(\alpha B)}{(AB)(\alpha\beta)}}}{1 + \sqrt{\frac{(A\beta)(\alpha B)}{(AB)(\alpha\beta)}}} = \frac{\sqrt{(AB)(\alpha\beta)} - \sqrt{(A\beta)(\alpha B)}}{\sqrt{(AB)(\alpha\beta)} + \sqrt{(A\beta)(\alpha B)}} \]

Q14. Prove the relationship between Yule's Coefficient of Association (\(Q\)) and Colligation (\(Y\)).

Solution: Let \( \sqrt{(AB)(\alpha\beta)} = x \) and \( \sqrt{(A\beta)(\alpha B)} = y \).
Then \( Y = \frac{x-y}{x+y} \) and \( Q = \frac{x^2-y^2}{x^2+y^2} \).
Consider the expression: \(\frac{2Y}{1+Y^2}\)
\(= \frac{2(\frac{x-y}{x+y})}{1 + (\frac{x-y}{x+y})^2} = \frac{\frac{2(x-y)}{x+y}}{\frac{(x+y)^2 + (x-y)^2}{(x+y)^2}}\)
\(= \frac{2(x-y)(x+y)}{(x+y)^2 + (x-y)^2} = \frac{2(x^2-y^2)}{x^2 + y^2 + 2xy + x^2 + y^2 - 2xy}\)
\(= \frac{2(x^2-y^2)}{2(x^2+y^2)} = \frac{x^2-y^2}{x^2+y^2} = Q\).
Hence proved, \(Q = \frac{2Y}{1+Y^2}\).

Q15. Given the first four central moments: \(\mu_1=0, \mu_2=16, \mu_3=-64, \mu_4=162\). Calculate the coefficient of skewness (\(\beta_1\)).

Solution:
The moment measure of skewness is \(\beta_1 = \frac{\mu_3^2}{\mu_2^3}\).
\(\beta_1 = \frac{(-64)^2}{(16)^3}\)
\(\beta_1 = \frac{4096}{4096} = 1\).
Since \(\mu_3\) is negative, the distribution is negatively skewed.

Q16. Using the data from Q15, calculate the coefficient of kurtosis (\(\beta_2\)) and comment on the shape.

Solution:
The moment measure of kurtosis is \(\beta_2 = \frac{\mu_4}{\mu_2^2}\).
\(\beta_2 = \frac{162}{(16)^2} = \frac{162}{256} = 0.6328\).
Since \(\beta_2 < 3\), the distribution curve is Platykurtic.

Q17. Given \(N=500\), \((A)=300\), \((B)=250\), \((AB)=150\). Find the remaining ultimate class frequencies: \((A\beta), (\alpha B),\) and \((\alpha\beta)\).

Solution:
1) \((A\beta) = (A) - (AB) = 300 - 150 = 150\).
2) \((\alpha B) = (B) - (AB) = 250 - 150 = 100\).
3) \((\alpha\beta) = N - (A) - (B) + (AB) = 500 - 300 - 250 + 150 = 100\).
The frequencies are 150, 100, and 100 respectively.

Q18. Check if the following data is consistent: \(N=1000\), \((A)=600\), \((B)=500\), \((AB)=50\).

Solution: For consistency, \((\alpha\beta) \ge 0\).
\((\alpha\beta) = N - (A) - (B) + (AB)\)
\((\alpha\beta) = 1000 - 600 - 500 + 50 = -50\).
Since the frequency of a class cannot be negative (\(-50 < 0\)), the given data is inconsistent.

Q19. In a group of 400 people, 250 are educated (A) and 150 are employed (B). 100 people are both educated and employed (AB). Find Yule’s Coefficient of Association (\(Q\)).

Solution:
\(N=400, (A)=250, (B)=150, (AB)=100\).
\((A\beta) = (A) - (AB) = 250 - 100 = 150\).
\((\alpha B) = (B) - (AB) = 150 - 100 = 50\).
\((\alpha\beta) = N - (A) - (B) + (AB) = 400 - 250 - 150 + 100 = 100\).
\(Q = \frac{(AB)(\alpha\beta) - (A\beta)(\alpha B)}{(AB)(\alpha\beta) + (A\beta)(\alpha B)}\)
\(Q = \frac{(100)(100) - (150)(50)}{(100)(100) + (150)(50)} = \frac{10000 - 7500}{10000 + 7500}\)
\(Q = \frac{2500}{17500} = \frac{1}{7} \approx 0.143\).
There is a low positive association between education and employment.

Q20. Find Yule's Coefficient of Colligation (\(Y\)) for the data in Q19 and verify \(Q = \frac{2Y}{1+Y^2}\).

Solution:
\((AB)=100, (\alpha\beta)=100, (A\beta)=150, (\alpha B)=50\).
\(Y = \frac{\sqrt{(100)(100)} - \sqrt{(150)(50)}}{\sqrt{(100)(100)} + \sqrt{(150)(50)}} = \frac{100 - \sqrt{7500}}{100 + \sqrt{7500}}\)
\(\sqrt{7500} \approx 86.60\)
\(Y = \frac{100 - 86.60}{100 + 86.60} = \frac{13.40}{186.60} \approx 0.0718\).
Now verify: \(\frac{2Y}{1+Y^2} = \frac{2(0.0718)}{1 + (0.0718)^2} = \frac{0.1436}{1 + 0.00515} = \frac{0.1436}{1.00515} \approx 0.143\).
This matches the \(Q\) calculated in Q19 (\(0.143\)). Hence verified.
